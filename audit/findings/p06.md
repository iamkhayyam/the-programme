## CRITICAL

### F1. The third instance fails the paper's own definition: primes do not "concentrate" on the inert classes
- **Severity:** CRITICAL. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:355-359. The same claim appears at :83-88 (abstract), :125-127, :393 (table) and :438-439 (§6.3).
- **Quote:** "primes concentrate onto the inert sublattice $\mathcal{E}_K = \{a \in (\Z/3^K\Z)^* : a \equiv 2 \pmod{3}\}$, with $|\mathcal{E}_K|/|(\Z/3^K\Z)^*| = 1/2$; the level of distribution $5/8$ measures the depth of this concentration."
- **What is wrong:**
  - (SW3) needs two things. The complement $\mathcal C_n\setminus\mathcal E_n$ must carry vanishing measure, and $|\mathcal E_n| = e^{n(\theta^* r+o(1))}$ must hold with $\theta^*\in(0,1)$.
  - Measure: by Dirichlet / PNT in APs, primes are equidistributed between the classes 1 and 2 mod 3. The complement therefore carries half of all primes, not a vanishing share.
  - Size: $|\mathcal E_K| = 3^{K-1} = \tfrac12|\mathcal C_K|$. The exponent ratio is $\log|\mathcal E_K|/\log|\mathcal C_K| \to 1$, not 5/8 and not inside (0,1).
  - The constraint $p\equiv 2 \pmod 3$ applies only to the smaller member of a twin-prime pair (p04 Cor. "twin primes as inert-split pairs"). Theorem 5.1, however, is about $\Lambda(n)$, i.e. all primes.
  - "Measures the depth of this concentration" is never defined.
- **Evidence:** among primes 3 < p ≤ 10^8, 0.500036 are ≡ 2 (mod 3) and 0.499964 are ≡ 1 (mod 3) (`nt.py`).
- **Correction:** withdraw Proposition 5.2. Under Definition 2.1, the system $(\Z/3^K)^*$ with primes as the measure is not an SW system. The value 5/8 is not an exponent ratio of any set defined in the paper.

### F2. Theorem 5.1 as stated is trivial for every θ < 1, so "5/8 uniquely forced" and "no θ > 5/8 achievable" are false
- **Severity:** CRITICAL. **Status:** VERIFIED (proof below, plus numerics)
- **Location:** p06.tex:322-336. Related claims at :146-150, :597-605, :68-69 ("three proven instances") and :164-165.
- **Quote:** "$\sum_{K\ge1,\,3^K\le x^{5/8-\varepsilon}} \max_{a}|\sum_{n\le x,\,n\equiv a(3^K)}\Lambda(n)-x/\varphi(3^K)| \ll x/(\log x)^A$ … The constant $\thetaW = 5/8$ is uniquely forced … No value $\theta > 5/8$ is achievable for cascade moduli without additional hypotheses." Also: "no unconditional result achieves $\theta > \thetaW$ for this family."
- **What is wrong:** the sum runs over only $O(\log x)$ moduli, and the error is not normalised by $\varphi(q)$. That makes the statement provable up to $3^K \le x$ (θ = 1) with no input beyond Siegel–Walfisz:
  - For $3^K \le (\log x)^{A+2}$: Siegel–Walfisz gives each term $\ll x e^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}$, and there are $O(\log\log x)$ such K.
  - For $3^K > (\log x)^{A+2}$: the trivial bound gives $|\psi(x;3^K,a)-x/\varphi(3^K)| \le (x/3^K+1)\log x + 1.5x/3^K$. Summed over K, this geometric series is $\ll x/(\log x)^{A+1}$.
  - So the stated inequality holds for all $3^K\le x$. The exponent 5/8 plays no role, and "no θ > 5/8 achievable" is refuted by this one-line argument.
- **The meaningful version is a different claim.** Normalised per modulus, the statement would be $\max_a|\psi(x;3^K,a)-x/\varphi(3^K)| \ll x/(\varphi(3^K)(\log x)^A)$. That would be equidistribution for a *single* modulus up to $x^{5/8}$, which is beyond what GRH gives ($x^{1/2-\varepsilon}$). The best known results for prime-power moduli are BLT 3/8, Gallagher 2/5, Huxley 5/12 and Banks–Shparlinski ≈ 0.47 (lead facts 5 and 7).
- **Additional problem:** "no value θ > 5/8 is achievable" is an impossibility claim. Even in the normalised form it contradicts the standard conjectures (Montgomery/EH-type), which predict level 1−ε. No such upper bound is known for any modulus family.
- **Evidence:** `thm51.py`, x = 10^7, all 3^K ≤ x (θ up to 0.954). The whole sum is 9.6·10^-4·x, and the terms with 3^K > x^{5/8} together contribute < 5·10^-5·x. With p06's normalisation, θ = 1 is already "achieved".
- **Correction:** either state the normalised single-modulus form, in which case 5/8 would beat GRH and needs an extraordinary proof, or concede that the statement is trivial. Remove "uniquely forced", "no θ > 5/8 achievable", "no unconditional result achieves θ > θ_W", and the "[Proven]" status of the third instance.

### F3. The paper's own information-theory instances contradict its k = 1 assignment and the conjecture's formula
- **Severity:** CRITICAL. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:526-531 (Remark 9.2), :512-524 (Conjecture), :252-266 (Prop 3.2), :293-307 (Prop 4.2). Also p06.html:197 (table row 5).
- **Quote (tex):** "It encompasses information theory ($k=1$: entropy/capacity)".
- **Quote (HTML row 5):** "θ₁ = (2−1)/2 = 1/2 | θ₁ = 1/2 (binary channel) | θ₃ = (8−3)/8 = 5/8".
- **What is wrong:**
  - Under the conjecture, a k = 1 system must have θ* = θ_1 = 1/2.
  - The paper's own governing constants are Φ = H/log2|A| (Prop 3.2) and Φ = C/log2|X| (Prop 4.2). These take every value in (0,1); they are not 1/2 in general and need not be rational.
  - For example, a binary source with p = (1/3, 2/3) has H/log2|A| = 0.918296.
  - BSC capacity is 1 − H(ε), which equals 1/2 only at ε = 0.1100279; the binary erasure channel gives 1/2 only at erasure probability 1/2.
  - So the AEP and channel-coding "instances" are counterexamples to the Constitutional Forcing Conjecture as Remark 9.2 applies it. This is the same flaw as p01:382-398 (lead's AEP note).
  - p06 correctly treats H/log|A| as an exponent ratio, not as a fraction of the space; the fraction is 2^{n(H−log|A|)} → 0, e.g. 2.5·10^-25 at n = 1000 for p = (1/3, 2/3).
- **Evidence:** `info.py`.
- **Correction:** drop "k = 1: entropy/capacity" and the HTML row 5. State that Shannon constants are continuous functions of p and of p(y|x), not values of θ_k.

### F4. The "Sophie Germain non-instance" is labelled [Proven] but nothing is proven, and the notion is undefined
- **Severity:** CRITICAL. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:90-92, :153-155, :167-168, :447-463, :491, :550-552.
- **Quote:** "Sophie Germain prime pairs, whose coupled modular constraints confine the level of distribution to $\thetaBV = 1/2$"; "[Proven] Sophie Germain primes are a non-instance".
- **What is wrong:**
  - "Level of distribution of Sophie Germain pairs" is not a standard notion, and the paper never defines it.
  - No theorem in the literature gives any level of distribution for the sequence of SG pairs. Even the infinitude of SG primes is open.
  - The 1/2 the paper quotes is θ_BV, the Bombieri–Vinogradov level *for primes*. That is the same sieve input used for twin primes, so it is not specific to SG.
  - "Confine … to 1/2" is an upper-bound claim. Proving it would refute the (generalised) Elliott–Halberstam-type conjectures, which predict level 1−ε.
  - The "proof" is the formula θ_2 = (4−2)/4 plus an unreproducible computation (F7), with a circular citation (F8).
- **Correction:** relabel as a heuristic. Define the statistic, and remove "[Proven]", "confirmed" and "confine".

### F5. The channel-capacity identity in §6.2 is false
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:421.
- **Quote:** "$C = \log|\mathcal{X}| - \min_{p(x)} H(X|Y)$, total uncertainty minus what the channel obscures."
- **What is wrong:** take p(x) to be a point mass. Then H(X|Y) ≤ H(X) = 0, so $\min_{p(x)}H(X|Y)=0$ for every channel. The formula would then give C = log|X| for every channel, noisy or not.
- **Evidence:** for BSC(0.11) the formula gives 1.0, but the true C = 1 − H(0.11) = 0.500084 (`info.py`). The correct statement is $C=\max_{p}[H(X)-H(X|Y)]$. For symmetric channels, $C=\log|\mathcal Y| - H(\text{row of }W)$, which for the BSC is 1 − H(ε); equivalently, $\log|\mathcal X|-H(X|Y)$ evaluated at the uniform input.
- **Correction:** replace it with one of the correct forms above. Then note that the "(total − constraint)/total" reading does not survive, because the right form is a max of a difference.

### F6. The "coupling" argument that gives SG depth 2 applies verbatim to twin primes, and the data confirm it
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:454-456, :469-470, :528-529. This propagates lead fact 3.
- **Quote:** "the mod-$3$ residue of $2p+1$ is determined by that of $p$ (both satisfy $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$), giving only two independent constraint levels"; "the coupling between $p$ and $2p+1$ modulo $3$ reduces the effective $k$ from $3$ to $2$"; "prime constellations ($k=3$: twin primes via Eisenstein structure)".
- **What is wrong:**
  - The residue of p+2 mod 3 is equally "determined by" p mod 3. Any linear form has this property.
  - For p > 3, twin primes and SG primes each satisfy exactly one condition, p ≡ 2 (mod 3), and nothing further mod 9, 27 or 81.
  - By the programme's own independence criterion, both have depth 1. Nothing in the paper explains why twins start at 3 and SG at 3 − 1 = 2.
- **Evidence (`nt.py`, p ≤ 10^8):**
  - SG mod 9: {2: 141154, 5: 140997, 8: 140987}. Twin mod 9: {2: 146705, 5: 147163, 8: 146443}.
  - Mod 27 and mod 81: both are uniform over all classes ≡ 2 (mod 3).
  - `sgproxy.py`: the max z-scores of SG and twin counts mod 3^K (K = 2..15) agree with each other and with multinomial noise at every K. For example, at K = 10 (θ = 0.596) they are 3.77 vs 3.52, with an expected multinomial maximum of 4.57.
- **Correction:** either both constellations have the same depth or the criterion is not well defined. Drop "twin primes k = 3" and "SG k = 2".

### F7. The computation "δ·log x = 2.38 ± 0.18 … inconsistent with θ > 1/2 at p < 0.001" is unreproducible and, as reconstructed, an artefact of the main-term approximation
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED that δ is undefined and that a finite computation cannot test an asymptotic exponent. The reconstruction is SUSPECT (best-fitting interpretation, not confirmed by the author).
- **Location:** p06.tex:461-462.
- **Quote:** "Computational analysis confirms $\delta \cdot \log x = 2.38 \pm 0.18$ (stable), consistent with $\theta = 1/2$ and inconsistent with $\theta > 1/2$ at $p < 0.001$."
- **What is wrong:**
  - δ, the range of x, the test statistic and the null model appear nowhere in the paper or the repo; grep finds "2.38" only on this line.
  - A level of distribution is an asymptotic exponent, so no finite dataset can reject θ > 1/2.
- **Re-run (`delta.py`):** take δ = π_SG(x)/(2C₂·x/(log x·log 2x)) − 1, the relative error of the crude Hardy–Littlewood main term. Then δ·log x = 2.43, 2.39, 2.42, 2.43 and 2.37 at x = 10^6, 10^6.5, 10^7, 10^7.5 and 10^8, which matches "2.38, stable".
  - The pure integral $\int dt/(\log t\log(2t+1))$ against the crude term alone gives (I/crude − 1)·log x = 2.373 at x = 10^8.
  - That is just the second-order term of the HL main term, tending to 2. It involves no primes and says nothing about any level of distribution.
  - Against the HL integral, δ·log x ≈ 0 (−0.11 to +0.05).
  - Twin primes give the same phenomenon (δ·log x ≈ 2.42–2.63 against 2C₂x/log²x).
- **Correction:** delete the sentence, or give a full definition with code. Even then it cannot confirm a level of distribution.

### F9. The "non-instance" has no discriminating power, and three incompatible derivations of its depth exist in the programme
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED

### F10. Channel coding (Prop 4.2) violates (SW1)/(SW3) by the paper's own statement
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:289-291 vs :295-304.
- **Quote:** "there are $|\mathcal{X}|^{n \cdot 2^{nR}}$ possible codebooks … doubly exponential in $n$", then "(SW1): The codebook space is exponential in $n$."
- **What is wrong:**
  - (SW1) requires $|\mathcal C_n| = e^{n(r+o(1))}$, but the paper itself says the codebook space is doubly exponential.

  - Φ = C/log2|X| is not the exponent ratio of any set named in the proof.

### F11. The degraded-channel analogy in Remark 7.2 is backwards
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:467-469.
- **Quote:** "a degraded channel has lower capacity because extra dependence between input and output is introduced".
- **What is wrong:** capacity is the maximal mutual information, i.e. the maximal input–output dependence. Degrading a channel (X→Y→Z) *reduces* dependence: I(X;Z) ≤ I(X;Y) by the data-processing inequality. More dependence means higher capacity, not lower.
- **Correction:** "lower capacity because the degradation destroys input–output dependence (data-processing inequality)". With that correction the analogy to "coupling reduces k" no longer holds.

### F12. Prop 3.2 (AEP is an SW instance) is false as stated
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:252-266 and Definition :211-214.
- **What is wrong:**
  - For a uniform source, every $x^n$ has $-\tfrac1n\log p(x^n)=H$, so $T_\varepsilon^{(n)}=\mathcal A^n$. Then $\mathcal E_n\subsetneq\mathcal C_n$ fails and θ* = 1 ∉ (0,1).

### F16. Definition 2.1's (SW2)/(SW4) are not mathematical predicates, and "uniqueness" is meaningless for a level of distribution
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:208-219, :121, :224-229, :359-362, :597-607.
- **Quote:** "uniquely determined by the algebraic structure … No other value of $\theta^*$ is consistent"; "$\thetaW = 5/8$ is the unique value consistent with both the Eisenstein filtration … and the cyclic group cancellation"; "$\thetaW = \sup\{\theta : \text{equidistribution holds for } 3^K \leq x^\theta\}$".
- **What is wrong:**
  - "Intrinsic" and "algebraic structure" are undefined. H and C are continuous functions of real parameters, not algebraic invariants. Any function of a system is "uniquely determined" by it, so (SW4) is a tautology.
  - Levels of distribution are downward closed: if 5/8 holds, every θ < 5/8 holds. So "the unique value consistent" is false.
  - The sup in §11.4 is the true sup. That is unknown, conjecturally 1, and equal to 1 in the paper's own normalisation (F2). It is not "what our method proves".
- **Correction:** distinguish "proved exponent" from "true sup", and delete the uniqueness claims.

### F17. "5/8 forced by Eisenstein structure / by the prime 3" is contradicted by known results

- **Correction:** remove the "forced by 3 / Z[ω]" claims, and acknowledge that Pascadi's 5/8 has an unrelated origin.

### F20. Lead fact 2 (Counting Theorem miscount) propagates, which makes Conjecture 9.1's hypothesis unsatisfiable
- **Severity:** MAJOR. **Status:** VERIFIED
- **Location:** p06.tex:338-342, :424-426, :518-520.
- **Quote:** "of the $2^3 = 8$ binary configurations in a three-level cascade sieve, exactly $k = 3$ are invalid … leaving $5$ valid"; Conjecture (iii) "with $2^k$ total binary configurations of which $k$ are invalidated by the constraints".
- **What is wrong:**

- **Correction:** remove "derived" language. State θ_k as an ansatz, and restate (iii) coherently or drop it.
