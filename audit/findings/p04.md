# Audit of P04: "Ternary Forcing on the Hexagonal Lattice: Eisenstein Integers, Cascade Moduli, and Level of Distribution 5/8"

Files: `papers/p04-that-eisenstein-guy/p04.tex` (the PDF text matches the .tex, with compiled numbering Prop 2.4 = filtration, Cor 2.5 = weights, Prop 2.6 = Cauchy–Schwarz, Cor 2.7 = 2/3 ceiling, Prop 3.3 = Kloosterman, Thm 5.1 = uniqueness), `p04.html` (the web version, which differs in places), `P04_EisensteinIntegers.lean` (identical to `lean/TheProgramme/P04_EisensteinIntegers.lean`).

Scratch scripts are in `audit/scripts/p04/`:
- `kloosterman.py`: exact Kloosterman sums mod 3^K computed by FFT.
- `thm11_trivial.py`: the Theorem 1.1 sum at x = 2·10^7.
- `exponents.py`: exponent arithmetic.
- `conductor.py`: conductor of χ∘N.
- `lchi3.py`: L(s,χ_{-3}) on (0,1).

## What the headline theorem actually says (tex l.115–128)

The theorem is a Bombieri–Vinogradov-type sum over the **lacunary** family {3^K : 3^K ≤ x^{5/8−ε}}. That family has about (5/8)·log x / log 3 members. The sum has absolute values, a max over a, and no weights. Its target is x/(log x)^A. It is **not** a per-modulus statement, and it is **not** a weighted (well-factorable) statement. The corollary then says "level of distribution θ_W = 5/8" (l.126).

## Findings

### P04-01 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — Theorem 1.1 is trivially true for every exponent, so "θ_W = 5/8" is meaningless and the sharpness claim is false
- **Where:** p04.tex:115–128. Also l.103–104 (definition of level), l.126–127 (Corollary), l.398–401 (Thm 5.1(3)), l.60–61 (abstract).
- **Quote:** "`\sum_{K\ge1,\ 3^K\le x^{5/8-\varepsilon}} \max_a |\psi(x;3^K,a)-x/\varphi(3^K)| \ll x/(\log x)^A`" and "θ = 5/8 is achieved and is sharp; no larger value is achievable for cascade moduli without additional hypotheses".
- **What is wrong:** Because the family is lacunary, the trivial bound already sums to less than x/(log x)^A. The inequality therefore holds with 5/8 replaced by **any** fixed θ, including θ ≥ 1. So:
  - It carries no information about 5/8.
  - Under the paper's own definition (l.103–104: level = supremum of admissible θ), the "level" of this family is +∞, not 5/8.
  - Thm 5.1(3) ("sharp; no larger value achievable") is false.
- **Evidence (proof):** Take q = 3^K and gcd(a,3) = 1.
  - Trivially |ψ(x;q,a) − x/φ(q)| ≤ (x/q + 1)·log x + (3/2)·x/q.
  - Moduli with (log x)^{A+1} < 3^K ≤ x^θ: summing the geometric series gives ≪ x·log x·(log x)^{−A−1} + O(θ·(log x)^2) ≪ x/(log x)^A.
  - Moduli with 3^K ≤ (log x)^{A+1}: there are O(log log x) of them. Siegel–Walfisz gives each one ≪ x·exp(−c√log x). This step is even effective here (see P04-13).
  - Numerical illustration (`thm11_trivial.py`, x = 2·10^7): the sum over **all** 3^K ≤ x of max_a|E| is 10,410. Compare x/(log x)^2 = 70,767. Yet the relative error max|E|·φ(q)/x is 0.28 at q ≈ x^{0.59} and exceeds 1 for q ≥ x^{0.78}. The summed statement simply does not see the level.
- **Fix:** A meaningful statement for a sparse family must be per modulus: max_a |ψ(x;q,a) − x/φ(q)| ≪ x/(φ(q)(log x)^A) uniformly for q = 3^K ≤ x^θ. See P04-02.

### P04-02 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED (literature) — The meaningful (per-modulus) version of the claim would go beyond GRH, and nothing in the paper proves it (fact 5)
- **Where:** l.60–61, l.126–127, l.398–401.
- **Quote:** "We establish a level of distribution θ = 5/8 for primes in arithmetic progressions modulo powers of 3." Also "no larger value is achievable … without additional hypotheses (e.g., GRH)".
- **What is wrong:**
  - For a single modulus q, GRH gives ψ(x;q,a) = x/φ(q) + O(x^{1/2} log²x). That only reaches q ≤ x^{1/2−ε}.
  - Known unconditional ranges for prime-power moduli p^N:
    - Barban–Linnik–Tshudakov (1964): x^{3/8−ε}.
    - Gallagher: 2/5.
    - Huxley: 5/12.
    - Banks–Shparlinski: lower bounds of the correct order only, up to x^{0.4736}.
  - Averaging over the ~log Q moduli 3^K gains essentially nothing over the worst single modulus.
  - So "5/8 for q = 3^K" exceeds GRH. The remark that GRH would give *more* than 5/8 inverts the actual situation (fact 7).
- **Fix:** Withdraw the claim. At most, state the known 5/12 (Huxley) range for prime-power moduli.

### P04-03 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The 5/8 does not come out of the paper's own inequality: x^{1/2}Q^{5/8} ≤ x gives Q ≤ x^{4/5}
- **Where:** l.336–338 (Prop 3.3) and l.367–368 (main proof).
- **Quote:** "`E_{II} \ll x^{1/2}Q^{1/2}\cdot Q^{1/8+\varepsilon} = x^{1/2}Q^{5/8+\varepsilon}`. Power saving `E_{II}\ll x^{1-\varepsilon}` requires `Q\le x^{5/8-\varepsilon}`."
- **What is wrong:**
  - In general, x^{1/2}Q^c ≤ x^{1−ε} ⇔ Q ≤ x^{(1/2−ε)/c}. With c = 5/8 the threshold is Q ≤ x^{4/5}, not x^{5/8}. The paper has read the exponent of Q off as if it were the level.
  - The same paper computes the analogous step correctly at l.253–255: c = 3/4 gives Q ≤ x^{2/3}. The method is applied inconsistently between the two propositions.
  - Inserting Prop 3.3's own inputs into Prop 2.6's template, E = x^{1/2}·Q^{1/2}·(Kloosterman factor)^{1/2}:
    - per-modulus factor Q^{3/8} (l.329) gives x^{1/2}Q^{11/16}, i.e. level 8/11;
    - summed factor Q^{5/8} (l.332) gives x^{1/2}Q^{13/16}, i.e. level 8/13.
  - The factor Q^{1/8} at l.336 follows from neither reading. To land on level 5/8 one would need c = 4/5. (`exponents.py`)
- **Fix:** No consistent reading of the paper's own bounds produces 5/8. The number is inserted, not derived.

### P04-04 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The "beyond-Weil" Kloosterman bound |S(a,b;3^K)| ≪ (3^K)^{3/8+ε} is false; Weil's exponent 1/2 is sharp for 3^K
- **Where:** l.329 (also l.74–75, l.137–138, l.304–311). The HTML eq.(4) (p04.html:188) states the same bound in the form "(3^K)^{1/2−1/8}".
- **Quote:** "`|S(a,b;3^K)| \ll (3^K)^{3/8+\varepsilon}`".
- **What is wrong:**
  - Kloosterman sums to odd prime-power moduli have an explicit Salié-type evaluation (e.g. Iwaniec–Kowalski Ch. 12). For 3 ∤ ab with ab a square ℓ² mod 3^K, |S(a,b;3^K)| = 2·3^{K/2}·|cos(stuff)|.
  - So the sums are as large as Weil allows; no exponent below 1/2 is possible.
- **Evidence (`kloosterman.py`, exact FFT computation):**
  - max over 3∤b of |S(1,b;3^K)| / 3^{K/2} = 1.9966, 1.9985, 2.0000, … for K = 3…11.
  - The number of b with |S| > 3^{K/2} is 2·3^{K−2}.
  - Even for fixed (a,b) = (1,1), |S(1,1;3^K)|/3^{K/2} = 1.976, 1.9997, 2.0000, 2.0000 at K = 4, 6, 8, 10.
  - max|S|/q^{3/8} grows like q^{1/8}: 3.0, 4.6, 6.0, 7.9, 9.1 at K = 3, 6, 8, 10, 11.
- **Fix:** Remove Part II. No Kloosterman improvement exists for 3^K.

### P04-05 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The summation step is wrong, and the stated Prop 3.3 is *weaker* than Weil, not an improvement
- **Where:** l.306–311, l.331–332.
- **Quote:** "Summing over K with 3^K ≤ Q gives Σ_{3^K≤Q}|S(a,b;3^K)| ≪ Q^{5/8+ε}" and "improving the Weil bound … by 1/8 in the exponent".
- **What is wrong:**
  - Σ_{3^K≤Q} (3^K)^{3/8} is a geometric series: ≍ Q^{3/8} (ratio ≈ 2.96), not Q^{5/8}.
  - Weil summed over the family gives Σ 2·3^{K/2} ≪ Q^{1/2}, which is already **better** than Q^{5/8}. The displayed Prop 3.3 is therefore true but trivially so, and it is not an improvement.
  - The intermediate per-sum claim at l.329 is false (P04-04).
- **Fix:** Delete.

### P04-06 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The "ceiling 2/3 improved by 1/8 to 5/8" story is self-contradictory (direction of inequality and arithmetic)
- **Where:** l.69–70, 73–75, 80–82, 141–143, 255, 265–267, 270–274, 389–391. Also Lean l.102–119 and p04.html:166, 191.
- **Quote:** "gives a first ceiling of θ ≤ 2/3" … "improving the ceiling by 1/8 to θ = 5/8". The HTML has "from 2/3 to 5/8 (Part II)".
- **What is wrong:**
  1. **Direction.** A valid error bound for Q ≤ x^{2/3} proves level **≥ 2/3**, not "θ ≤ 2/3". If Part I worked, it would already beat 5/8, making Part II and the "uniqueness" theorem pointless.
  2. **A better estimate cannot lower the level.** Replacing Weil's q^{1/2} by a smaller q^{3/8} decreases the Q-exponent and so *raises* the admissible range (see P04-03). It cannot move the level from 2/3 down to 5/8.
  3. **Arithmetic.** 2/3 − 1/8 = 13/24 ≠ 5/8, and 2/3 − 5/8 = 1/24.
  4. **Three incompatible accounts of the "1/8":**
     - abstract: 2/3 → 5/8 "by 1/8";
     - l.310: Kloosterman exponent 1/2 → 3/8;
     - Lean `main_theorem_cascade`: level 1/2 + 1/8 = 5/8. This starts from 1/2, not from `weil_ceiling = 2/3`.
- **Fix:** No coherent reading exists. Remove the "ceiling" narrative.

### P04-07 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Prop 2.6's bound x^{1/2}Q^{3/4} is asserted without derivation, and the described mechanism is incoherent
- **Where:** l.247–268.
- **Quote:** "`E_{II}\ll x^{1/2}Q^{1/2}\cdot(Q^{1/2+\varepsilon})^{1/2}`" … "At each level, the inner sum over characters of Z/3^kZ contributes a factor ≤ Q^{1/2+ε} by the Weil bound … Three applications of Cauchy–Schwarz accumulate to Q^{3/4+ε}".
- **What is wrong:**
  - No bilinear form is opened.
  - No Poisson or completion step is shown that would produce a Kloosterman sum.
  - Sums over Dirichlet characters are not Kloosterman sums.
  - The displayed product contains one square root, not three nested Cauchy–Schwarz steps. Three nested steps would give exponents like 1/8 on the inner factor, as in Maynard's triply-well-factorable argument.
  - For the lacunary family, a bound of the form x^{1/2}Q^{3/4} is weaker than the trivial bound for large 3^K (P04-01), so it cannot be what drives any result.
- **Fix:** Prop 2.6 has no proof. It must be withdrawn or actually proved.

### P04-08 — MAJOR — VERIFIED (literature) — Watt's theorem is misdescribed; it says nothing about characters mod 3^K or pointwise Kloosterman sums
- **Where:** l.75–77, 314–329, 492–553, 560; bib l.620–623.
- **Quote:** "By Watt's theorem … the spectral mean value of Dirichlet polynomials over the family of primitive characters modulo 3^K satisfies moment estimates yielding |S(a,b;3^K)| ≪ (3^K)^{3/8+ε}".
- **What is wrong:**
  - N. Watt, J. Number Theory 53 (1995) 179–210, is a t-aspect mean value: ∫_0^T |ζ(1/2+it)|^4 |Σ_{m≤M} a_m m^{−it}|^2 dt ≪ T^{1+ε}(1 + M²T^{−1/2})·(coefficient norm).
  - It is proved via Deshouillers–Iwaniec / Kuznetsov bounds for sums of Kloosterman sums over many moduli. It was used by Baker–Harman–Pintz for primes in [x − x^{0.525}, x].
  - It involves no modulus q and no Dirichlet characters. Its hypotheses are not "M ≤ q, MR ≤ q², no Siegel zeros".
  - A mean value of Dirichlet polynomials cannot yield a pointwise bound for one Kloosterman sum. In any case such a bound is false (P04-04).
  - The parameters M = Q^{2/3}, R = Q^{1/3} (l.496–501) are never used anywhere in the proof. Their arithmetic (5/12, 5/24) is correct but idle.
- **Fix:** Remove the Watt "conditions". Cite Watt only for what it proves.

### P04-11 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Condition W3 conflates Siegel zeros with the spectral bound θ ≤ 7/32 (Kim–Sarnak), which is known unconditionally
- **Where:** l.514, 531–532, 545.
- **Quote:** "Condition W3 (Absence of Siegel zeros, θ_f ≤ 7/32)" … "Standard zero-density estimates for this family then give θ_f ≤ 7/32."
- **What is wrong:**
  - 7/32 is the Kim–Sarnak bound 7/64 in Deshouillers–Iwaniec normalisation. With λ_1 ≥ 1/4 − (7/64)² = 975/4096, √(1 − 4λ_1) = 7/32 (`exponents.py`).
  - It bounds exceptional Laplace eigenvalues of Maass forms. It has been unconditional since 2003 and has nothing to do with Siegel zeros of Dirichlet L-functions.
  - Zero-density estimates cannot produce it.
- **Fix:** Drop W3. It is not a hypothesis of Watt's theorem, and the spectral input is already known.

### P04-12 — MAJOR — VERIFIED (computed) — W3 Step 1 is false: the base-changed character does not have "matching" conductor
- **Where:** l.518–522.
- **Quote:** "The ramification (3) = (1−ω)² ensures that the conductor of Ψ_χ in Q(ω) matches the conductor of χ in Q exactly."
- **What is wrong:** For χ primitive mod 3^K, the conductor of Ψ = χ∘N:
  - is trivial for K = 1, since χ_{−3}∘N ≡ 1 because norms prime to 3 are ≡ 1 mod 3;
  - is (1−ω)^{2K−1}, of norm 3^{2K−1}, for K ≥ 2.
  - This is neither (3^K) = (1−ω)^{2K} nor of norm 3^K.
  - It agrees with the conductor–discriminant formula: 3·N(f_Ψ) = f(χ)·f(χχ_{−3}) = 3^{2K}.
- **Evidence:** brute force in `conductor.py` for K = 1…5.
- **Fix:** Correct the statement. In any case it is irrelevant (P04-13).

### P04-13 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — W3 Step 2 misattributes Stark/Baker, and the conclusion is trivial anyway (fact 6)
- **Where:** l.76–78, 524–526; bib l.570–573, 610–613.
- **Quote:** "Since Q(ω) is a CM field with class number 1, the Stark–Baker theorem gives an effective zero-free region for L(s,Ψ_χ) that precludes Siegel zeros."
- **What is wrong:**
  - Baker (1966) and Stark (1967) solve the class-number-one problem. Stark (1974) gives effective Brauer–Siegel results and localises possible Siegel zeros of Dedekind zeta functions to quadratic subfields.
  - Neither gives zero-free regions for Hecke L-functions of a fixed field with growing conductor. The class number of the fixed base field Q(ω) is irrelevant.
  - The claim itself is trivial: (Z/3^K)^* is cyclic of even order, so it has exactly one real non-principal character, induced from χ_{−3} mod 3. Its L-function equals L(s,χ_{−3}), a single fixed function. Numerically L(s,χ_{−3}) > 0 on (0,1): 0.349 at s = 0.05, 0.481 at 0.5, 0.602 at 0.99 (`lchi3.py`).
  - Complex characters have no exceptional zeros under the standard zero-free region.
  - Siegel zeros are irrelevant to any per-modulus level beyond 1/2 in any case.
- **Fix:** Replace with the one-line cyclicity argument, and cite no Stark/Baker.

### P04-14 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The "three-level filtration" does not exist; the filtration has 2K (or K) steps (propagates fact 3)
- **Where:** l.65–66, 204–222. Lean l.78–80.
- **Quote:** "The filtration has exactly three independent levels at scales 3, 3², 3³ (corresponding to j = 2, 4, 6)."
- **What is wrong:**
  - The (1−ω)-adic filtration of Z[ω]/(3^K) has 2K steps, each with quotient F_3 (this part is correct).
  - On the rational integers, Z/3^K has the K-step 3-adic filtration.
  - Nothing distinguishes j = 2, 4, 6. For K ≤ 2 there is no 3³ level at all, and for K > 3 the levels 3⁴, … are silently discarded.
  - The proof (l.220–221) only explains why even j correspond to rational powers of 3. It gives no reason for "three".
  - This is the same "mod 3 / mod 9 / mod 27" triple as the twin-prime "three levels" (fact 3). Those conditions add nothing beyond p ≡ 2 (mod 3). Numerically, the 58,979 twin-prime pairs with 3 < p ≤ 10^7 are equidistributed over the 3 classes ≡ 2 mod 3 modulo 9 (19,674 / 19,689 / 19,616) and over the 9 such classes modulo 27 (6,449–6,652 each).
  - Also, nothing in the argument uses Z[ω]. The problem lives in Z/3^K, while Z[ω]/(3^K) is a different ring of order 9^K.
- **Fix:** Delete Prop 2.4. By the programme's own criterion (C2), the depth is 1.

### P04-15 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — "Triply-well-factorable weights" are mis-defined and irrelevant: Theorem 1.1 contains no weights
- **Where:** l.66–68, 224–243, 360–362, 393–396, 405–410.
- **Quote:** "sieve weights w(d) supported on d | q admit a canonical three-scale decomposition w(d) = α(d₁)β(d₂)γ(d₃) … For general moduli q, no such canonical decomposition exists."
- **What is wrong:**
  - **Maynard's definition.** Weights λ_q are triply well-factorable of level Q if, for *every* Q₁Q₂Q₃ = Q, λ = α∗β∗γ (a Dirichlet convolution) with 1-bounded α, β, γ supported on [1,Q_i]. This is a property of a weight sequence over a dense set of moduli, not of a single modulus.
  - **The paper's version is broken.** A pointwise product w(3^j) = α(3^{j₁})β(3^{j₂})γ(3^{j₃}) is ill-defined, because 3^j = 3^{j₁}3^{j₂}3^{j₃} in many ways. If it is required for all splittings (with nonzero values), then f(j₁) + g(j₂) + h(j₃) = W(j₁+j₂+j₃) forces w to be geometric.
  - **Theorem 1.1 has no weights.** It has absolute values and a max over a. The known level > 1/2 results (BFI 4/7, Maynard 3/5, Lichtman, Pascadi 5/8) need a fixed residue a and well-factorable weights, not max_a |·| (fact 7).
- **Fix:** Delete Cor 2.5 and every appeal to it.

### P04-16 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Cyclicity of (Z/3^K)^* is neither special to 3 nor a source of cancellation
- **Where:** l.72–74, 137–138, 293–300, 318–320, 393–396.
- **Quote:** "This product structure obstructs the uniform character cancellation that a single cyclic group enables. The special role of cascade moduli 3^K is that (Z/3^KZ)^* is a single cyclic group".
- **What is wrong:**
  - (Z/q)^* is cyclic for q = 2, 4, p^k and 2p^k, so for every prime modulus and every odd prime power (5^K, 7^K, …).
  - For prime moduli p, the group is cyclic, yet Kloosterman sums are Sato–Tate distributed (Katz) and Weil's bound is attained asymptotically.
  - P04-04 shows the same for 3^K.
  - Nothing in Parts I or II uses p = 3 or ramification in Q(ω), so "the Eisenstein structure is specific to prime powers of 3" (l.242) is false.
- **Fix:** Remove the claim.

### P04-17 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The Thm 5.1 sharpness argument misuses Pascadi and GRH (facts 4 and 7)
- **Where:** l.398–401, 411–415, 561–562.
- **Quote:** "Sharpness follows from the matching lower bound in Pascadi for smooth moduli, which includes 3^K for all K sufficiently large" and "the lower bound matching our upper bound".
- **What is wrong:**

A single sparse family like 3^K inherits nothing from a dense average: from Σ_{q≤Q}|E(q)| ≪ x/(log x)^A one gets only |E(3^K)| ≪ x/(log x)^A, which is weaker than the trivial bound.
  - 3^K is not squarefree, so it is not even a Zhang/Polymath "smooth modulus".
  - "e.g. GRH" is wrong: GRH gives 1/2, not more (fact 7).
  - Thm 5.1(3) is also contradicted by P04-01.
- **Fix:** Delete Thm 5.1.

## Verdict
The main theorem is not proved in any meaningful sense.
- **As literally stated** (l.115–128), it is true but trivial for **every** exponent. The trivial bound covers 3^K > (log x)^{A+1} and Siegel–Walfisz covers the rest. So "θ_W = 5/8" has no content and the sharpness/uniqueness theorem is false.
- **The meaningful per-modulus version** (level 5/8 for each q = 3^K) would exceed what GRH gives (1/2). The best known results for prime-power moduli are around 5/12, with lower bounds of the right order up to 0.4736.
- **The "proof" does not establish either version.**
  - The first false step is Prop 2.4 (l.212–213): there is no "three-level" filtration.
  - Cor 2.5 then invents weights that Theorem 1.1 does not contain.
  - Prop 2.6's bound x^{1/2}Q^{3/4} is asserted without derivation.
  - Part II's pointwise Kloosterman bound q^{3/8} (l.329) is false, refuted by exact computation (|S| reaches 2·3^{K/2}).
  - Even granting every assertion, the paper's own final inequality x^{1/2}Q^{5/8} gives Q ≤ x^{4/5}, not x^{5/8}.
  - The Watt "conditions" W1 and W2 are verified by invalid logic and fail for most of the family. W3 is not a condition of Watt's theorem and conflates Siegel zeros with Kim–Sarnak's 7/32.
- **The Lean file** formalises only Eisenstein-integer arithmetic and the identity 1/2 + 1/8 = 5/8.
