# Audit of P09: "The Constitutional Constant θ_FFT = 1/2" (papers/p09-wakil-theta-fft/)

Files reviewed: `p09.tex` (700 lines; `p09.pdf` is a compile of it with identical content), `references.bib`, `P09_WakilThetaFFT.lean` (byte-identical to `lean/TheProgramme/P09_WakilThetaFFT.lean`), and the companion `p09.html` in the same folder, which is a *different* version: it carries the "k = 1 Constitutional Forcing" framing, which the .tex never states.
Scripts (all re-runnable): `audit/scripts/p09/{dof,energy,table,compress,complex_pascal,timing}.py` (numpy 2.5.3).

**Summary.** The one true fact here is classical: for real x, X[N−k] = conj X[k], so the real part of the DFT has N independent real parameters out of 2N. That is a fraction of 1/2 in *real* degrees of freedom, and the proof in lines 214–252 is correct for that count. Almost everything the paper builds on it is wrong or unsupported:
- the paper's own definition of θ_FFT (counting coefficients) never gives 1/2;
- the energy corollary is false;
- the "empirical" measure is a different quantity, it can never exceed 1/2, and some table entries are impossible under the paper's definition;
- the claimed "2× compression floor" is illusory, because the input already had only N reals;
- the Khayyam bridge rests on a wrong Hausdorff dimension and a wrong limit (the void fraction tends to 1, not 1/2);
- the Lean file proves only arithmetic and gives the DFT content as axioms.

## CRITICAL

### P09-01 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — Under the paper's own definition (counting coefficients), θ_FFT is never 1/2
- **Where:** p09.tex:192–196 (Def. 2.3), 209–211 (Thm 3.1), 117–119 (Thm 1.1), 251.
- **Quote:** "θ_FFT(x) := (number of upper-half coefficients derivable from lower half)/(total number of coefficients). For real-valued x we shall prove θ_FFT(x) = 1/2 exactly." and "x̂_k for k∈{0,…,⌊N/2⌋} uniquely determines x̂ entirely, and the fraction of independently informative coefficients is exactly θ_FFT = 1/2."
- **Problem:** Definition 2.3 counts complex coefficients.
  - Derivable (redundant) bins number N − ⌊N/2⌋ − 1. That gives 1/2 − 1/N for even N and 1/2 − 1/(2N) for odd N.
  - The determining set {0,…,⌊N/2⌋} has (⌊N/2⌋+1)/N bins, which is 1/2 + 1/N (even) or 1/2 + 1/(2N) (odd).
  - Neither is ever 1/2. Examples: N=2 gives 0 vs 1; N=4 gives 1/4 vs 3/4; N=8 gives 3/8 vs 5/8; N=32768 gives 16383/32768 vs 16385/32768.
  - The proof gets 1/2 only by switching silently to a different measure (real degrees of freedom: N out of 2N, line 251). The DC and Nyquist edge cases are exactly what make the coefficient count differ from 1/2.
- **Evidence:** `dof.py`. The rank of the real map x ↦ (Re X, Im X) is N for every N tested (2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 16, 17), so the real-dof ratio is exactly 1/2. The coefficient fractions are as listed above.
- **Fix:** Define θ_FFT as (dim_ℝ of the image of ℝ^N under the DFT)/(dim_ℝ ℂ^N) = N/2N. State explicitly that in complex bins the independent count is ⌊N/2⌋+1 of N, not N/2. Remove "coefficients" from Thm 1.1, Thm 3.1 and Def 2.3.

### P09-02 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The "2× lossless compression floor" is illusory: the half-spectrum holds exactly as many reals as the input
- **Where:** p09.tex:139–147, 568–577 (η_const = 2), 579–586 (Thm 7.1), 609–616 ("67× … decomposes as 2 × 33.5"), 680–683. Also p09.html:202 ("factor of 2 is constitutionally guaranteed, and a factor of ≈1365 arises from … sparsity").
- **Quote:** "retains only the independent spectral half achieves at minimum a 2× lossless compression … The constitutional floor η_const = 2 is exact and universal for real inputs."
- **Problem:**
  - A real tensor of N reals maps to a half-spectrum (rfft) that stores ⌊N/2⌋+1 complex numbers. Two of their imaginary parts are identically 0, so that is exactly N reals.
  - The "2×" is measured against the 2N-real full complex spectrum, which is itself a 2× inflation of the input that nobody stores. Relative to the weights, η_const = 1.
  - So η_total = 2·η_sparse double-counts. The "2 × 33.5 = 67×" and "2 × 1365 = 2730×" decompositions are wrong in their factor of 2.
  - "Tight … achieved by white-noise inputs" (585) is vacuous: white noise gets 1× relative to the input.
  - In floating point the FFT round-trip is not bit-lossless.
- **Evidence:** `compress.py`. For N = 32768, rfft gives 16385 bins, i.e. 32768 stored reals, with Im X0 = Im X_{N/2} = 0. That equals the 32768 input reals.
- **Fix:** State that conjugate symmetry gives no compression relative to the real input; it only avoids a 2× inflation. Set η_const = 1 and remove the factor 2 from every total-compression decomposition.

### P09-03 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The Khayyam bridge's "1/2" is false: the mod-2 void fraction tends to 1, and the paper's own formula gives 1/3
- **Where:** p09.tex:476–482, 497 (Table 6.1), 513–526 (proof sketch), 676–678 (Conclusion 4). Also p09.html:191 and 193–194 (promoted to "Theorem 6.1").
- **Quote:** "void fraction → 1 − 1/p^{d_p−1}. For p = 2 this gives void fraction → 1/2: exactly half the entries in the binary case vanish." HTML: "half the binomial coefficients are even in the limit" and "θ_Khayyam,2 = 1/2 be the mod-2 survivor fraction".
- **Problem:**
  - By Lucas/Fine, the first p^m rows contain (p(p+1)/2)^m nonzero entries out of p^m(p^m+1)/2. The nonzero fraction is ~2((p+1)/(2p))^m → 0, so the void fraction → 1 for every p. Almost all binomial coefficients are even.
  - The paper's formula 1 − 1/p^{d_p−1}, with its own d_p = log(p+1)/log p, equals 1/(p+1). That is 1/3 for p=2, not 1/2: an arithmetic error on top of a wrong formula.
  - The proof sketch itself (518–519) says the survival fraction 3^m/C(2^m+1,2) → 0. That contradicts the statement.
  - The "conjugate pairing of rows k and 2^m−k" (521) does not exist: those rows have different lengths (k+1 vs 2^m−k+1).
  - "Void" entries are not "derivable from the rest" in the DFT sense, so the two "redundancies" are not the same concept.
  - The true binomial analogue of conjugate symmetry is C(n,k) = C(n,n−k). It gives ≈1/2 redundancy for any modulus and has nothing to do with Lucas or the fractal.
  - The sketch admits "the full duality … warrants a separate detailed treatment". The proposition is unproved, yet the HTML calls it "Theorem 6.1" and the Conclusion calls it "exact".
- **Evidence:** `complex_pascal.py`. Void fraction for p=2 at m=1..7 is 0, 0.10, 0.25, 0.40, 0.54, 0.65, 0.74, rising toward 1. For p=3 at m=1..5 it is 0, 0.20, 0.43, 0.61, 0.74. The nonzero counts match (p(p+1)/2)^m exactly.
- **Fix:** Withdraw Prop 6.2, Table 6.1 row "Fraction redundant", Conclusion item 4, and HTML Thm 6.1. If a binomial analogue is wanted, use row symmetry C(n,k) = C(n,n−k).

### P09-04 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The "empirical θ_FFT" is a different quantity, can never exceed 1/2, and the table contains an impossible value
- **Where:** p09.tex:284–293 (definition), 316–333 (Table 4.1), 335–338, 445–451 (Remark), 82 and 669–672 (abstract and conclusion "max deviation < 3×10⁻⁴").
- **Quote:** "θ^emp := Σ_{k>N/2}|x̂_k|² / Σ_k |x̂_k|²"; table row "Attention weights (sparse) & 0.500247 & 2.5×10⁻⁴".
- **Problem:**
  - For real x, conjugate symmetry gives the identity θ^emp = 1/2 − (|X_0|² + |X_{N/2}|²)/(2E), with no Nyquist term for odd N. So θ^emp ≤ 1/2 always, with equality iff the DC and Nyquist bins are both 0.
  - The value 0.500247 > 1/2 is therefore impossible for the stated 1-D measure on real data.
  - θ^emp is data-dependent: a constant signal or (−1)^n gives θ^emp = 0, and a signal with mean 0.1 gives 0.495.
  - θ^emp is an energy ratio, not a count of derivable coefficients. It cannot tell real from complex input: x = (1+2i)·x_R has exactly the same θ^emp as x_R. It therefore does not "confirm" θ_FFT.
  - "Empirical confirmation" of a two-line identity is in any case meaningless. Only σ_mirror (≈ 1 − 3×10⁻¹⁶) checks the symmetry.
- **Evidence:**
  - `energy.py`: the identity holds to 1e-16 on N = 7, 8, 33, 32768. The maximum θ^emp over 20,000 random real signals is 0.49999997. ones(N) and (−1)^n both give 0.0.
  - `table.py`, 200 seeds per case, shapes as described (512×64, N=32768):
    - The stated 1-D flattened measure always lies in [0.4998, 0.5000], with typical deviation ≈1/N ≈ 3×10⁻⁵, not < 10⁻⁶. The probability that it rounds to 0.500000 is only 1.5–3% per run.
    - Per-axis FFTs give 0.484–0.498.
    - Values above 0.5, such as 0.5002–0.502, occur only if a 2-D FFT is split by flattened index. That split is not a conjugate-pair partition (row N₁/2 is cut in half), so the deviation is structural, not numerical.
- **Fix:** Remove Table 4.1 and the "empirical confirmation" claim from the title and abstract. If kept, report max|X[N−k] − conj X[k]|/‖X‖ (≈10⁻¹⁶), and state θ^emp = 1/2 − (|X_0|²+|X_{N/2}|²)/(2E).

### P09-05 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The explanation of the 2.5×10⁻⁴ deviation is incoherent
- **Where:** p09.tex:335–338, 445–451.
- **Quote:** "arises from numerical precision in the mirror-symmetry quotient at very small denominator values" and "comes from … the ε stabiliser in the denominator of σ_mirror".
- **Problem:**
  - The deviation is reported in the θ^emp column. The formula for θ^emp has no ε and does not involve σ_mirror.
  - σ_mirror in the same row is 1.000000.
  - The denominator ‖x̂_{N−k}‖ is a norm over the whole spectrum, so it is not small.
  - Double-precision FFT error is ~10⁻¹⁶ relative (the paper itself bounds it at < 5×10⁻¹³, line 440), so 2.5×10⁻⁴ cannot be precision.
  - Per P09-04, the real cause is the unpaired DC/Nyquist energy (1-D), or the non-pairwise split (2-D).
- **Evidence:** `energy.py`, `table.py`; σ_mirror = 0.9999999999999997 for N = 32768.
- **Fix:** Remove the remark and attribute any deviation to the DC/Nyquist (or partition) terms.

### P09-06 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Corollary 3.2 ("Energy conservation") is false as stated, for every N
- **Where:** p09.tex:254–266.
- **Quote:** "Σ_{k=1}^{⌊N/2⌋}|x̂_k|² = Σ_{k=⌈N/2⌉+1}^{N−1}|x̂_k|²."
- **Problem:** The index bounds are wrong.
  - Even N: the left side includes the self-paired Nyquist bin k = N/2.
  - Odd N: the right side starts one index too late and drops the partner of k = (N−1)/2.
  - Counterexample: the delta signal (all |X_k| = 1) gives LHS = RHS + 1 for every N, e.g. N=4: 2 vs 1; N=5: 2 vs 1; N=2: 1 vs 0.
  - The label "energy conservation" is also a misnomer: that name belongs to Parseval, and this is magnitude symmetry.
- **Evidence:** `energy.py` (delta and random counterexamples for N = 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9). The corrected version Σ_{k=1}^{⌈N/2⌉−1} = Σ_{k=⌊N/2⌋+1}^{N−1} checks numerically.
- **Fix:** Use Σ_{k=1}^{⌈N/2⌉−1}|x̂_k|² = Σ_{k=⌊N/2⌋+1}^{N−1}|x̂_k|² and rename it "magnitude symmetry".

### P09-07 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Wrong Hausdorff dimension for Pascal's triangle mod p (d_3 = log4/log3 ≈ 1.262 should be log6/log3 ≈ 1.631)
- **Where:** p09.tex:467–470, 533, 549, 645. This is inherited from papers/p02-khayyams-triangle/Paper_02_Khayyams_Triangle.tex:108–110 and P02_KhayyamsTriangle.lean:11.
- **Quote:** "Hausdorff dimension d_p = log(p+1)/log p" and "d_3 = log 4/log 3 ≈ 1.262".
- **Problem:**
  - The first p^m rows contain (p(p+1)/2)^m nonzero entries, so the dimension is log(p(p+1)/2)/log p. For p = 3 that is log 6/log 3 = 1.6309.
  - The paper's formula agrees with the correct one only at p = 2, because p+1 = p(p+1)/2 iff p = 2.
  - log4/log3 is the Koch-curve dimension.
  - The bib's own Reiter (1993) entry, the standard reference, gives ln[p(p+1)/2]/ln p. OEIS A083093 and A153459 give log6/log3 = 1.63093 for mod 3.
- **Evidence:** `complex_pascal.py`. The direct count gives 6, 36, 216, 1296, 7776 = 6^m for p = 3. The table of d_p (paper vs true): p=3 1.2619 vs 1.6309; p=5 1.1133 vs 1.6826; p=7 1.0686 vs 1.7124.
- **Fix:** d_p = log(p(p+1)/2)/log p and d_3 = log 6/log 3 ≈ 1.631 throughout. The same fix is needed in p02.

### P09-09 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Proposition 5.1 (complex inputs) is false in both directions
- **Where:** p09.tex:366–386.
- **Quote:** "θ_FFT(x) = 1/2 ⟺ x̂_I and x̂_R carry equal total power. In general θ_FFT(x) ∈ (0,1)".
- **Problem:**
  - With X = A + iB (A, B the DFTs of x_R and x_I), |X_k|² − |X_{N−k}|² = 4·Im(A_k·conj B_k). Upper/lower balance depends on this cross term, not on ‖A‖ = ‖B‖. The proof ignores the cross terms.
  - Counterexample to ⇒: x = (1+2i)x_R has power ratio 4 but θ^emp equal to the real case (0.499621 = θ(x_R)). Under the counting definition it is also as redundant as a real signal, since X_{N−k} = ((1+2i)/(1−2i))·conj X_k.
  - Counterexample to ⇐: x = e^{2πin/N} has equal powers but θ^emp = 0.
  - θ = 0 and θ = 1 are both attained (e^{∓2πin/N}), which contradicts "∈ (0,1)". Line 366 itself says [0,1].
- **Evidence:** `complex_pascal.py`.
- **Fix:** The correct statement is that upper and lower energies are equal iff Σ_{k lower} Im(A_k conj B_k) = 0, together with the DC/Nyquist caveat.

### P09-13 — MAJOR — VERIFIED (construction) / SUSPECT (what was actually run) — The 2730× at 96% energy cannot come from the described "low-rank plus Gaussian noise" matrices; the gradient-descent explanation has no support
- **Where:** p09.tex:301–311, 326–328, 342–350, 682–683.
- **Quote:** "structured weights achieve 2730× FFT compression at 96% energy because gradient-descent training drives weight tensors onto low-dimensional manifolds in frequency space".
- **Problem:**
  - The test matrices are synthetic ("low-rank plus Gaussian noise"), not trained weights, so nothing about gradient descent was tested.
  - Low rank does not imply DFT sparsity: the 2-D DFT of UVᵀ is (FU)(FV)ᵀ, which is still rank r but dense.
  - With random factors (rank 1–16, noise 0.1), 96% energy needs 19,871–23,767 of 32,768 coefficients (1.4–1.6×), not about 12 (32768/12 = 2730.67 → 2730).
  - The 2730× therefore implies an FFT-sparse construction (e.g. a few pure sinusoids), which is circular.
  - The "Random control 5× at 52%" entry is consistent with i.i.d. Gaussian noise (f(1−ln f) = 0.522 at f = 0.2; simulated 0.518–0.521), so it is the only reproducible entry.
  - The "moderate" entry in a numerical column is not a measurement.
  - "Attention (key/query) weights … modelling MoE expert layers" is a mislabel: MoE experts are FFN blocks.
- **Evidence:** `compress.py`.
- **Fix:** Publish the generator and code, or drop the 2730× claim and the causal sentence.

### P09-14 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The "constitutional universality" conjecture is refuted by its own listed example (wavelets); real-to-real transforms have zero redundancy
- **Where:** p09.tex:651–657.
- **Quote:** "For every real-valued signal domain equipped with a natural basis change (Fourier, modular, wavelet, p-adic), there exists a constitutional constant θ ∈ (0,1) such that θ of the representation … is derivable from the remainder".
- **Problem:**
  - Any real invertible transform ℝ^N → ℝ^N (orthonormal Haar or other wavelets, DCT, Hartley) has no derivable coefficients, so θ = 0, which is outside (0,1).
  - This also shows θ_FFT = 1/2 is an artefact of using a complex-valued representation. The real Discrete Hartley Transform (Bracewell 1983) carries the same information with no redundancy.
- **Evidence:** `timing.py`: the Haar matrix is orthogonal with rank 64/64; the Hartley cas-matrix has rank 64/64.
- **Fix:** Withdraw the conjecture.

### P09-15 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The k = 1 "Constitutional Forcing" framing (HTML/website/Lean) is a relabelling, uses a different formula, and the value 1/2 cannot identify depth k = 1
- **Where:** p09.html:150, 183–187; P09_WakilThetaFFT.lean:75–78; EssayDetail.tsx:1018; programme.ts:111.
- **Quote:** "(CF2) configuration space of size 2¹ = 2: {upper half informative, upper half redundant}; (CF3) exactly one configuration class invalid …; (CF4) θ_FFT = (2¹−1)/2¹ = 1/2. This is identical in formal structure to the Bombieri–Vinogradov barrier".
- **Problem:**
  - The "binary configuration space" is invented after the fact. Nothing in the DFT is a choice between "informative" and "redundant" halves. The redundancy is the codimension of a real N-dimensional subspace inside ℂ^N.
  - The formula used is (2^k − 1)/2^k, not the programme's (2^k − k)/2^k. They coincide only at k = 1.
  - Under the programme's formula, θ_1 = θ_2 = 1/2, so the value 1/2 does not single out depth 1. This is the same ambiguity that sank the "Kolmogorov 3/4 at k = 2" claim.
  - Bombieri–Vinogradov's 1/2 comes from the large sieve, not from a configuration count.
  - The .tex version never makes the k = 1 claim at all, so the two versions of p09 disagree.
- **Evidence:** arithmetic: (2²−2)/2² = 1/2.
- **Fix:** Drop the k = 1 "forcing" claim, or give a non-circular criterion that distinguishes k = 1 from k = 2.

### P09-17 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The Lean file formalises no DFT mathematics; every DFT statement is an axiom and the "theorems" are arithmetic
- **Where:** P09_WakilThetaFFT.lean:32–42, 48–60, 67–69, 76–80, 87–91; LEAN_VERIFICATION_REPORT.md:229–245; paper subtitle "Formal Proof".
- **Quote:** `axiom conjugate_symmetry …`, `def thetaFFT : ℚ := 1 / 2`, `axiom structural_bridge : True`.
- **Problem:**
  - Conjugate symmetry, DC realness, Nyquist realness and per-bin magnitude symmetry are all `axiom`s. The axioms are true statements, so they are not inconsistent, but none is proved, even though each is a 3-line computation.
  - `energy_conservation` is merely a consequence of `conjugate_symmetry`, yet it is added as a separate axiom.
  - The "verified" theorems have no link to the DFT:
    - `thetaFFT` is the literal 1/2.
    - `theta_fft_value` proves N/(2N) = 1/2.
    - `independent_dof_even` proves 1+1+2(N/2−1) = N, an arithmetic identity, not independence. The odd-N case is absent.
    - `khayyam_bridge` proves 1/2 = (2¹−1)/2¹, using 2^k−1, not 2^k−k.
    - `compression_floor` proves 1/(1/2) = 2.
  - `axiom structural_bridge : True` is vacuous.
  - The verification report lists 4 axioms for P09, but the file has 5.
  - (SUSPECT, not checked because no Lean toolchain is installed here) `field_simp` in `theta_fft_value` needs (N:ℚ) ≠ 0, which its discharger may not derive from `hN : N ≥ 2`.
- **Fix:** Prove `conjugate_symmetry` from `dftCoeff` (exp periodicity plus conj of a sum) and derive the rest. Delete `structural_bridge`. Correct the report's axiom count, and stop calling the file a formal proof of θ_FFT.

### P09-20 — MINOR — VERIFIED — Accounts of the history and novelty are inaccurate ("sixty years", "1965 — named 2026")
- **Where:** p09.html:219; CoverLetter.tsx:78, 137; Home.tsx:288; EssayDetail.tsx:1016–1018. (p09.tex:122–123 itself correctly says "classical".)
- **Problem:**
  - Hermitian symmetry of the Fourier coefficients of real data predates the FFT by well over a century. It is why real trigonometric (cos/sin) series are used: Euler and Lagrange, Fourier (1807/1822), and Gauss's c.1805 interpolation treatise, published posthumously in *Werke* III (1866), see Heideman–Johnson–Burrus, IEEE ASSP Mag. 1(4):14–21, Oct 1984.
  - Real-input FFT algorithms that exploit it date at least to Cooley–Lewis–Welch (1970) and Sorensen–Jones–Heideman–Burrus (IEEE TASSP 1987).
  - It already has standard names: "Hermitian symmetry" or "conjugate symmetry"; FFTW calls it "Hermitian redundancy". So "named 2026" is a rebranding, not a discovery.
  - The website's "Gauss had the algorithm in 1805 and did not publish it" is broadly correct (published posthumously in 1866).
  - p09 makes no speed-up claim. Real-input FFTs are ~2× cheaper; numpy here gives rfft 235 µs vs complex fft 722 µs at N = 32768 (`timing.py`).
