# Audit of p07: "Condition W3: Absence of Siegel Zeros for Characters Modulo 3^K via the CM Structure of Z[ω]"

Files: `papers/p07-condition_w3/p07.tex` (719 lines), `papers/p07-condition_w3/P07_ConditionW3.lean` (90 lines). I also read `p07.html`, `papers/p04-that-eisenstein-guy/p04.tex` and the p07 part of `LEAN_VERIFICATION_REPORT.md`.

Scripts: `audit/scripts/p07/p07_checks.py` and `audit/scripts/p07/p04_trivial.py`. Both have been run and their outputs are quoted below.

## What "Condition W3" is, as stated

**Theorem 1.1** (p07.tex:127-138). For every *primitive* χ mod q = 3^K, L(s,χ) has no **real** zero in σ > 1 − c/log q, with c absolute and effective. It then adds: "In particular, Watt's condition θ_f ≤ 7/32 holds".

**Proof structure.** The proof runs:

1. lift χ to a "primitive Hecke character Ψ_χ of Q(√−3) of conductor 𝔭^{2K}";
2. apply a "Stark–Baker theorem" giving a real-zero-free region for every Hecke L-function of every CM field;
3. use L(s,Ψ_χ) = L(s,χ)L(s,χε).

**Verdict in one line.** The statement is true, but it is classical and almost trivial (finding F4). The proof is invalid: Step 2 states an open problem as a theorem, and Step 1 is false in several ways. W3 supplies nothing that a level 5/8 result needs.

## Findings

### F1 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The "Stark–Baker theorem" (Thm 3.2) is not a theorem: as stated it would solve the Landau–Siegel zero problem
- **Where:** p07.tex:309-317, also :83-85, :342-349, :622-627. The same claim appears in p04.tex:77-78 and :524-526.
- **Quote:** "Let $F$ be a CM field and let $\Psi$ be a Hecke character of $F$ of conductor $\mathfrak{f}$. Then $L(s,\Psi)$ has no real zero in the region $\sigma > 1 - c_F/\log(\Nf(\mathfrak{f})\cdot|\disc(F)|)$ for an effectively computable constant $c_F>0$ depending only on $[F:\Q]$."
- **What is wrong:** Take F = Q(√−d), which is any imaginary quadratic field and hence CM of degree 2, and take Ψ trivial. Then L(s,Ψ) = ζ_F(s) = ζ(s)L(s,χ_{−d}). Since ζ(σ) < 0 on (0,1), the "theorem" says that L(s,χ_{−d}) has no real zero in σ > 1 − c_2/log d, for **every** d, with c_2 absolute and effective.
  - Base change also covers even real characters: take F = Q(√−3) and Ψ = χ_D∘N, so that L(s,Ψ) = L(s,χ_D)L(s,χ_Dχ_{−3}).
  - So the statement implies that no Siegel zero exists for any real character, with an effective constant. That would give the effective bound h(−d) ≫ √d/log d.
  - This is the open Landau–Siegel zero problem. The best effective lower bound known is of order log d (Goldfeld–Gross–Zagier; Oesterlé).
- **What Stark and Baker actually proved:** Stark (1967) and Baker (1966) proved the class-number-one theorem: the nine fields listed are the only imaginary quadratic fields with h = 1. That theorem says nothing about zero-free regions of Hecke L-functions of Q(√−3).
  - Q(√−3) having h = 1 was already known to Gauss.
  - Heegner (1952) is omitted.
- **Errors inside the proof sketch:**
  - (i) "since $\bar\Psi = \Psi\circ\iota$" (:328) is false in general. For Ψ = χ∘N we have Ψ∘ι = Ψ but Ψ̄ = χ̄∘N, which differs from Ψ whenever χ is complex.
  - (ii) No form of Baker's theorem bounds "$L(\beta,\Psi)\cdot L(\beta,\chi_{-3})$ … away from zero in the effective zero-free region" (:333-335).
  - (iii) "See [IwKow2004], Chapter 11, for the complete argument" (:337-338). Iwaniec–Kowalski Chapter 11 is "Sums over finite fields". Class-number bounds are in Chapters 22-23.
  - (iv) The theorem carries the citation GoldfeldSzpiro1995 (:309). That paper ("Bounds for the order of the Tate–Shafarevich group", Compositio 97 (1995) 71-87) is about Sha and Szpiro's conjecture and is unrelated.
  - (v) p04.tex:525 cites Stark 1974 ("Some effective cases of the Brauer–Siegel theorem") for this step. Stark 1974 shows that a Siegel zero of ζ_K for an almost-normal field K must come from a quadratic subfield. It gives nothing for a quadratic field itself, and it does not exclude Siegel zeros.
- **Related false statements:**
  - Remark 3.3 (:347-349): "For non-CM fields, Siegel zeros remain possible unconditionally: the CM condition is essential to exclude them." This is false, because every imaginary quadratic field is CM and the Siegel-zero question for its ζ_F is exactly the open problem.
  - Acknowledgements (:622-625): "Alan Baker and Harold Stark, whose independent proofs of the class number 1 problem … provide the effective zero-free region". This is false; their theorem gives no zero-free region.
  - The abstract (:83-85) says class number 1 is what makes Step 2 work, yet Thm 3.2 has no class-number hypothesis. The paper is inconsistent with itself here.
- **Fix:** Delete Thm 3.2 and everything that depends on it. If a citation is wanted, the correct facts are Landau/Page: at most one exceptional zero, and it belongs to a real character. They are used in F4.

### F2 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — The "canonical lifting" (Prop 2.5) is false: no Hecke character can satisfy χ(n) = Ψ_χ((n)), and the construction actually given yields χ(n)²
- **Where:** p07.tex:210-263, with the abstract at :77-82.
- **Quote:** "$\chi(n) = \Psi_\chi\bigl((n)\bigr)$ for all $n \in \Z$ with $\gcd(n,3)=1$" (:216-217). The derivation reads: "$\Psi_\chi((n)) = \chi(n^2 \bmod 3^K)\cdot(\text{correction})$. The correction is $\chi(n)^{-1}\cdot\chi(n^2) = \chi(n)$ … giving $\Psi_\chi((n)) = \chi(n)$" (:259-262).
- **What is wrong:**
  - (a) A Hecke character evaluated on ideals cannot distinguish (n) from (−n), so the identity forces χ(−1) = 1. Exactly half of the characters mod 3^K are odd: −1 is the element of order 2 in a cyclic group of order 2·3^{K−1}. For those, the identity is impossible for any Grössencharacter, of finite or infinite order.
  - (b) The paper actually builds α ↦ χ(N(α)), i.e. Ψ = χ∘N (:230-232). For that Ψ, Ψ((n)) = χ(n²) = χ(n)², which equals χ(n) only when χ(n) = 1.
  - (c) The algebra is wrong on its own terms: χ(n²)·χ(n) = χ(n)³, not χ(n).
  - (d) Lemma 4.1 (:416) uses Ψ_χ(𝔮) = χ(ℓ)² for inert ℓ, which is the χ∘N convention. Prop 2.5 at n = ℓ gives Ψ_χ((ℓ)) = χ(ℓ). These contradict each other. For example, ℓ = 2 is inert, and χ(2) is a primitive root of unity of order ord(χ), because 2 generates (Z/3^K)^×.
- **Fix:** Define Ψ_χ := χ∘N_{K/Q}, drop the identity χ(n) = Ψ((n)), and keep only L(s,χ∘N) = L(s,χ)L(s,χε), which is correct for this Ψ.

### F3 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The conductor of the lift is 𝔭^{2K−1} for K ≥ 2 (trivial for K = 1), not 𝔭^{2K}; the "conductor matching" picture is backwards
- **Where:** p07.tex:79-82, :213-214, :244-254, :265-273, :366, :489-490, :562-566.
- **Quotes:**
  - "A primitive character $\chi$ of conductor $3^K$ induces a Hecke character $\Psi_\chi$ of conductor $\pp^{2K}$ because $(3^K)=\pp^{2K}$" (:245-247).
  - "For unramified primes $\ell\neq3$, the conductor of the lifted character in $\K$ would be $\ell^K$ (inert) or $\pp^K\bar\pp^K$ (split), neither of which matches $\ell^K$ exactly" (:270-272).
- **Evidence:**
  - The norm maps local unit groups as N(U_K^{(m)}) = U_Q^{(⌈m/2⌉)} for m ≥ 1, and N(U_K^{(0)}) = U_Q^{(1)}. Hence f(χ∘N) = 𝔭^{2K−1} for K ≥ 2 and (1) for K = 1.
  - The conductor–discriminant formula agrees: |d_K|·N𝔣 = f(χ)f(χε) gives 3·N𝔣 = 3^K·3^K, so N𝔣 = 3^{2K−1}.
  - Brute force in `p07_checks.py` §3 finds conductor exponents {0, 3, 5, 7} for K = 1..4. The paper claims {2, 4, 6, 8}.
  - For an unramified prime the opposite holds: f(χ∘N) = ℓ^K·O_K exactly. The script confirms this for inert ℓ = 5, K = 1, 2.
  - The "primitivity" argument (:251-254) only considers even exponents 𝔭^{2j}, which is why it misses 2K−1. In Cor 3.4 (:366), N(𝔣) = 3^{2K} should be 3^{2K−1}; this does not change the asymptotics.
- **So:** The supposed "geometric heart" (:267) is false. Ramification at 3 is exactly where the conductor does **not** extend.
- **Fix:** Replace with f(χ∘N) = 𝔭^{2K−1} (K ≥ 2), and delete Remark 2.6 and Prop 5.1(1).

### F4 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Condition W3 as stated is classical and almost trivial, so the CM/Hecke route is unnecessary
- **Where:** p07.tex:127-138 and :122-123 ("Condition (W3) is the substantive analytic condition").
- **Evidence:**
  - (Z/3^K)^× is cyclic of order 2·3^{K−1}, so there is exactly one real non-principal character mod 3^K. It has conductor 3, i.e. it is χ_{−3}.
  - `p07_checks.py` §1, for K = 1..5: the real characters have conductors {1, 3}, and the number of primitive *real* characters is 1, 0, 0, 0, 0. So for K ≥ 2 **every primitive χ mod 3^K is complex**. For complex χ, the classical de la Vallée-Poussin/Landau argument (Davenport Ch. 14) already rules out every zero, real ones included, in σ ≥ 1 − c₀/log(q(|t|+2)), with c₀ absolute and effective.
  - For K = 1 the only primitive character is χ_{−3}, which is one fixed L-function. L(1,χ_{−3}) = π/(3√3) = 0.6045997880780726, matching mpmath to 30 digits. L(0,χ_{−3}) = 1/3. Over a 2000-point grid on (0,1), L(σ,χ_{−3}) ≥ 0.3335, so there is no real zero in (0,1) at all.
  - Hence W3 holds with an absolute effective c, by results going back to Landau (1918). The base change is also circular: since L(s,χ∘N) = L(s,χ)L(s,χε), any zero-free region for L(s,Ψ_χ) is the *same* statement as one for the pair χ, χε mod 3^K. The detour through Q(√−3) adds nothing.
- **Fix:** Replace the paper by the paragraph above, credited to Landau/Page and Davenport Ch. 14.

### F5 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — W3 cannot "render θ_W = 5/8 unconditional"; the claimed implication is false at every level
- **Where:** p07.tex:72-74, :134-137, :588-616 (Thm 7.1 and Cor 7.2), :574-578.
- **Quote:** "Theorem 1.1 of [WakilEisenstein]---the level of distribution $\theta_W=5/8$ for cascade moduli---holds unconditionally." (:607-608)
- **What is wrong (1): the zero-free region is far too thin.** A zero-free region of width c/log q only matters for q ≤ exp(c√log x), the Siegel–Walfisz range. At q = x^{5/8}, a zero at β = 1 − c/log q contributes x^β = x·e^{−8c/5}: a constant factor, with no saving at all (`p07_checks.py` §6). Power-range results for q = 3^K need Gallagher/Iwaniec zero-free regions for **complex** characters of large conductor (Postnikov's method) and zero-density bounds. W3 says nothing about either, and even those inputs reach only about x^{3/8} to x^{0.47} (Barban–Linnik–Tshudakov, Gallagher, Huxley, Banks–Shparlinski).
- **What is wrong (2): a meaningful level 5/8 is beyond GRH.** For a single modulus, GRH gives an error of √x·log²x, which is o(x/φ(q)) only for q ≤ x^{1/2−ε}.
- **What is wrong (3): p04's theorem as written does not need W3 at all.** p04 Thm 1.1 (p04.tex:115-128) sums over the O(log x) moduli 3^K only, so it is trivially true for **any** exponent below 1:
  - each term is ≤ (x/3^K + 1)·log x + x/φ(3^K);
  - the terms with 3^K ≥ (log x)^{A+2} add up to ≪ x/(log x)^{A+1};
  - the O(log log x) terms with 3^K < (log x)^{A+2} are handled by Siegel–Walfisz, which is effective here because the only real character is χ_{−3}.
  - `p04_trivial.py` at x = 10^7: the sum over **all** 3^K ≤ x, i.e. up to x^{0.954}, is 0.00096·x.
  - So W3, Watt and the Eisenstein structure are all irrelevant to p04's statement as written.
- **Fix:** Withdraw Cor 7.2 and the abstract claim. State that W3 is a Siegel–Walfisz-range input only.

### F6 — CRITICAL — VERIFIED — "Watt's theorem" with conditions W1–W3 is not Watt's theorem, and "θ_f ≤ 7/32" is the Kim–Sarnak bound, which has nothing to do with Siegel zeros
- **Where:** p07.tex:106-123, :134-137, :467-472, :588-602. The same claim is in p04.tex:514-516, :532, :545.
- **Quotes:**
  - "Watt's theorem requires three conditions on the family of Dirichlet characters modulo $q=3^K$: (W1) $M\le q$ … (W2) $MR\le q^2$ … (W3) No Siegel zeros" (:108-118).
  - "The complete absence of Siegel zeros … combined with the standard zero-density estimate … gives Watt's condition $\theta_f\le7/32$" (:467-472).
- **What is wrong:**
  - Watt, J. Number Theory 53 (1995) 179-210, is a mean-value theorem for ∫|ζ(1/2+it)|⁴|P(it)|² dt, proved with the Kuznetsov formula. It is in the t-aspect, involves no family of characters mod q, and has no "M ≤ q", "MR ≤ q²" or Siegel-zero hypothesis.
  - "θ ≤ 7/32" is the Kim–Sarnak bound toward Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture: θ = √max(0, 1−4λ₁), in the Deshouillers–Iwaniec/Pascadi normalisation, which is 2·7/64. It is an **unconditional theorem for every level q** about exceptional Maass eigenvalues, so nothing needs to be "verified" for 3^K.
  - Neither the absence of Siegel zeros nor any zero-density estimate implies a bound on Maass eigenvalues. Step "W3 ⇒ θ_f ≤ 7/32" is a non sequitur.
  - In Pascadi's work, 7/32 enters as θ_max in (5−4θ_max)/(8−6θ_max). With θ_max = 7/32 this gives 66/107.
- **Fix:** Remove W1–W3 as "Watt's conditions". If Kim–Sarnak is needed, cite it directly.

### F7 — CRITICAL (headline claim of the abstract) — VERIFIED — The "specificity to 3^K" (Prop 5.1) is false
- **Where:** p07.tex:91-94, :483-526.
- **Quote:** "No other prime-power family $q=\ell^K$ with $\ell\neq3$ satisfies all three properties simultaneously." (:499-500)
- **Counterexamples:** ℓ = 7, 11, 19, 43, 67, 163. For each, Q(√−ℓ) is an imaginary quadratic (CM) field with class number 1, ramified only at ℓ, with (ℓ) = 𝔩², and (Z/ℓ^K)^× is cyclic. Class numbers h(−d) = 1 were checked by counting reduced forms (`p07_checks.py` §7).
  - The proof only tests Q(√−3) against other ℓ (:523-525: "the relevant CM field is not $\Qomega$ and a separate argument would be required").
  - The same argument works word for word with Q(√−ℓ), and Thm 3.2 as stated does not even need h = 1.
- **Property (2) does not single out 3:** (Z/ℓ^K)^× is cyclic for every odd ℓ, as p04 Lemma 3.1 itself says (p04.tex:290).
- **The conclusion W3 does not single out 3 either:** For every odd ℓ, the only real character mod ℓ^K has conductor ℓ, so all primitive characters mod ℓ^K with K ≥ 2 are complex (F4).
- **The paper's own test fails for ℓ = 3:** The "matching" criterion (:516-518) compares |(O/(ℓ^K))^×| with |(Z/ℓ^K)^×|. For ℓ = 3 it also fails: |(O/𝔭^{2K})^×| = 2·3^{2K−1} ≠ 2·3^{K−1}.
- **Property (3) is false as well:** "characters of $(\Z/3^K\Z)^\times$ correspond precisely to Hecke characters of $\K$ of conductor $\pp^{2K}$" (:495-497).
  - The ray class group of Q(√−3) mod 𝔭^m has order 3^{max(m−2,0)} (`p07_checks.py` §4). So there are 3^{2K−2} ray-class characters mod 𝔭^{2K}, against 2·3^{K−1} Dirichlet characters.
  - χ ↦ χ∘N is 2-to-1, since χ and χε have the same image (ε∘N = 1).
- **Fix:** Delete Prop 5.1 and the abstract sentence.

### F8 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — "2 splits in K (since −3 ≡ 1 mod 8)" is false: −3 ≡ 5 (mod 8) and 2 is inert in Z[ω]
- **Where:** p07.tex:520-521.
- **Evidence:** x² + x + 1 has no root mod 2 (`p07_checks.py` §3), and (−3) mod 8 = 5. Z[ω]/(2) = F₄. This also contradicts the paper's own rule "ℓ ≡ 2 (mod 3) ⇒ inert" (:408) and p04 Prop 6.3 (p04.tex:449-460).
- **Fix:** Write "2 is inert". Property (1) still fails for 2 because 2 is unramified.

### F9 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — Norm-map surjectivity claim (:236-242) is false
- **Quote:** "the norm map $\Nf\colon(\OK/\pp^{2K})^\times\to(\Z/3^K\Z)^\times$ is surjective … every element is a norm … $n=\Nf(g^{\lceil j/2\rceil}\cdot u)$ for appropriate unit $u$"
- **Evidence:**
  - Units of Z[ω] have norm 1, so N(g^{⌈j/2⌉}u) = g^{2⌈j/2⌉}, an even power of g, never g^j for odd j.
  - a² − ab + b² mod 3 ∈ {0, 1}, so every unit norm is ≡ 1 (mod 3).
  - The computed image size is 3^{K−1}, i.e. index 2, for K = 1..4 (`p07_checks.py` §2). For example, 2 mod 3 is never a norm.
  - Squares in a cyclic group of even order form an index-2 subgroup, contrary to "every element of a cyclic group is a square times a unit-order element".
- **Consequence:** χ∘N cannot see ε, which is exactly why χ and χε have the same lift.
- **Fix:** The image is {n ≡ 1 (mod 3)}.

### F10 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — The K = 1 case contradicts itself in three places
- **Where:** p07.tex:421-426 versus :433-434 versus :452-456, and :213-214.
- **Quotes:**
  - (a) "$\chi=\varepsilon$, $\chi\varepsilon=\varepsilon^2=\chi^0$ (principal)" (:422-423);
  - (b) "The twist $\chi\varepsilon$ is a Dirichlet character of conductor $\mathrm{lcm}(3^K,3)=3^K$ for $K\ge1$" (:433-434);
  - (c) "$\chi\varepsilon$ is non-principal for all $K\geq1$ … $\chi\varepsilon\neq\chi^0$" (:454-456).
- **What is wrong:** (b) and (c) contradict (a). For K = 1, χε is principal with conductor 1.
  - Moreover L(s,Ψ_ε) = ζ_K(s) (:425-426) means Ψ_ε is the **trivial** character, of conductor (1). That contradicts "primitive Hecke character of conductor 𝔭^{2K}" (:213-214).
  - In fact, no non-trivial finite-order Hecke character of conductor 𝔭² exists, because the ray class group mod 𝔭² is trivial (`p07_checks.py` §4).
  - The pole remark (:452-456) is wrong for K = 1: ζ has a pole at 1. The argument survives only because ζ(β) ≠ 0 for β ∈ (0,1).
- **Fix:** Treat K = 1 separately; there χ_{−3} is a single fixed function (F4).

### F12 — MAJOR — VERIFIED — W3 does not touch the step where p04 actually invokes "Watt", and that step is false
- **Where:** p07.tex:556-560 (the "Part II" summary) and :106-108. These depend on p04.tex:304-339.
- **Quote (p07):** "This enables Kloosterman sum cancellation beyond the Weil bound, improving the ceiling to $\theta=5/8$." The underlying p04 claim is "$|S(a,b;3^K)|\ll(3^K)^{3/8+\varepsilon}$" (p04:329).
- **Evidence:**
  - Kloosterman sums to prime-power moduli are evaluated explicitly (Salié-type formulas) and reach size 2√q.
  - Computed values of |S(1,1;3^K)| are 17.78, 53.99, 162.00 for K = 4, 6, 8, against 2·3^{K/2} = 18, 54, 162 and (3^K)^{3/8} = 5.2, 11.8, 27.0 (`p07_checks.py` §5).
  - Summing (3^K)^{3/8} over 3^K ≤ Q gives ≍ Q^{3/8}, not Q^{5/8} as p04:332 says.
  - The condition x^{1/2}Q^{5/8} ≤ x^{1−ε} gives Q ≤ x^{4/5}, not the x^{5/8} that p04:338 states.
  - Siegel zeros do not enter Kloosterman-sum bounds at all, so W3 is logically disconnected from the 5/8 step.
  - "The unit group … is cyclic (a consequence of the prime-power structure enforced by ramification)" (:557-558) is false: cyclicity of (Z/ℓ^K)^× holds for every odd ℓ and has nothing to do with Z[ω].
  - "three independent levels" (:551) is also wrong: the filtration 𝔭^j has 2K steps. "Triply-well-factorable" (Maynard) is a property imposed on sieve weights of level D, not something "forced" by the divisors 3^j of 3^K.
- **Fix:** Retract §6.
