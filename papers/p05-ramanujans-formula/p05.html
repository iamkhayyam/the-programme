RAMANUJAN’S DIMENSIONAL FORCING: A UNIVERSAL FORMULA FOR SIEVE LEVELS

KHAYYAM WAKIL

Abstract. We study the formula \[ \thetak = \frac {2^k - k}{2^k} = 1 - \frac {k}{2^k}, \] proposed as the level of distribution of \(k\)-fold sieves with constitutional structure. The formula rests on a counting postulate: a system of depth \(k\) is one in which exactly \(k\) of the \(2^k\) binary conﬁgurations are eliminated, which gives conﬁguration density \((2^k-k)/2^k\). This is a postulate, not a consequence of the binary model: if a conﬁguration is invalid whenever some constraint fails, there are \(2^k-1\) invalid conﬁgurations, not \(k\). For \(k=3\) the formula gives \(5/8\). We record the Eisenstein-integer arithmetic attached to the moduli \(q = 3^K\) (the ramiﬁcation \((3) = (1-\omega )^2\) and the cyclicity of \((\Z /3^K\Z )^*\)) and explain why it does not yield a level of distribution \(5/8\): the summed estimate over the moduli \(3^K\) holds for every exponent up to \(1\) by a trivial argument, while the per-modulus statement would go beyond what the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis gives. The value \(5/8\) for cascade moduli is therefore a conjecture. Pascadi’s exponent \(5/8\) for general moduli with triply-well-factorable weights has a diﬀerent origin, and its agreement with \(\theta _3\) is a numerical coincidence. The correspondence between \(\thetak \) and analytic levels of distribution is conjectural for every \(k\), and for \(k = 4, 5\) no object to which a prediction would apply has been speciﬁed.

1. Introduction

1.1. The formula and its meaning. The Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem [1, 15] states that for every \(A > 0\) there is \(B = B(A)\) such that \[ \sum _{q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}} \max _{\gcd (a,q)=1} \left | \psi (x;q,a) - \frac {x}{\varphi (q)} \right | \ll \frac {x}{(\log x)^A}, \] where \(\psi (x;q,a) = \sum _{n \leq x,\ n \equiv a\,(q)} \Lambda (n)\). This is an average over moduli; in this sense primes have level of distribution \(\thetaBV = 1/2\). Levels beyond \(1/2\) are known in modiﬁed settings (well-factorable weights and a ﬁxed residue class, or smooth moduli): Bombieri, Friedlander and Iwaniec [2] obtained \(4/7\) in 1986; Zhang [17] obtained \(1/2 + 1/584\) for smooth moduli, improved to \(1/2 + 7/300\) by Polymath [12]; and for triply-well-factorable weights Maynard [9] obtained \(3/5\), Lichtman [6] \(66/107\), and Pascadi [11] \(5/8\), for general moduli.

This paper studies the formula \begin {equation} \label {eq:formula} \thetak = \frac {2^k - k}{2^k}, \end {equation} proposed as a universal formula for the level of distribution as a function of sieve depth \(k\). It gives \(\theta _1 = \theta _2 = 1/2\), \(\theta _3 = 5/8\) and \(\theta _4 = 3/4\). Formula (1) is not derived: it follows from a counting postulate (Postulate 3.4), and its relation to analytic levels of distribution is conjectural (Conjecture 6.4). The value \(\theta _3 = 5/8\) agrees with Pascadi’s exponent; we regard this as a numerical coincidence (Section 6.3).

1.2. About the title. The title is an homage to Ramanujan. The ﬁrst version of this subsection attributed to him a “principle” that certain numbers force exact asymptotic formulas through their structural properties alone; no such principle appears in his work, and no result of Ramanujan is used in this paper. (His partition congruences, such as \(p(5n+4) \equiv 0 \pmod {5}\) [14], are congruences rather than asymptotic formulas; the asymptotic formula for the partition function is due to Hardy and Ramanujan [5].)

The ﬁrst version also argued that the prime \(3\) forces \(\theta = 5/8\): twin primes require \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\) for \(p > 3\), and this was taken to give three constitutional constraints on a conﬁguration space with \(2^3 = 8\) elements. That argument is withdrawn. For \(p > 3\), twin primes satisfy the single condition \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\); the conditions modulo \(9\) and \(27\) are implied by it, so by the independence requirement of Deﬁnition 3.2(b) the depth is \(1\), not \(3\) (Example 3.3).

1.3. Logical status. We state the logical status of every signiﬁcant claim.

  • Postulate 3.4: a system of depth \(k\) has exactly \(k\) eliminated conﬁgurations among \(2^k\). This is not a theorem: in the binary model of Deﬁnition 3.1 the number of invalid conﬁgurations is \(2^k - 1\) (Proposition 3.5, proved). The conﬁguration density \((2^k - k)/2^k\) (Proposition 3.6) follows from the postulate.

  • Proposition 4.1: the numbers \(416/729\) and \(5/8\) diﬀer by about \(9\%\), and the ﬁrst is not a density of residue classes. The earlier claim that the large sieve “connects to” conﬁguration density is withdrawn.

  • Proposition 2.3: the proportion of nonzero entries in the ﬁrst \(3^K\) rows of Khayyam’s Triangle mod \(3\) is \(2^{K+1}/(3^K+1) \to 0\). It does not match \((2^K - K)/2^K\), and the triangle is not a combinatorial origin of formula (1).

  • Proposition 5.7 (trivial): the summed estimate over the moduli \(3^K\) (Theorem 5.7 of the ﬁrst version) holds for every exponent up to \(1\), so it carries no information about \(5/8\).

  • Conjecture 5.8: per-modulus equidistribution for \(3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon }\). This would go beyond the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis.

  • Conjecture 6.4: \(\thetak \) equals an analytic level of distribution for general \(k\).

  • The “geometric proof” of \(\theta _3 = 5/8\) (Remark 5.6); the twin-prime threshold conjecture (Remark 6.5); the empirical column of Table 2; the Sophie Germain non-instance (Remark 6.7).

1.4. Position in the programme. This paper is the ﬁfth in a nine-paper programme (p01–p09) on constitutional sieve theory [18, 19, 20, 22, 21, 23]. The counting formula is introduced in [18], where it is also a postulate. The level \(5/8\) for the moduli \(3^K\) is a conjecture of [22]. Condition W3 of [23] is true, with a one-line proof (Appendix A), and does not bear on any level of distribution. The comparison with information theory is in [21]. The present paper discusses the formula and the distinction between two densities.

2. Khayyam’s Triangle Modulo \(3\)

Deﬁnition 2.1 (Khayyam’s Triangle mod \(3\)). Khayyam’s Triangle modulo \(3\) is the inﬁnite triangular array with entry \(\binom {n}{k} \bmod 3\) in position \((n,k)\), \(0 \leq k \leq n\).

Lemma 2.2 (Lucas [7]). For a prime \(p\) and integers \(0 \leq k \leq n\), write \(n = \sum _i n_i p^i\) and \(k = \sum _i k_i p^i\) in base \(p\). Then \[ \binom {n}{k} \equiv \prod _i \binom {n_i}{k_i} \pmod {p}. \] In particular, \(\binom {n}{k} \not \equiv 0 \pmod {p}\) if and only if \(k_i \leq n_i\) for all \(i\).

Proposition 2.3 (Nonzero entries of Khayyam’s Triangle mod \(3\)). For \(K \geq 0\), the ﬁrst \(3^K\) rows (\(0 \leq n < 3^K\)) of Khayyam’s Triangle modulo \(3\) contain exactly \(6^K\) nonzero entries out of \(3^K(3^K+1)/2\), a proportion \[ \frac {2^{K+1}}{3^K + 1}. \] For \(K = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\) this is \(1, 4/5, 4/7, 16/41, 16/61\), and it tends to \(0\) as \(K \to \infty \).

Proof. Write \(n = \sum _{i<K} n_i 3^i\). By Lemma 2.2 with \(p=3\), the number of \(k\) with \(\binom {n}{k} \not \equiv 0 \pmod {3}\) is \(\prod _{i<K}(n_i+1)\). Summing over the \(3^K\) choices of digits gives \((1+2+3)^K = 6^K\). The ﬁrst \(3^K\) rows contain \(\sum _{n<3^K}(n+1) = 3^K(3^K+1)/2\) entries, and \(2 \cdot 6^K/(3^K(3^K+1)) = 2^{K+1}/(3^K+1)\). □

Remark 2.4. The ﬁrst version of Proposition 2.3 asserted that the Sierpiński structure partitions the conﬁgurations of a \(K\)-level cascade sieve into exactly \(2^K - K\) valid and \(K\) invalid ones, matching the count of Section 3; its proof restated that count by forward reference. The assertion is withdrawn: the proportion in Proposition 2.3 tends to \(0\), whereas \((2^K - K)/2^K\) tends to \(1\), and no correspondence between nonzero entries and conﬁgurations is established. The ﬁrst version also described the Sierpiński pattern and the Eisenstein ﬁltration of Section 5 as “two faces of the same structure”; no such connection is established.

3. The Counting Postulate

3.1. Conﬁguration space and constitutional constraints.

Deﬁnition 3.1 (Conﬁguration space). For a \(k\)-level sieve, the conﬁguration space is \(\mathcal {C}_k = \{0,1\}^k\), with \(|\mathcal {C}_k| = 2^k\). Each conﬁguration \(c = (c_1, \ldots , c_k)\) records whether the constitutional constraint is satisﬁed (\(c_i = 1\)) or fails (\(c_i = 0\)) at level \(i\).

Deﬁnition 3.2 (Constitutional constraint). A constraint at level \(i\) is constitutional if:

(a)
it is forced by the problem structure—not a free parameter; and
(b)
it is independent of the constraints at the other levels, in the sense that it is not implied by them. (For congruence conditions to pairwise coprime moduli the Chinese Remainder Theorem guarantees this; for nested moduli such as \(3, 9, 27\) it does not.)

Example 3.3 (Twin primes). For a twin prime pair \((p, p+2)\) with \(p > 3\): if \(p \equiv 0 \pmod {3}\) then \(3 \mid p\); if \(p \equiv 1 \pmod {3}\) then \(3 \mid (p+2)\). Only \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\) permits both members to avoid divisibility by \(3\). This constraint satisﬁes Deﬁnition 3.2(a). The ﬁrst version took “three nested levels” with moduli \(3, 9, 27\), giving \(k = 3\) and \(|\mathcal {C}_3| = 8\). The conditions at the moduli \(9\) and \(27\) are not independent of the condition modulo \(3\): \(p\) and \(p+2\) are both prime to \(9\) (or to \(27\)) exactly when they are both prime to \(3\), so the admissible residues modulo \(9\) and \(27\) are exactly the \(3\) and \(9\) lifts of \(2 \bmod 3\). These conditions fail Deﬁnition 3.2(b), and by that criterion twin primes have depth \(1\). The assignment \(k = 3\) to twin primes (and to the cascade moduli \(3^K\)) is a proposal of the programme [18, 22] that is currently unsupported.

3.2. The counting postulate.

Postulate 3.4 (Nested constraint count). A \(k\)-fold sieve with constitutional constraints at \(k\) nested levels is said to have depth \(k\). For such a sieve we postulate that exactly \(k\) conﬁguration classes in \(\mathcal {C}_k\) are eliminated, one for each level, so that \(2^k - k\) remain.

The ﬁrst version stated Postulate 3.4 as a theorem, with a proof by induction on \(k\). That proof is wrong: on passing from \(k-1\) to \(k\) levels, each of the \(k-1\) previously invalid conﬁgurations splits into two, so they contribute \(2(k-1)\) invalid conﬁgurations, not \(k-1\). The postulate is not a consequence of Deﬁnition 3.1, as the next proposition shows. It is the deﬁnition of what we mean by depth \(k\), and the formula \(\thetak \) below is stipulated, not derived.

Proposition 3.5 (The binary model). In \(\mathcal {C}_k = \{0,1\}^k\), call \(c\) valid if \(c_i = 1\) for all \(i\), and invalid otherwise (some constraint fails). Then exactly one conﬁguration is valid and \(2^k - 1\) are invalid. For each \(i\), exactly \(2^{k-1}\) conﬁgurations fail constraint \(i\), and exactly \(k\) conﬁgurations fail exactly one constraint.

Proof. The only valid conﬁguration is \((1, \ldots , 1)\), so the other \(2^k - 1\) are invalid. Fixing \(c_i = 0\) leaves \(2^{k-1}\) choices for the remaining coordinates. A conﬁguration that fails exactly one constraint is determined by which constraint fails. □

Thus in the binary model the number of invalid conﬁgurations is \(2^k - 1\) for every \(k\) (for example \(7\), not \(3\), when \(k = 3\)). The revised Lean ﬁle P05 proves this as invalid_count.

Proposition 3.6 (Conﬁguration density). For a sieve of depth \(k\) in the sense of Postulate 3.4, \[ \rhoc _k = \frac {2^k - k}{2^k}. \]

Proof. Immediate from Postulate 3.4: \(2^k - k\) of the \(2^k\) conﬁguration classes remain. □

4. Two Densities

Earlier attempts to compute \(\theta _3\) via ternary residue analysis obtained approximately \(0.571\) rather than \(5/8 = 0.625\). The ﬁrst version described this as a \(12\%\) discrepancy explained by a conﬂation of two densities. The diﬀerence is about \(9\%\) (\(0.625/0.5706 - 1 \approx 9.5\%\), or \(1 - 0.5706/0.625 \approx 8.7\%\)), and the “pattern density” is the smaller of the two numbers. We record what the two numbers are.

Proposition 4.1 (Two numbers).

(1)
The “pattern density” of the ﬁrst version is \[ \rhop _3 = \prod _{i=1}^{3}\!\bigl (1 - 3^{-i}\bigr ) = \frac {416}{729} \approx 0.5706. \] This equals \(|\mathrm {GL}_3(\mathbb {F}_3)|/3^9\), the probability that a random \(3 \times 3\) matrix over \(\mathbb {F}_3\) is invertible. It is not a proportion of residue classes modulo \(27\): the proportion of residues modulo \(27\) prime to \(3\) is \(18/27 = 2/3\), and the proportion admissible for twin primes (\(p\) and \(p+2\) both prime to \(3\)) is \(9/27 = 1/3\).
(2)
Under Postulate 3.4, \(\rhoc _3 = 5/8 = 0.625\). In the binary model of Proposition 3.5, in which a conﬁguration is valid when all its coordinates equal \(1\), the proportion of valid conﬁgurations is \(1/2^k\) (that is, \(1/8\) for \(k = 3\)), and \(2^k - 1\) conﬁgurations are invalid.

In particular \(\rhop _3 < \rhoc _3\).

Proof. (1) \((2/3)(8/9)(26/27) = 416/729\), and \(|\mathrm {GL}_3(\mathbb {F}_3)| = (3^3-1)(3^3-3)(3^3-3^2) = 26 \cdot 24 \cdot 18 = 27 \cdot 416\), so \(|\mathrm {GL}_3(\mathbb {F}_3)|/3^9 = 416/729\). There are \(\varphi (27) = 18\) residues modulo \(27\) prime to \(3\), and \(9\) of them are \(\equiv 2 \pmod {3}\). (2) is Postulate 3.4 and Proposition 3.5. □

Remark 4.2. The ﬁrst version asserted that the Montgomery–Vaughan large sieve [10] “connects to conﬁguration density, not pattern density, because Dirichlet characters are indexed by constraint-satisfaction classes”. This is withdrawn. The large sieve inequality sums over all primitive characters modulo \(q\) for every \(q \leq Q\); the characters modulo \(q\) are indexed by the dual group of \((\Z /q\Z )^*\), not by binary constraint classes, and there is no “compatible” subfamily. The ﬁrst version’s proof also deﬁned conﬁguration density by “all coordinates equal to \(1\)”, which is the binary model of Proposition 3.5 and gives \(1/2^k\), not \((2^k - k)/2^k\). No relation between either number and a level of distribution is established; the conjectured relation for \(\rhoc _k\) is Conjecture 6.4.

5. Eisenstein Integers and the Moduli \(3^K\)

The ﬁrst version of this section claimed a “geometric proof” that, for the cascade moduli \(q = 3^K\), the analytic level of distribution is exactly \(5/8\), via two structural facts about \(\Zomega \) each contributing half the improvement over the Weil bound. That claim is withdrawn. This section keeps the correct Eisenstein-integer arithmetic, states what is true about the summed estimate (Proposition 5.7: it holds for every exponent up to \(1\)), and records the per-modulus statement as a conjecture (Conjecture 5.8). The steps of the withdrawn argument are listed in Remark 5.6.

5.1. The Eisenstein integers and ramiﬁcation of 3. Let \(\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}\), so \(\omega ^2 + \omega + 1 = 0\). The Eisenstein integers are \(\Zomega = \{a + b\omega : a, b \in \Z \}\) with norm \(\N (a + b\omega ) = a^2 - ab + b^2\).

Lemma 5.1. \(\N (1 - \omega ) = 3\).

Proof. \(\N (1-\omega ) = 1 - (1)(-1) + (-1)^2 = 3\). □

Proposition 5.2 (Ramiﬁcation of \(3\)). In \(\Zomega \), the prime \(3\) ramiﬁes: \((3) = (1-\omega )^2 \cdot u\) for a unit \(u\).

Proof. Since \(1 + \omega + \omega ^2 = 0\), we have \(3 = -\omega ^2(1-\omega )^2\). □

A rational prime \(\ell > 3\) is inert in \(\Zomega \) (stays prime) if \(\ell \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\), and splits as \(\ell = \pi \bar {\pi }\) if \(\ell \equiv 1 \pmod {3}\). By Example 3.3, every twin prime \(p > 3\) satisﬁes \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\) (inert) while \(p+2 \equiv 1 \pmod {3}\) (splits). So twin pairs with \(p > 3\) are inert–split pairs; this restates \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\) and adds no further constraint.

5.2. The \((1-\omega )\)-adic ﬁltration.

Proposition 5.3 (The \((1-\omega )\)-adic ﬁltration). The ring \(\Zomega /(3^K)\) carries the ﬁltration \[ \Zomega \supset (1-\omega ) \supset (1-\omega )^2 \supset \cdots \supset (1-\omega )^{2K}, \] with \((3^K) = (1-\omega )^{2K}\) and each quotient \((1-\omega )^j/(1-\omega )^{j+1} \cong \Z /3\Z \).

Proof. From Proposition 5.2, \((3) = (1-\omega )^2\), so \((3^K) = (1-\omega )^{2K}\). Each successive quotient is \(\Zomega /(1-\omega ) \cong \Z /3\Z \). □

The ﬁltration has \(2K\) steps, each with quotient \(\mathbb {F}_3\); nothing in it singles out three levels. (The ﬁrst version called it a “three-level ﬁltration”.)

5.3. Cyclicity of \((\Z /3^K\Z )^*\) and Kloosterman sums.

Lemma 5.4 (Cyclic structure). \((\Z /3^K\Z )^* \cong \Z /(2 \cdot 3^{K-1}\Z )\) for all \(K \geq 1\).

Proof. \(\varphi (3^K) = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}\); the unit group of \(\Z /p^K\Z \) is cyclic for all odd prime powers \(p^K\). □

Since \((\Z /p^K\Z )^*\) is cyclic for every odd prime \(p\), cyclicity does not distinguish \(3\) among the odd primes. The ﬁrst version contrasted it with squarefree moduli, claiming that a product of unit groups “obstructs the uniform character cancellation that a single cyclic group enables”. That claim is unsupported and is withdrawn: Weil’s bound for Kloosterman sums holds for squarefree moduli as well, and the known levels beyond \(1/2\) (Section 6.3) concern general moduli.

For integers \(a, b\) and \(q \geq 1\), let \(S(a, b; q) = \sum _{x \in (\Z /q\Z )^*} e\bigl ((a x + b \bar {x})/q\bigr )\) be the Kloosterman sum, where \(\bar {x}\) is the inverse of \(x\) modulo \(q\) and \(e(t) = e^{2\pi i t}\).

Proposition 5.5 (The Kloosterman bound \(q^{3/8}\) fails). For every \(q \geq 1\), \[ \sum _{a \bmod q} |S(a, 1; q)|^2 = q\,\varphi (q). \] Consequently \(\max _{a} |S(a, 1; q)| \geq \varphi (q)^{1/2}\), which for \(q = 3^K\) equals \((2/3)^{1/2} q^{1/2}\). In particular no bound \(|S(a, b; 3^K)| \ll (3^K)^{3/8 + \varepsilon }\) uniform in \(a, b\) can hold.

Proof. Expanding the square and summing over \(a\) ﬁrst, \[ \sum _{a \bmod q} |S(a,1;q)|^2 = \sum _{x, y \in (\Z /q\Z )^*} e\Bigl (\frac {\bar {x} - \bar {y}}{q}\Bigr ) \sum _{a \bmod q} e\Bigl (\frac {a(x-y)}{q}\Bigr ) = q \sum _{x \in (\Z /q\Z )^*} 1 = q\,\varphi (q). \] The sum has \(q\) terms, so the largest \(|S(a,1;q)|^2\) is at least \(\varphi (q)\). □

Remark 5.6 (The withdrawn argument). The ﬁrst version derived \(\theta _3 = 5/8\) for cascade moduli from the following steps, all of which are withdrawn.

(i)
A “three-level ﬁltration” of \(\Zomega /(3^K)\). The ﬁltration has \(2K\) steps (Proposition 5.3).
(ii)
A corollary stating that the ﬁltration induces a canonical decomposition \(w(d) = \alpha (d_1)\beta (d_2)\gamma (d_3)\), \(d_i = 3^{j_i}\), of sieve weights on powers of \(3\), called triply well-factorable. The factorisation \(3^j = 3^{j_1}3^{j_2}3^{j_3}\) is not unique; triple well-factorability in the sense of Maynard, Lichtman and Pascadi is the condition that for every factorisation \(D = Q_1Q_2Q_3\) the weight is a Dirichlet convolution \(\alpha * \beta * \gamma \) with \(\alpha , \beta , \gamma \) supported on \([1,Q_1]\), \([1,Q_2]\), \([1,Q_3]\); and weights supported on powers of \(3\) do not detect primes.
(iii)
A bound \(E_{II} \ll x^{1/2}Q^{1/2} \cdot Q^{1/4+\varepsilon }\) for the Type II error, described as giving a “ceiling \(\theta \leq 2/3\)”. No derivation was given, and a range in which a method gives a power saving is a lower bound for the level, not a ceiling.
(iv)
The Kloosterman bound \(|S(a,b;3^K)| \ll (3^K)^{3/8+\varepsilon }\), which is false (Proposition 5.5). Its proof sketch invoked Watt’s theorem, which concerns the Riemann zeta function and not characters modulo \(q\) (Appendix A).
(v)
The claim that (iv) “improves \(2/3\) to \(5/8\)”. Since \(5/8 < 2/3\), this would lower the level; and even granting (iv), the stated bound \(x^{1/2}Q^{1/2} \cdot Q^{3/16+\varepsilon } = x^{1/2}Q^{11/16+\varepsilon }\) gives a power saving for \(Q \leq x^{8/11-\delta }\), not \(x^{5/8}\).

5.4. What is true about the moduli \(3^K\).

Proposition 5.7 (The summed estimate is trivial). For every \(A > 0\), \[ \sum _{\substack {K \geq 1 \\ 3^K \leq x}} \max _{\gcd (a,3)=1} \left | \psi (x;3^K,a) - \frac {x}{\varphi (3^K)} \right | \ll _A \frac {x}{(\log x)^A}. \] In particular the estimate holds with the range \(3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon }\) of Theorem 5.7 of the ﬁrst version replaced by \(3^K \leq x^{\theta }\) for any \(\theta \leq 1\).

Proof. Let \(L = (\log x)^{A+1}\). For \(3^K \leq L\) the Siegel–Walﬁsz theorem gives \(\max _a |\psi (x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi (3^K)| \ll _A x\exp (-c\sqrt {\log x})\), and there are \(O(\log \log x)\) such \(K\). For \(3^K > L\), the trivial bounds \(\psi (x;q,a) \leq (x/q + 1)\log x\) and \(x/\varphi (3^K) = 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)\) give \(|\psi (x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi (3^K)| \leq (x/3^K + 1)\log x + 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)\). Summing this geometric series over the \(O(\log x)\) values of \(K\) with \(L < 3^K \leq x\) gives \(\ll x\log x/L + (\log x)^2 \ll x/(\log x)^A\). □

Proposition 5.7 uses only the Siegel–Walﬁsz theorem and the trivial bound: no Eisenstein-integer arithmetic, Kloosterman sums or Condition W3. Theorem 5.7 of the ﬁrst version is therefore true, but its exponent \(5/8\) plays no role, and the assertions that \(5/8\) is “uniquely forced” and that the ﬁltration and cyclicity “force \(5/8\) and nothing else” are withdrawn. The sum involves only \(O(\log x)\) moduli, so it is not a level of distribution in the usual sense. The meaningful statement is per modulus.

Conjecture 5.8 (Cascade moduli; cf. [22]). For every \(\varepsilon > 0\) and \(A > 0\), uniformly for \(q = 3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon }\), \[ \max _{\gcd (a,3)=1} \left | \psi (x;q,a) - \frac {x}{\varphi (q)} \right | \ll _{\varepsilon ,A} \frac {x}{\varphi (q)(\log x)^A}. \]

For a single modulus the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis gives this only for \(q \leq x^{1/2-\varepsilon }\), so Conjecture 5.8 would go beyond GRH. The best known results for prime-power moduli are those of Barban–Linnik–Tshudakov (exponent \(3/8\)), Gallagher (\(2/5\)) and Huxley (\(5/12\)); Banks and Shparlinski obtain lower bounds of the correct order for \(\psi (x;q,a)\) for prime-power moduli up to \(x^{0.4736}\). Montgomery’s conjecture predicts the statement for every exponent below \(1\), so \(5/8\) would be a proved exponent rather than a sharp one.

6. The Formula \(\thetak \)

6.1. Statement.

Deﬁnition 6.1 (Ramanujan’s Dimensional Forcing formula). For a sieve of depth \(k\) in the sense of Postulate 3.4, set \[ \thetak := \rhoc _k = \frac {2^k - k}{2^k}. \]

The ﬁrst version stated this as a theorem which asserted, in addition, that \(\thetak \) equals the analytic level of distribution when sieve weights are \(k\)-fold factorable over cascade moduli, citing Bombieri–Vinogradov for \(k=1\) and Pascadi together with its own Theorem 5.7 for \(k=3\). Those assertions are withdrawn. The equality with an analytic level is unproved for every \(k\) and is the content of Conjecture 6.4; for \(k=3\) see Section 5, and for the comparison with known levels see Section 6.3.

6.2. The Fibonacci remark.

Proposition 6.2 (Fibonacci identity). \(\theta _3 = 5/8 = F_5/F_6\), where \(F_n\) is the \(n\)-th Fibonacci number.

Proof. \(F_5 = 5\), \(F_6 = 8\); \(F_5/F_6 = 5/8\). □

Remark 6.3. The value \(\theta _3 = 5/8 = 0.625\) lies just above \(1/\varphi \approx 0.618\), where \(\varphi = (1+\sqrt {5})/2\) is the golden ratio, with \(|\theta _3 - 1/\varphi | \approx 0.007\). Formula (1) does not involve Fibonacci numbers; the coincidence at \(k=3\) is noted without over-interpretation.

6.4. Consequences and conjectures.

Conjecture 6.4 (General forcing). For any \(k\)-fold constitutional sieve of depth \(k\) (Postulate 3.4), the analytic level of distribution equals the conﬁguration density \(\thetak = (2^k - k)/2^k\).

Conjecture 6.4 is not yet a precise statement: no system has been shown to have depth \(k \geq 2\) in the sense of Deﬁnition 3.2, and the sequence and family of moduli whose level is meant have not been speciﬁed. For \(k = 3\) the programme proposes the moduli \(3^K\) (Conjecture 5.8); for \(k \geq 4\) no candidate has been proposed.

Remark 6.5 (The twin-prime threshold conjecture is withdrawn). The ﬁrst version stated a conjecture that \(\theta _5 = 27/32\) holds analytically and that, combined with the Goldston–Pintz–Yıldırım theorem [4], this would imply inﬁnitely many twin primes. The implication is false, and the conjecture is withdrawn. No level of distribution is known to imply inﬁnitely many twin primes: a level \(\theta > 1/2\) gives bounded gaps between primes [4]; even level \(1 - \varepsilon \) (the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture) gives gaps at most \(12\) [8], and at most \(6\) under the generalised Elliott–Halberstam conjecture [13]; and the parity barrier prevents these sieve methods from reaching gap \(2\). Moreover, no object of depth \(5\) has been speciﬁed.

Proposition 6.6 (Limit of \(\thetak \)). \(\displaystyle \lim _{k \to \infty } \thetak = \lim _{k \to \infty } \left (1 - \frac {k}{2^k}\right ) = 1\).

Proof. \(k/2^k \to 0\) as \(k \to \infty \) by l’Hôpital’s rule. □

The ﬁrst version described Proposition 6.6 as consistency with the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture [3]. That conjecture concerns all moduli \(q \leq x^{1-\varepsilon }\), while \(\thetak < 1\) for every \(k\), so the limit carries no evidential weight.

Remark 6.7 (Sophie Germain pairs). The ﬁrst version listed Sophie Germain pairs \((p, 2p+1)\) as a “conﬁrmed non-instance” at \(k = 2\). Appendix A. Watt’s Theorem and Condition W3

The ﬁrst version of this appendix “veriﬁed” three conditions W1–W3 for an application of Watt’s theorem [16] to Dirichlet \(L\)-functions modulo \(3^K\). Watt’s theorem is a mean-value estimate in the \(t\)-aspect for \(\int |\zeta (1/2+it)|^4 |M(it)|^2\,dt\), where \(M\) is a Dirichlet polynomial; it involves no characters modulo \(q\) and has no conditions W1–W3. The “veriﬁcation” of W1 and W2 is withdrawn, as is the use of Watt’s theorem for Kloosterman sums (Remark 5.6(iv)).

Condition W3. No \(L(s,\chi )\) with \(\chi \) primitive modulo \(3^K\) has a real zero in \(\sigma > 1 - c/\log 3^K\). This statement is true, with a one-line proof: \((\Z /3^K\Z )^*\) is cyclic of even order, so the only real non-principal character modulo \(3^K\) is the one induced from \(\chi _{-3}\) modulo \(3\); an exceptional zero can only belong to a real character; so any such zero would be a zero of the single ﬁxed \(L\)-function \(L(s,\chi _{-3})\), which is positive on \((0,1]\). No CM theory is needed (the ﬁrst version’s appeal to a zero-free region for \(L\)-functions of CM ﬁelds is withdrawn), W3 makes no result unconditional, and nothing in it is speciﬁc to \(3\): \((\Z /\ell ^K\Z )^*\) is cyclic for every odd prime \(\ell \). W3 is not needed for Proposition 5.7, which uses only the Siegel–Walﬁsz theorem.
