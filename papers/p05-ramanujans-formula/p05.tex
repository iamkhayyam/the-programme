\begin{abstract}
We study the formula
\[
  \thetak = \frac{2^k - k}{2^k} = 1 - \frac{k}{2^k},
\]
proposed as the level of distribution of $k$-fold sieves with constitutional
structure. The formula rests on a counting postulate: a system of depth $k$ is
one in which exactly $k$ of the $2^k$ binary configurations are eliminated,
which gives configuration density $(2^k-k)/2^k$. This is a postulate, not a
consequence of the binary model: if a configuration is invalid whenever some
constraint fails, there are $2^k-1$ invalid configurations, not $k$. For $k=3$
the formula gives $5/8$. We record the Eisenstein-integer arithmetic attached to
the moduli $q = 3^K$ (the ramification $(3) = (1-\omega)^2$ and the cyclicity of
$(\Z/3^K\Z)^*$) and explain why it does not yield a level of distribution $5/8$:
the summed estimate over the moduli $3^K$ holds for every exponent up to $1$ by
a trivial argument, while the per-modulus statement would go beyond what the
Generalised Riemann Hypothesis gives. The value $5/8$ for cascade moduli is
therefore a conjecture. Pascadi's exponent $5/8$ for general moduli with
triply-well-factorable weights has a different origin, and its agreement with
$\theta_3$ is a numerical coincidence. The correspondence between $\thetak$ and
analytic levels of distribution is conjectural for every $k$, and for $k = 4, 5$
no object to which a prediction would apply has been specified.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The formula and its meaning}

The Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem~\cite{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965} states
that for every $A > 0$ there is $B = B(A)$ such that
\[
  \sum_{q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}} \max_{\gcd(a,q)=1}
  \left| \psi(x;q,a) - \frac{x}{\varphi(q)} \right| \ll \frac{x}{(\log x)^A},
\]
where $\psi(x;q,a) = \sum_{n \leq x,\ n \equiv a\,(q)} \Lambda(n)$. This is an
average over moduli; in this sense primes have level of distribution
$\thetaBV = 1/2$. Levels beyond $1/2$ are known in modified settings
(well-factorable weights and a fixed residue class, or smooth moduli):
Bombieri, Friedlander and Iwaniec~\cite{BFI1986} obtained $4/7$ in 1986;
Zhang~\cite{Zhang2014} obtained $1/2 + 1/584$ for smooth moduli, improved to
$1/2 + 7/300$ by Polymath~\cite{Polymath2014}; and for triply-well-factorable
weights Maynard~\cite{Maynard2020} obtained $3/5$, Lichtman~\cite{Lichtman2023}
$66/107$, and Pascadi~\cite{Pascadi2025} $5/8$, for general moduli.

This paper studies the formula
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:formula}
  \thetak = \frac{2^k - k}{2^k},
\end{equation}
proposed as a universal formula for the level of distribution as a function of
sieve depth $k$. It gives $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 1/2$, $\theta_3 = 5/8$ and
$\theta_4 = 3/4$. Formula~\eqref{eq:formula} is not derived: it follows from a
counting postulate (Postulate~\ref{post:count}), and its relation to analytic
levels of distribution is conjectural (Conjecture~\ref{conj:general}). The value
$\theta_3 = 5/8$ agrees with Pascadi's exponent; we regard this as a numerical
coincidence (Section~\ref{sec:known}).

\subsection{About the title}
\label{sec:title}

The title is an homage to Ramanujan. The first version of this subsection
attributed to him a ``principle'' that certain numbers force exact asymptotic
formulas through their structural properties alone; no such principle appears in
his work, and no result of Ramanujan is used in this paper. (His partition
congruences, such as $p(5n+4) \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$~\cite{Ramanujan1919}, are
congruences rather than asymptotic formulas; the asymptotic formula for the
partition function is due to Hardy and Ramanujan~\cite{HardyRamanujan1918}.)

The first version also argued that the prime $3$ forces $\theta = 5/8$: twin
primes require $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ for $p > 3$, and this was taken to give
three constitutional constraints on a configuration space with $2^3 = 8$
elements. That argument is withdrawn. For $p > 3$, twin primes satisfy the
single condition $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$; the conditions modulo $9$ and $27$ are
implied by it, so by the independence requirement of
Definition~\ref{def:constitutional}(b) the depth is $1$, not $3$
(Example~\ref{ex:twin}).

\subsection{Logical status}
\label{sec:logstatus}

We state the logical status of every significant claim.

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{[Postulate]}] Postulate~\ref{post:count}: a system of depth $k$
    has exactly $k$ eliminated configurations among $2^k$. This is not a
    theorem: in the binary model of Definition~\ref{def:config} the number of
    invalid configurations is $2^k - 1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:binary}, proved).
    The configuration density $(2^k - k)/2^k$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:density}) follows from the postulate.
  \item[\textbf{[Proved]}] Proposition~\ref{prop:two-densities}: the numbers
    $416/729$ and $5/8$ differ by about $9\%$, and the first is not a density of
    residue classes. The earlier claim that the large sieve ``connects to''
    configuration density is withdrawn.
  \item[\textbf{[Proved]}] Proposition~\ref{prop:khayyam-count}: the
    proportion of nonzero entries in the first $3^K$ rows of Khayyam's Triangle
    mod~$3$ is $2^{K+1}/(3^K+1) \to 0$. It does not match $(2^K - K)/2^K$, and the
    triangle is not a combinatorial origin of formula~\eqref{eq:formula}.
  \item[\textbf{[Proved]}] Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial} (trivial): the summed
    estimate over the moduli $3^K$ (Theorem~5.7 of the first version) holds for
    every exponent up to $1$, so it carries no information about $5/8$.
  \item[\textbf{[Conjectured]}] Conjecture~\ref{conj:cascade}: per-modulus
    equidistribution for $3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$. This would go beyond
    the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis.
  \item[\textbf{[Conjectured]}] Conjecture~\ref{conj:general}: $\thetak$ equals
    an analytic level of distribution for general $k$.
  \item[\textbf{[Withdrawn]}] The ``geometric proof'' of $\theta_3 = 5/8$
    (Remark~\ref{rem:withdrawn}); the twin-prime threshold conjecture
    (Remark~\ref{rem:twinprime}); the empirical column of
    Table~\ref{tab:validation}; the Sophie Germain non-instance
    (Remark~\ref{rem:sg}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Position in the programme}

This paper is the fifth in a nine-paper programme (p01--p09) on constitutional
sieve theory~\cite{WakilCF,WakilKhayyam,WakilSC,WakilEisenstein,WakilShannon,WakilW3}.
The counting formula is introduced in~\cite{WakilCF}, where it is also a
postulate. The level $5/8$ for the moduli $3^K$ is a conjecture
of~\cite{WakilEisenstein}. Condition~W3 of~\cite{WakilW3} is true, with a
one-line proof (Appendix~\ref{app:watt}), and does not bear on any level of
distribution. The comparison with information theory is
in~\cite{WakilShannon}. The present paper discusses the formula and the
distinction between two densities.

\section{Khayyam's Triangle Modulo~$3$}
\label{sec:khayyam}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{definition}[Khayyam's Triangle mod~$3$]
Khayyam's Triangle modulo~$3$ is the infinite triangular array with entry
$\binom{n}{k} \bmod 3$ in position $(n,k)$, $0 \leq k \leq n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Lucas~\cite{Lucas1878}]
\label{lem:lucas}
For a prime $p$ and integers $0 \leq k \leq n$, write $n = \sum_i n_i p^i$ and
$k = \sum_i k_i p^i$ in base $p$. Then
\[
  \binom{n}{k} \equiv \prod_i \binom{n_i}{k_i} \pmod{p}.
\]
In particular, $\binom{n}{k} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$ if and only if $k_i \leq
n_i$ for all $i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Nonzero entries of Khayyam's Triangle mod~$3$]
\label{prop:khayyam-count}
For $K \geq 0$, the first $3^K$ rows ($0 \leq n < 3^K$) of Khayyam's Triangle
modulo~$3$ contain exactly $6^K$ nonzero entries out of $3^K(3^K+1)/2$, a
proportion
\[
  \frac{2^{K+1}}{3^K + 1}.
\]
For $K = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ this is $1, 4/5, 4/7, 16/41, 16/61$, and it tends to
$0$ as $K \to \infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $n = \sum_{i<K} n_i 3^i$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:lucas} with $p=3$, the number
of $k$ with $\binom{n}{k} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}$ is $\prod_{i<K}(n_i+1)$.
Summing over the $3^K$ choices of digits gives $(1+2+3)^K = 6^K$. The first $3^K$
rows contain $\sum_{n<3^K}(n+1) = 3^K(3^K+1)/2$ entries, and
$2 \cdot 6^K/(3^K(3^K+1)) = 2^{K+1}/(3^K+1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:two-faces}
The first version of Proposition~\ref{prop:khayyam-count} asserted that the
Sierpi\'nski structure partitions the configurations of a $K$-level cascade sieve
into exactly $2^K - K$ valid and $K$ invalid ones, matching the count of
Section~\ref{sec:combinatorial}; its proof restated that count by forward
reference. The assertion is withdrawn: the proportion in
Proposition~\ref{prop:khayyam-count} tends to $0$, whereas $(2^K - K)/2^K$ tends
to $1$, and no correspondence between nonzero entries and configurations is
established. The first version also described the Sierpi\'nski pattern and the
Eisenstein filtration of Section~\ref{sec:geometric} as ``two faces of the same
structure''; no such connection is established.
\end{remark}

\section{The Counting Postulate}
\label{sec:combinatorial}

\subsection{Configuration space and constitutional constraints}

\begin{definition}[Configuration space]
\label{def:config}
For a $k$-level sieve, the configuration space is $\mathcal{C}_k = \{0,1\}^k$,
with $|\mathcal{C}_k| = 2^k$. Each configuration $c = (c_1, \ldots, c_k)$
records whether the constitutional constraint is satisfied ($c_i = 1$) or fails
($c_i = 0$) at level $i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Constitutional constraint]
\label{def:constitutional}
A constraint at level $i$ is \emph{constitutional} if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item it is forced by the problem structure---not a free parameter; and
  \item it is independent of the constraints at the other levels, in the sense
    that it is not implied by them. (For congruence conditions to pairwise
    coprime moduli the Chinese Remainder Theorem guarantees this; for nested
    moduli such as $3, 9, 27$ it does not.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Twin primes]
\label{ex:twin}
For a twin prime pair $(p, p+2)$ with $p > 3$: if $p \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$ then
$3 \mid p$; if $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ then $3 \mid (p+2)$. Only $p \equiv 2
\pmod{3}$ permits both members to avoid divisibility by~$3$. This constraint
satisfies Definition~\ref{def:constitutional}(a). The first version took
``three nested levels'' with moduli $3, 9, 27$, giving $k = 3$ and
$|\mathcal{C}_3| = 8$. The conditions at the moduli $9$ and $27$ are not
independent of the condition modulo~$3$: $p$ and $p+2$ are both prime to $9$
(or to $27$) exactly when they are both prime to~$3$, so the admissible residues
modulo $9$ and $27$ are exactly the $3$ and $9$ lifts of $2 \bmod 3$. These
conditions fail Definition~\ref{def:constitutional}(b), and by that criterion
twin primes have depth~$1$. The assignment $k = 3$ to twin primes (and to the
cascade moduli $3^K$) is a proposal of the
programme~\cite{WakilCF,WakilEisenstein} that is currently unsupported.
\end{example}

\subsection{The counting postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Nested constraint count]
\label{post:count}
A $k$-fold sieve with constitutional constraints at $k$ nested levels is said to
have \emph{depth} $k$. For such a sieve we postulate that exactly $k$
configuration classes in $\mathcal{C}_k$ are eliminated, one for each level, so
that $2^k - k$ remain.
\end{postulate}

The first version stated Postulate~\ref{post:count} as a theorem, with a proof by
induction on $k$. That proof is wrong: on passing from $k-1$ to $k$ levels, each
of the $k-1$ previously invalid configurations splits into two, so they
contribute $2(k-1)$ invalid configurations, not $k-1$. The postulate is not a
consequence of Definition~\ref{def:config}, as the next proposition shows. It is
the definition of what we mean by depth $k$, and the formula $\thetak$ below is
stipulated, not derived.

\begin{proposition}[The binary model]
\label{prop:binary}
In $\mathcal{C}_k = \{0,1\}^k$, call $c$ \emph{valid} if $c_i = 1$ for all $i$,
and \emph{invalid} otherwise (some constraint fails). Then exactly one
configuration is valid and $2^k - 1$ are invalid. For each $i$, exactly
$2^{k-1}$ configurations fail constraint~$i$, and exactly $k$ configurations fail
exactly one constraint.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The only valid configuration is $(1, \ldots, 1)$, so the other $2^k - 1$ are
invalid. Fixing $c_i = 0$ leaves $2^{k-1}$ choices for the remaining coordinates.
A configuration that fails exactly one constraint is determined by which
constraint fails.
\end{proof}

Thus in the binary model the number of invalid configurations is $2^k - 1$ for
every $k$ (for example $7$, not $3$, when $k = 3$). The revised Lean file P05
proves this as \texttt{invalid\_count}.

\begin{proposition}[Configuration density]
\label{prop:density}
For a sieve of depth $k$ in the sense of Postulate~\ref{post:count},
\[
  \rhoc_k = \frac{2^k - k}{2^k}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Postulate~\ref{post:count}: $2^k - k$ of the $2^k$ configuration
classes remain.
\end{proof}

together with the number of invalid configurations in the binary model.

\section{Two Densities}
\label{sec:densities}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Earlier attempts to compute $\theta_3$ via ternary residue analysis obtained
approximately $0.571$ rather than $5/8 = 0.625$. The first version described
this as a $12\%$ discrepancy explained by a conflation of two densities. The
difference is about $9\%$ ($0.625/0.5706 - 1 \approx 9.5\%$, or
$1 - 0.5706/0.625 \approx 8.7\%$), and the ``pattern density'' is the smaller of
the two numbers. We record what the two numbers are.

\begin{proposition}[Two numbers]
\label{prop:two-densities}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item The ``pattern density'' of the first version is
    \[
      \rhop_3 = \prod_{i=1}^{3}\!\bigl(1 - 3^{-i}\bigr) = \frac{416}{729}
      \approx 0.5706.
    \]
    This equals $|\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_3)|/3^9$, the probability that a
    random $3 \times 3$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}_3$ is invertible. It is not a
    proportion of residue classes modulo $27$: the proportion of residues
    modulo $27$ prime to $3$ is $18/27 = 2/3$, and the proportion admissible for
    twin primes ($p$ and $p+2$ both prime to $3$) is $9/27 = 1/3$.
  \item Under Postulate~\ref{post:count}, $\rhoc_3 = 5/8 = 0.625$. In the binary
    model of Proposition~\ref{prop:binary}, in which a configuration is valid
    when all its coordinates equal $1$, the proportion of valid configurations
    is $1/2^k$ (that is, $1/8$ for $k = 3$), and $2^k - 1$ configurations are
    invalid.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $\rhop_3 < \rhoc_3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) $(2/3)(8/9)(26/27) = 416/729$, and
$|\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_3)| = (3^3-1)(3^3-3)(3^3-3^2) = 26 \cdot 24 \cdot 18
= 27 \cdot 416$, so $|\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_3)|/3^9 = 416/729$. There are
$\varphi(27) = 18$ residues modulo $27$ prime to $3$, and $9$ of them are
$\equiv 2 \pmod{3}$. (2) is Postulate~\ref{post:count} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:binary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:largesieve}
The first version asserted that the Montgomery--Vaughan large
sieve~\cite{MV1973} ``connects to configuration density, not pattern density,
because Dirichlet characters are indexed by constraint-satisfaction classes''.
This is withdrawn. The large sieve inequality sums over all primitive characters
modulo $q$ for every $q \leq Q$; the characters modulo $q$ are indexed by the
dual group of $(\Z/q\Z)^*$, not by binary constraint classes, and there is no
``compatible'' subfamily. The first version's proof also defined configuration
density by ``all coordinates equal to $1$'', which is the binary model of
Proposition~\ref{prop:binary} and gives $1/2^k$, not $(2^k - k)/2^k$. No relation
between either number and a level of distribution is established; the
conjectured relation for $\rhoc_k$ is Conjecture~\ref{conj:general}.
\end{remark}

\section{Eisenstein Integers and the Moduli $3^K$}
\label{sec:geometric}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The first version of this section claimed a ``geometric proof'' that, for the
cascade moduli $q = 3^K$, the analytic level of distribution is exactly $5/8$,
via two structural facts about $\Zomega$ each contributing half the improvement
over the Weil bound. That claim is withdrawn. This section keeps the correct
Eisenstein-integer arithmetic, states what is true about the summed estimate
(Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial}: it holds for every exponent up to $1$), and
records the per-modulus statement as a conjecture
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:cascade}). The steps of the withdrawn argument are listed
in Remark~\ref{rem:withdrawn}.

\subsection{The Eisenstein integers and ramification of 3}

Let $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$, so $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$. The Eisenstein
integers are $\Zomega = \{a + b\omega : a, b \in \Z\}$ with norm
$\N(a + b\omega) = a^2 - ab + b^2$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:norm}
$\N(1 - \omega) = 3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\N(1-\omega) = 1 - (1)(-1) + (-1)^2 = 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Ramification of $3$]
\label{prop:ramification}
In $\Zomega$, the prime $3$ ramifies: $(3) = (1-\omega)^2 \cdot u$ for a unit $u$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$, we have $3 = -\omega^2(1-\omega)^2$.
\end{proof}

A rational prime $\ell > 3$ is \emph{inert} in $\Zomega$ (stays prime) if
$\ell \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, and \emph{splits} as $\ell = \pi\bar{\pi}$ if $\ell
\equiv 1 \pmod{3}$. By Example~\ref{ex:twin}, every twin prime $p > 3$ satisfies
$p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ (inert) while $p+2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ (splits). So twin
pairs with $p > 3$ are inert--split pairs; this restates $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$
and adds no further constraint.

\subsection{The $(1-\omega)$-adic filtration}

\begin{proposition}[The $(1-\omega)$-adic filtration]
\label{prop:filtration}
The ring $\Zomega/(3^K)$ carries the filtration
\[
  \Zomega \supset (1-\omega) \supset (1-\omega)^2 \supset \cdots
  \supset (1-\omega)^{2K},
\]
with $(3^K) = (1-\omega)^{2K}$ and each quotient
$(1-\omega)^j/(1-\omega)^{j+1} \cong \Z/3\Z$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From Proposition~\ref{prop:ramification}, $(3) = (1-\omega)^2$, so $(3^K) =
(1-\omega)^{2K}$. Each successive quotient is $\Zomega/(1-\omega) \cong \Z/3\Z$.
\end{proof}

The filtration has $2K$ steps, each with quotient $\mathbb{F}_3$; nothing in it
singles out three levels. (The first version called it a ``three-level
filtration''.)

\subsection{Cyclicity of $(\Z/3^K\Z)^*$ and Kloosterman sums}

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic structure]
\label{lem:cyclic}
$(\Z/3^K\Z)^* \cong \Z/(2 \cdot 3^{K-1}\Z)$ for all $K \geq 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\varphi(3^K) = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}$; the unit group of $\Z/p^K\Z$ is cyclic for
all odd prime powers $p^K$.
\end{proof}

Since $(\Z/p^K\Z)^*$ is cyclic for every odd prime $p$, cyclicity does not
distinguish $3$ among the odd primes. The first version contrasted it with
squarefree moduli, claiming that a product of unit groups ``obstructs the
uniform character cancellation that a single cyclic group enables''. That claim
is unsupported and is withdrawn: Weil's bound for Kloosterman sums holds for
squarefree moduli as well, and the known levels beyond $1/2$ (Section~\ref{sec:known})
concern general moduli.

For integers $a, b$ and $q \geq 1$, let
$S(a, b; q) = \sum_{x \in (\Z/q\Z)^*} e\bigl((a x + b \bar{x})/q\bigr)$ be the
Kloosterman sum, where $\bar{x}$ is the inverse of $x$ modulo $q$ and
$e(t) = e^{2\pi i t}$.

\begin{proposition}[The Kloosterman bound $q^{3/8}$ fails]
\label{prop:kloosterman}
For every $q \geq 1$,
\[
  \sum_{a \bmod q} |S(a, 1; q)|^2 = q\,\varphi(q).
\]
Consequently $\max_{a} |S(a, 1; q)| \geq \varphi(q)^{1/2}$, which for $q = 3^K$
equals $(2/3)^{1/2} q^{1/2}$. In particular no bound
$|S(a, b; 3^K)| \ll (3^K)^{3/8 + \varepsilon}$ uniform in $a, b$ can hold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the square and summing over $a$ first,
\[
  \sum_{a \bmod q} |S(a,1;q)|^2
  = \sum_{x, y \in (\Z/q\Z)^*} e\Bigl(\frac{\bar{x} - \bar{y}}{q}\Bigr)
    \sum_{a \bmod q} e\Bigl(\frac{a(x-y)}{q}\Bigr)
  = q \sum_{x \in (\Z/q\Z)^*} 1 = q\,\varphi(q).
\]
The sum has $q$ terms, so the largest $|S(a,1;q)|^2$ is at least
$\varphi(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The withdrawn argument]
\label{rem:withdrawn}
The first version derived $\theta_3 = 5/8$ for cascade moduli from the following
steps, all of which are withdrawn.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item A ``three-level filtration'' of $\Zomega/(3^K)$. The filtration has
    $2K$ steps (Proposition~\ref{prop:filtration}).
  \item A corollary stating that the filtration induces a canonical
    decomposition $w(d) = \alpha(d_1)\beta(d_2)\gamma(d_3)$, $d_i = 3^{j_i}$, of
    sieve weights on powers of $3$, called triply well-factorable. The
    factorisation $3^j = 3^{j_1}3^{j_2}3^{j_3}$ is not unique; triple
    well-factorability in the sense of Maynard, Lichtman and Pascadi is the
    condition that for every factorisation $D = Q_1Q_2Q_3$ the weight is a
    Dirichlet convolution $\alpha * \beta * \gamma$ with $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$
    supported on $[1,Q_1]$, $[1,Q_2]$, $[1,Q_3]$; and weights supported on powers
    of $3$ do not detect primes.
  \item A bound $E_{II} \ll x^{1/2}Q^{1/2} \cdot Q^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ for the
    Type~II error, described as giving a ``ceiling $\theta \leq 2/3$''. No
    derivation was given, and a range in which a method gives a power saving is
    a lower bound for the level, not a ceiling.
  \item The Kloosterman bound $|S(a,b;3^K)| \ll (3^K)^{3/8+\varepsilon}$, which
    is false (Proposition~\ref{prop:kloosterman}). Its proof sketch invoked
    Watt's theorem, which concerns the Riemann zeta function and not characters
    modulo $q$ (Appendix~\ref{app:watt}).
  \item The claim that (iv) ``improves $2/3$ to $5/8$''. Since $5/8 < 2/3$, this
    would lower the level; and even granting (iv), the stated bound
    $x^{1/2}Q^{1/2} \cdot Q^{3/16+\varepsilon} = x^{1/2}Q^{11/16+\varepsilon}$
    gives a power saving for $Q \leq x^{8/11-\delta}$, not $x^{5/8}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{What is true about the moduli $3^K$}

\begin{proposition}[The summed estimate is trivial]
\label{prop:trivial}
For every $A > 0$,
\[
  \sum_{\substack{K \geq 1 \\ 3^K \leq x}}
  \max_{\gcd(a,3)=1}
  \left| \psi(x;3^K,a) - \frac{x}{\varphi(3^K)} \right|
  \ll_A \frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.
\]
In particular the estimate holds with the range $3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$
of Theorem~5.7 of the first version replaced by $3^K \leq x^{\theta}$ for any
$\theta \leq 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $L = (\log x)^{A+1}$. For $3^K \leq L$ the Siegel--Walfisz theorem gives
$\max_a |\psi(x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi(3^K)| \ll_A x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x})$, and there
are $O(\log\log x)$ such $K$. For $3^K > L$, the trivial bounds
$\psi(x;q,a) \leq (x/q + 1)\log x$ and $x/\varphi(3^K) = 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)$ give
$|\psi(x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi(3^K)| \leq (x/3^K + 1)\log x + 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)$.
Summing this geometric series over the $O(\log x)$ values of $K$ with
$L < 3^K \leq x$ gives $\ll x\log x/L + (\log x)^2 \ll x/(\log x)^A$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial} uses only the Siegel--Walfisz theorem and the
trivial bound: no Eisenstein-integer arithmetic, Kloosterman sums or
Condition~W3. Theorem~5.7 of the first version is therefore true, but its
exponent $5/8$ plays no role, and the assertions that $5/8$ is ``uniquely
forced'' and that the filtration and cyclicity ``force $5/8$ and nothing else''
are withdrawn. The sum involves only $O(\log x)$ moduli, so it is not a level of
distribution in the usual sense. The meaningful statement is per modulus.

\begin{conjecture}[Cascade moduli; cf.~\cite{WakilEisenstein}]
\label{conj:cascade}
For every $\varepsilon > 0$ and $A > 0$, uniformly for $q = 3^K \leq
x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$,
\[
  \max_{\gcd(a,3)=1} \left| \psi(x;q,a) - \frac{x}{\varphi(q)} \right|
  \ll_{\varepsilon,A} \frac{x}{\varphi(q)(\log x)^A}.
\]
\end{conjecture}

For a single modulus the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis gives this only for
$q \leq x^{1/2-\varepsilon}$, so Conjecture~\ref{conj:cascade} would go beyond
GRH. The best known results for prime-power moduli are those of
Barban--Linnik--Tshudakov (exponent $3/8$), Gallagher ($2/5$) and Huxley
($5/12$); Banks and Shparlinski obtain lower bounds of the correct order for
$\psi(x;q,a)$ for prime-power moduli up to $x^{0.4736}$. Montgomery's conjecture predicts the statement for every exponent
below $1$, so $5/8$ would be a proved exponent rather than a sharp one.

\section{The Formula $\thetak$}
\label{sec:formula}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Statement}

\begin{definition}[Ramanujan's Dimensional Forcing formula]
\label{def:formula}
For a sieve of depth $k$ in the sense of Postulate~\ref{post:count}, set
\[
  \thetak := \rhoc_k = \frac{2^k - k}{2^k}.
\]
\end{definition}

The first version stated this as a theorem which asserted, in addition, that
$\thetak$ equals the analytic level of distribution when sieve weights are
$k$-fold factorable over cascade moduli, citing Bombieri--Vinogradov for $k=1$
and Pascadi together with its own Theorem~5.7 for $k=3$. Those assertions are
withdrawn. The equality with an analytic level is unproved for every $k$ and is
the content of Conjecture~\ref{conj:general}; for $k=3$ see
Section~\ref{sec:geometric}, and for the comparison with known levels see
Section~\ref{sec:known}.

\subsection{The Fibonacci remark}

\begin{proposition}[Fibonacci identity]
\label{prop:fibonacci}
$\theta_3 = 5/8 = F_5/F_6$, where $F_n$ is the $n$-th Fibonacci number.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F_5 = 5$, $F_6 = 8$; $F_5/F_6 = 5/8$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The value $\theta_3 = 5/8 = 0.625$ lies just above $1/\varphi \approx 0.618$,
where $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ is the golden ratio, with
$|\theta_3 - 1/\varphi| \approx 0.007$. Formula~\eqref{eq:formula} does not
involve Fibonacci numbers; the coincidence at $k=3$ is noted without
over-interpretation.
\end{remark}

weights~\cite{BFI1986}; $1/2 + 1/584$~\cite{Zhang2014} and
$1/2 + 7/300$~\cite{Polymath2014} for smooth moduli; and
$3/5$~\cite{Maynard2020}, $66/107$~\cite{Lichtman2023} and
$5/8$~\cite{Pascadi2025} for triply-well-factorable weights. Every value of
$\thetak$ has a power of $2$ as its denominator, so none of $4/7$, $3/5$,
$66/107$, $1/2 + 1/584 = 293/584$ and $1/2 + 7/300 = 157/300$ is of the form $(2^k-k)/2^k$; all of them lie
strictly between $\theta_2 = 1/2$ and $\theta_3 = 5/8$. The same weight class
gives $3/5$, $66/107$ and $5/8$ through different analytic inputs, so the
exponent is not determined by a count of constraints. Pascadi's $5/8$ is
obtained from large-sieve inequalities for exceptional Maass forms together with
the Kim--Sarnak bound; it concerns general moduli and not the moduli $3^K$, and
its agreement with $\theta_3$ is a numerical coincidence, not evidence for
formula~\eqref{eq:formula}.

\subsection{Consequences and conjectures}

\begin{conjecture}[General forcing]
\label{conj:general}
For any $k$-fold constitutional sieve of depth $k$
(Postulate~\ref{post:count}), the analytic level of distribution equals the
configuration density $\thetak = (2^k - k)/2^k$.
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:general} is not yet a precise statement: no system has been
shown to have depth $k \geq 2$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:constitutional},
and the sequence and family of moduli whose level is meant have not been
specified. For $k = 3$ the programme proposes the moduli $3^K$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:cascade}); for $k \geq 4$ no candidate has been proposed.

\begin{remark}[The twin-prime threshold conjecture is withdrawn]
\label{rem:twinprime}
The first version stated a conjecture that $\theta_5 = 27/32$ holds
analytically and that, combined with the Goldston--Pintz--Y\i{}ld\i{}r\i{}m
theorem~\cite{GPY2009}, this would imply infinitely many twin primes. The
implication is false, and the conjecture is withdrawn. No level of distribution
is known to imply infinitely many twin primes: a level $\theta > 1/2$ gives
bounded gaps between primes~\cite{GPY2009}; even level $1 - \varepsilon$ (the
Elliott--Halberstam conjecture) gives gaps at most $12$~\cite{Maynard2015}, and
at most $6$ under the generalised Elliott--Halberstam
conjecture~\cite{Polymath8b}; and the parity barrier prevents these sieve methods
from reaching gap $2$. Moreover, no object of depth $5$ has been specified.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Limit of $\thetak$]
\label{prop:eh}
$\displaystyle\lim_{k \to \infty} \thetak = \lim_{k \to \infty}
\left(1 - \frac{k}{2^k}\right) = 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$k/2^k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$ by l'H\^opital's rule.
\end{proof}

The first version described Proposition~\ref{prop:eh} as consistency with the
Elliott--Halberstam conjecture~\cite{EH1970}. That conjecture concerns all moduli
$q \leq x^{1-\varepsilon}$, while $\thetak < 1$ for every $k$, so the limit
carries no evidential weight.

\begin{remark}[Sophie Germain pairs]
\label{rem:sg}
The first version listed Sophie Germain pairs $(p, 2p+1)$ as a ``confirmed
non-instance'' at $k = 2$. For $p > 3$ they satisfy the single condition
$p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, the same as twin primes. Since $\theta_1 = \theta_2 =
1/2$, assigning them depth $1$ or depth $2$ predicts the same value, so the
example cannot discriminate between depths and is not a falsification test. A
level of distribution for Sophie Germain pairs is not a defined notion, and no
such result is known. The entry is withdrawn.
\end{remark}

\appendix
\section{Watt's Theorem and Condition W3}
\label{app:watt}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The first version of this appendix ``verified'' three conditions W1--W3 for an
application of Watt's theorem~\cite{Watt1995} to Dirichlet $L$-functions
modulo $3^K$. Watt's theorem is a mean-value estimate in the $t$-aspect for
$\int |\zeta(1/2+it)|^4 |M(it)|^2\,dt$, where $M$ is a Dirichlet polynomial; it
involves no characters modulo $q$ and has no conditions W1--W3. The
``verification'' of W1 and W2 is withdrawn, as is the use of Watt's theorem for
Kloosterman sums (Remark~\ref{rem:withdrawn}(iv)).

\textbf{Condition W3.} No $L(s,\chi)$ with $\chi$ primitive modulo $3^K$ has a
real zero in $\sigma > 1 - c/\log 3^K$. This statement is true, with a one-line
proof: $(\Z/3^K\Z)^*$ is cyclic of even order, so the only real non-principal
character modulo $3^K$ is the one induced from $\chi_{-3}$ modulo~$3$; an
exceptional zero can only belong to a real character; so any such zero would be
a zero of the single fixed $L$-function $L(s,\chi_{-3})$, which is positive on
$(0,1]$. No CM theory is needed (the first version's appeal to a zero-free region
for $L$-functions of CM fields is withdrawn), W3 makes no result unconditional,
and nothing in it is specific to $3$: $(\Z/\ell^K\Z)^*$ is cyclic for every odd
prime $\ell$. W3 is not needed for Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial}, which uses
only the Siegel--Walfisz theorem.
