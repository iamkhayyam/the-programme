\begin{abstract}
We discuss primes in arithmetic progressions to the moduli $q = 3^K$
(``cascade moduli'') and record the arithmetic of the Eisenstein integers
$\Zomega$ at the ramified prime $3$. The original version of this paper
claimed a level of distribution $\theta = 5/8$ for these moduli. Its main
theorem, a Bombieri--Vinogradov-type sum over the moduli
$3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$, is true, but it remains true with $5/8$
replaced by any fixed $\theta < 1$: the family contains only $O(\log x)$
moduli, and the trivial bound together with the Siegel--Walfisz theorem
already suffices. It therefore carries no information about $5/8$. The
meaningful statement, an asymptotic formula for $\psi(x;3^K,a)$ for each
single modulus $3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$, is stated here as a
conjecture. It would go beyond what the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis gives
(moduli up to $x^{1/2}$). For prime-power moduli the known ranges have
improved from $x^{3/8}$ (Barban--Linnik--Tshudakov) through $x^{2/5}$
(Gallagher) to $x^{5/12}$ (Huxley), with lower bounds of the correct order up
to $x^{0.4736}$ (Banks--Shparlinski). The argument offered for $5/8$ in the original version
is withdrawn, and Section~\ref{sec:withdrawn} records where each step fails.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Background}

The Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem~\cite{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965} states
that for every $A > 0$ there is $B = B(A)$ such that
\[
  \sum_{q \leq x^{1/2}/(\log x)^B} \max_{a\colon \gcd(a,q)=1}
  \left|\psi(x;q,a) - \frac{x}{\varphi(q)}\right| \ll \frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.
\]
It is a statement about the \emph{average} over the moduli
$q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}$, not about each modulus separately. The primes
are said to have \emph{level of distribution} $\theta$ if the same bound
holds with the sum extended over $q \leq x^{\theta-\varepsilon}$, for every
$\varepsilon > 0$; Bombieri--Vinogradov gives $\theta = 1/2$. The Generalised
Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) also gives level $1/2$ and does not push it beyond
$1/2$; the Elliott--Halberstam conjecture, a separate conjecture, asserts
every $\theta < 1$.

Levels above $1/2$ are known only for modified problems. Bombieri,
Friedlander and Iwaniec~\cite{BFI1986} obtained $4/7$ for a fixed residue
class with well-factorable weights. Zhang~\cite{Zhang2014} and
Polymath8a~\cite{Polymath8a} obtained levels slightly above $1/2$ (up to
$1/2 + 7/300 \approx 0.5233$ in~\cite{Polymath8a}) for a fixed residue class
and squarefree moduli whose prime factors are all at most $x^\delta$ (more
precisely, densely divisible moduli). Pascadi~\cite{Pascadi2025} proved that
smooth numbers, and primes weighted by triply-well-factorable weights, are
equidistributed in arithmetic progressions to general moduli up to
$x^{5/8-o(1)}$, using large-sieve inequalities for exceptional Maass forms
together with the Kim--Sarnak bound. None of these results concerns the
moduli $3^K$, which are not squarefree for $K \geq 2$.

The present paper concerns the sparse family of \emph{cascade moduli}
$q = 3^K$. For such a family a summed statement of Bombieri--Vinogradov type
is uninformative (Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Remark~\ref{rem:trivial}); the
meaningful question is how large $3^K$ may be while each single modulus
still satisfies the prime number theorem in progressions
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}).

\subsection{The summed estimate}

\begin{theorem}[Summed estimate for cascade moduli]
\label{thm:main}
For every fixed $\theta \in (0,1)$ and every $A > 0$,
\[
  \sum_{\substack{K \geq 1 \\ 3^K \leq x^{\theta}}}
  \max_{\substack{a \bmod 3^K \\ \gcd(a,3)=1}}
  \left|\,\psi(x;3^K,a) - \frac{x}{\varphi(3^K)}\,\right|
  \ll_A \frac{x}{(\log x)^A},
\]
where $\psi(x;q,a) = \sum_{\substack{n \leq x \\ n \equiv a \pmod{q}}} \Lambda(n)$.
\end{theorem}

The original version stated Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with
$\theta = 5/8 - \varepsilon$ and deduced from it a ``level of distribution
$\theta_W = 5/8$ over cascade moduli''. That deduction is withdrawn. The
proof is in Section~\ref{sec:mainproof}.

\begin{remark}[Why Theorem~\ref{thm:main} says nothing about $5/8$]
\label{rem:trivial}
The family $\{3^K \leq x^\theta\}$ has only about $\theta\log x/\log 3$
members, so the trivial bound handles every modulus larger than a power of
$\log x$ (Section~\ref{sec:mainproof}). The argument never uses
$\theta < 5/8$; in fact it works for every fixed $\theta > 0$, including
$\theta \geq 1$. A ``level'' defined as the supremum of the admissible
$\theta$ is therefore not finite for this family. Numerically, at
$x = 2\cdot 10^7$ the sum in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} taken over \emph{all}
$3^K \leq x$ (that is, up to $\theta \approx 0.98$) equals $10{,}410$, while
$x/(\log x)^2 \approx 70{,}767$. At the same time the normalised error
$\varphi(q)\max_a|\psi(x;q,a) - x/\varphi(q)|/x$ is $0.28$ at
$q = 3^9 \approx x^{0.59}$ and exceeds $1$ for every $q = 3^K$ with
$3^{12} \leq q \leq x$. The summed statement does not see where the
individual moduli stop being equidistributed. (The computation is
\texttt{audit/scripts/p04/thm11\_trivial.py} in the programme repository.)
\end{remark}

\subsection{The per-modulus question}

\begin{conjecture}[Per-modulus level $5/8$ for cascade moduli]
\label{conj:main}
For every $\varepsilon > 0$ and $A > 0$,
\[
  \max_{\substack{a \bmod 3^K \\ \gcd(a,3)=1}}
  \left|\,\psi(x;3^K,a) - \frac{x}{\varphi(3^K)}\,\right|
  \ll_{\varepsilon,A} \frac{x}{\varphi(3^K)(\log x)^A},
\]
uniformly for all $K \geq 1$ with $3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Status of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}]
\label{rem:status}
We record three points.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{It goes beyond GRH.} For a single modulus $q$, GRH gives
    $\psi(x;q,a) = x/\varphi(q) + O(x^{1/2}\log^2 x)$~\cite[Ch.~20]{Davenport2000},
    which has the form required in Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} only when
    $\varphi(q) \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-A-2}$. The conjecture asks for moduli
    up to $x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$, so it asserts more than GRH is known to give.
  \item \emph{Known results.} For a fixed odd prime $p$, the asymptotic
    formula for primes in progressions to the modulus $p^N$ is known
    uniformly for $p^N \leq x^{3/8-\varepsilon}$ (Barban, Linnik and
    Tshudakov~\cite{BLT1964}), for $p^N \leq x^{2/5-\varepsilon}$
    (Gallagher~\cite{Gallagher1972}) and for $p^N \leq x^{5/12-\varepsilon}$
    (Huxley). Banks and Shparlinski~\cite{BanksShparlinski2019} obtained
    lower bounds of the correct order of magnitude up to $x^{0.4736}$. See
    Baier and Pujahari~\cite{BaierPujahari2022} for this history. All of these
    exponents are below $1/2$.
  \item \emph{Nothing in this paper supports the exponent $5/8$.}
    Section~\ref{sec:heuristic} explains where the value came from, and
    Section~\ref{sec:withdrawn} lists the steps of the original argument and
    why they fail. The conjecture is stated at $5/8$ only because that is the
    value the programme proposed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Notation}

Throughout: $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$, so $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$. The
Eisenstein integers are $\Zomega = \{a + b\omega : a,b \in \Z\}$ with norm
$\N(a+b\omega) = a^2 - ab + b^2$, and $\pp = (1-\omega)$. We write $\Lambda$
for the von Mangoldt function, $\varphi$ for Euler's totient, $\tau$ for the
divisor function, and $\chi_{-3} = \bigl(\tfrac{-3}{\cdot}\bigr)$ for the
non-principal character modulo $3$. For a Dirichlet character
$\chi \pmod{q}$, $\chi^0$ denotes the principal character. Implicit
constants may depend on $A$, $\varepsilon$, and absolute numerical
constants.

\section{The Eisenstein integers at the prime $3$}
\label{sec:eisenstein}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Ramification of $3$ in $\Zomega$}

\begin{lemma}[Norm of $1-\omega$]
\label{lem:norm}
$\N(1-\omega) = 3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\N(a+b\omega) = a^2 - ab + b^2$ with $a=1$, $b=-1$: $\N(1-\omega) =
1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Ramification]
\label{prop:ramification}
In $\Zomega$, the rational prime $3$ ramifies: $3 = u\,(1-\omega)^2$ for a
unit $u \in \Zomega^\times$, so $(3) = \pp^2$ with $\pp = (1-\omega)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $1 + \omega + \omega^2 = 0$, we have $3 = -\omega^2(1-\omega)^2$, and
$-\omega^2$ is a unit in $\Zomega$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Basic invariants of $\Qomega$]
\label{prop:invariants}
The field $\Qomega = \Q(\sqrt{-3})$ is imaginary quadratic, hence a CM field.
Its ring of integers is $\Zomega$, its discriminant is $-3$, its class number
is $1$, and its unit group is $\mu_6 = \{\pm1, \pm\omega, \pm\omega^2\}$. The
prime $3$ is the only rational prime that ramifies in $\Qomega$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $-3 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, the ring of integers is
$\Z[(1+\sqrt{-3})/2] = \Z[1+\omega] = \Zomega$, of discriminant $-3$. The
Minkowski bound is $(2/\pi)\sqrt{3} \approx 1.10 < 2$, so every ideal class
contains an ideal of norm $1$, and the class number is $1$. A unit
$a + b\omega$ satisfies $a^2 - ab + b^2 = 1$, whose six integer solutions give
$\mu_6$. A prime ramifies if and only if it divides the discriminant.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The prime $3$ is also the conductor of the quadratic character $\chi_{-3}$
attached to $\Qomega/\Q$. None of this is special to $3$: for instance $7$ is
the only prime that ramifies in $\Q(\sqrt{-7})$, which also has class
number~$1$. The original remark that this ramification gives the moduli $3^K$
``a geometric structure that generic squarefree moduli do not'' is withdrawn;
no such structure was exhibited or used.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Splitting of primes $\ell \neq 3$]
\label{prop:inertsplit}
A rational prime $\ell \neq 3$ is inert in $\Zomega$ if and only if
$\ell \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$. It splits as $\ell = \pi\bar\pi$, with $(\pi) \neq
(\bar\pi)$ primes of norm $\ell$, if and only if $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$.
In particular $2$ is inert.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\Zomega \cong \Z[x]/(x^2+x+1)$, the prime $\ell$ splits if
$x^2 + x + 1$ has a root modulo $\ell$ and is inert otherwise (the
Dedekind--Kummer criterion; for $\ell \neq 3$ there is no repeated root, as
the discriminant of $x^2+x+1$ is $-3$). A root $r$ satisfies $r^3 = 1$ and
$r \neq 1$ (as $3 \not\equiv 0 \pmod \ell$), so a root exists if and only if
$\F_\ell^\times$ has an element of order $3$, that is, if and only if
$3 \mid \ell - 1$. See also~\cite{Cox1989}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The $(1-\omega)$-adic filtration}

\begin{proposition}[$(1-\omega)$-adic filtration]
\label{prop:filtration}
The ring $\Zomega/(3^K)$, which has $9^K$ elements, carries the filtration
\[
  \Zomega/(3^K) = \pp^0/\pp^{2K} \supset \pp/\pp^{2K} \supset \pp^2/\pp^{2K}
  \supset \cdots \supset \pp^{2K}/\pp^{2K} = 0
\]
with $2K$ steps, and each successive quotient $\pp^j/\pp^{j+1}$ is
isomorphic to $\Zomega/\pp \cong \F_3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ramification} gives $(3^K) = \pp^{2K}$. Since $\pp$ is
principal, multiplication by $(1-\omega)^j$ induces an isomorphism
$\Zomega/\pp \to \pp^j/\pp^{j+1}$, and $\Zomega/\pp \cong \F_3$ because
$\N(1-\omega) = 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No three-level structure]
\label{rem:nothree}
The original version asserted that this filtration has ``exactly three
independent levels'' at the scales $3, 3^2, 3^3$ (that is, $j = 2, 4, 6$).
That is false. All $2K$ steps are alike. The even steps $j = 2, 4, \dots, 2K$
correspond to the $K$ rational levels $3, 3^2, \dots, 3^K$ (the $3$-adic
filtration of $\Z/3^K\Z$), and nothing distinguishes $j = 2, 4, 6$: for
$K \leq 2$ there is no level $3^3$, and for $K > 3$ the levels
$3^4, \dots, 3^K$ are just like the first three. Moreover, the questions of
Section~\ref{sec:intro} concern $\Z/3^K\Z$, not the ring $\Zomega/(3^K)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Characters modulo $3^K$}
\label{sec:characters}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic structure]
\label{lem:cyclic}
$(\Z/3^K\Z)^* \cong \Z/(2 \cdot 3^{K-1}\Z)$ for all $K \geq 1$, and $2$ is a
generator.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\varphi(3^K) = 3^K - 3^{K-1} = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}$. The powers of $2$ modulo
$9$ are $2, 4, 8, 7, 5, 1$, so $2$ is a primitive root modulo $9$, and a
primitive root modulo $p^2$, for an odd prime $p$, is a primitive root modulo
every $p^K$~\cite[Ch.~4]{IrelandRosen1990}. For $K = 1$, $2$ generates
$(\Z/3\Z)^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Cyclicity is not special to $3$: $(\Z/q\Z)^*$ is cyclic exactly when
$q = 1, 2, 4, p^k$ or $2p^k$ with $p$ an odd
prime~\cite[Ch.~4]{IrelandRosen1990}, so in particular for every odd prime
power. Nor is it a source of extra cancellation in Kloosterman sums: for
$q = 3^K$ the Weil exponent $1/2$ is attained (Section~\ref{sec:withdrawn},
item~\ref{wd:kloosterman}). The original remark that the product structure of
$(\Z/q\Z)^*$ for squarefree $q$ ``obstructs'' a cancellation that a single
cyclic group enables is withdrawn.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Real characters modulo $3^K$]
\label{cor:real}
For each $K \geq 1$ there is exactly one real non-principal Dirichlet
character modulo $3^K$, namely the character induced by $\chi_{-3}$. In
particular, for $K \geq 2$ every primitive character modulo $3^K$ is complex.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The character group of a cyclic group of even order is cyclic of the same
order, so it has exactly one element of order $2$. The character induced by
$\chi_{-3}$ is real and non-principal, so it is that element. It has
conductor $3$, so it is not primitive modulo $3^K$ for $K \geq 2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Base change to $\Qomega$]
\label{prop:basechange}
Let $\chi$ be a Dirichlet character modulo $3^K$, $K \geq 1$, and put
$\Psi_\chi(\mathfrak a) = \chi(\N\mathfrak a)$ for nonzero ideals
$\mathfrak a$ of $\Zomega$ (so $\Psi_\chi(\mathfrak a) = 0$ when
$\pp \mid \mathfrak a$). That is, $\Psi_\chi = \chi\circ\N$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\Psi_\chi((n)) = \chi(n)^2$ for every $n \in \Z$;
  \item for $\Re s > 1$,
    \[
      L(s,\chi\circ\N) := \sum_{\mathfrak a} \Psi_\chi(\mathfrak a)\,
      (\N\mathfrak a)^{-s} = L(s,\chi)\,L(s,\chi\chi_{-3});
    \]
  \item if $\chi$ is primitive, $\Psi_\chi$ is the trivial character for
    $K = 1$, and its conductor is $\pp^{2K-1}$ for $K \geq 2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\N((n)) = n^2$. (ii) Both sides are Euler products, since $\Psi_\chi$
is completely multiplicative on ideals. At $\ell = 3$ both local factors are
$1$, because $\chi(3) = 0$. If $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, then $\ell$ splits
into two primes of norm $\ell$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:inertsplit}) and
$\chi_{-3}(\ell) = 1$, so both sides have local factor
$(1 - \chi(\ell)\ell^{-s})^{-2}$. If $\ell \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, then $\ell$ is
inert, $\N((\ell)) = \ell^2$ and $\chi_{-3}(\ell) = -1$, so both sides have
local factor
$(1 - \chi(\ell)^2\ell^{-2s})^{-1} = (1-\chi(\ell)\ell^{-s})^{-1}(1+\chi(\ell)\ell^{-s})^{-1}$.
(iii) is proved in~\cite{WakilW3}. It agrees with the conductor--discriminant
formula $3\cdot\N\mathfrak f(\Psi_\chi) = \mathfrak f(\chi)\,\mathfrak f(\chi\chi_{-3})$,
which gives $\N\mathfrak f(\Psi_\chi) = 3^{2K-1}$ for $K \geq 2$, since then
$\chi\chi_{-3}$ is again primitive modulo $3^K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No exceptional zeros for the moduli $3^K$]
\label{rem:W3}
For real $\sigma > 0$,
\[
  L(\sigma,\chi_{-3}) = \sum_{m \geq 0}\bigl((3m+1)^{-\sigma} - (3m+2)^{-\sigma}\bigr) > 0,
\]
since every term is positive; for instance $L(1,\chi_{-3}) = \pi/(3\sqrt3)$.
An exceptional (Siegel) zero of an $L$-function modulo $q$ can only belong
to a real non-principal character~\cite[Ch.~14]{Davenport2000}. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:real}, the only such character modulo $3^K$ has the same
$L$-function as $\chi_{-3}$, which has no zero in $(0,1)$. Hence no
character modulo $3^K$ has an exceptional zero. This is Condition~W3
of~\cite{WakilW3}, which is proved there by exactly this argument. It implies
that the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions to the moduli $3^K$
holds in the Siegel--Walfisz range with effectively computable
constants~\cite[Ch.~20]{Davenport2000}. It has no bearing on
Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}: for $q = x^\theta$ one has
$x^{1 - c/\log q} = e^{-c/\theta}x$, so a zero-free region of width
$c/\log q$ gives no saving for moduli of size a power of $x$.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}
\label{sec:mainproof}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Write $q = 3^K$, let $\gcd(a,3) = 1$, and put
$E(q,a) = \psi(x;q,a) - x/\varphi(q)$ and $y = (\log x)^{A+1}$.

\emph{Large moduli, $y < 3^K \leq x^\theta$.} Since $\Lambda(n) \leq \log x$
for $n \leq x$, at most $x/q + 1$ integers $n \leq x$ lie in a given class
modulo $q$, and $x/\varphi(q) = 3x/(2q)$, we have
\[
  |E(q,a)| \leq \Bigl(\frac{x}{q} + 1\Bigr)\log x + \frac{3x}{2q}
  = \frac{x}{q}\Bigl(\log x + \frac32\Bigr) + \log x .
\]
The geometric series gives $\sum_{3^K > y} 3^{-K} < \tfrac32\, y^{-1}$, and
there are fewer than $\log x/\log 3$ values of $K$ with $3^K \leq x^\theta$.
Hence these moduli contribute
\[
  \ll \frac{x\log x}{y} + (\log x)^2 \ll \frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.
\]

\emph{Small moduli, $3^K \leq y$.} There are $O_A(\log\log x)$ of them. By the
Siegel--Walfisz theorem~\cite[Ch.~22]{Davenport2000},
$\max_a |E(q,a)| \ll_A x\exp(-c_A\sqrt{\log x})$ uniformly for
$q \leq (\log x)^{A+1}$, so these moduli contribute
$\ll_A x\exp(-c_A\sqrt{\log x})\log\log x \ll_A x/(\log x)^A$.

Adding the two ranges proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

By Remark~\ref{rem:W3}, the Siegel--Walfisz step, and hence the implied
constant in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, is effective for these moduli. For
$\theta \geq 1$ the same argument applies, the only change being that each
modulus $3^K > x$ contributes at most $\log x + 3/2$; this is the observation
in Remark~\ref{rem:trivial}.

\section{Where the value $5/8$ came from (heuristic, not evidence)}
\label{sec:heuristic}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This section explains why the programme expected the exponent $5/8$. It
contains no argument for Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item The value is $\theta_3 = (2^3 - 3)/2^3 = 5/8$ in the programme's
    formula $\theta_k = (2^k - k)/2^k$. That formula is a postulate of the
    programme (a system ``of depth $k$'' is defined to be one in which
    exactly $k$ configuration classes are
    eliminated)~\cite{WakilCF,WakilRamanujan}; it is not a theorem. If
    ``invalid'' means ``some constraint fails'', then $2^k - 1$ of the $2^k$
    configurations are invalid, not $k$.
  \item The depth $k = 3$ was assigned to cascade moduli through three
    ``levels'' modulo $3$, $9$ and $27$. This assignment is proposed and
    currently unsupported. The $(1-\omega)$-adic filtration has $2K$ steps,
    not three (Remark~\ref{rem:nothree}). For twin primes $p > 3$, the
    conditions modulo $9$ and $27$ are implied by $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$: the
    classes of $p$ modulo $9$ (or $27$) allowed by $3 \nmid p(p+2)$ are
    exactly the lifts of $2 \bmod 3$. By the programme's own independence
    criterion (C2) of~\cite{WakilCF}, such coupled constraints give depth~$1$,
    for which the formula gives $\theta_1 = 1/2$.
  \item Pascadi's exponent $5/8$~\cite{Pascadi2025} concerns smooth numbers,
    and primes with triply-well-factorable weights, to general moduli. It is
    not about the moduli $3^K$, and it neither confirms nor bounds anything in
    this paper. Its numerical agreement with $\theta_3$ is a coincidence, not
    evidence.
\end{enumerate}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} lies above the GRH range $1/2$, above the best
known asymptotic range $5/12$ for prime-power moduli, and above the range
$0.4736$ known for lower bounds (Remark~\ref{rem:status}).

\section{The Hexagonal Lattice}
\label{sec:geometry}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This section records two elementary facts about the hexagonal lattice
$\Zomega$. Neither is used elsewhere in the paper.

\begin{proposition}[Legendre duality of Eisenstein norm]
\label{prop:legendre}
The Legendre transform of the Eisenstein norm $f(a,b) = a^2 - ab + b^2$ is
\[
  f^*(p,q) = \frac{p^2 + pq + q^2}{3},
\]
with dual coordinates $p = 2a-b$, $q = 2b-a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$f^*(p,q) = \sup_{a,b}[pa + qb - f(a,b)]$; since $f$ is a positive definite
quadratic form, the supremum is attained at the critical point. Setting
$\partial f/\partial a = p$
and $\partial f/\partial b = q$ gives $p = 2a - b$ and $q = 2b - a$. Inverting:
$a = (2p+q)/3$, $b = (p+2q)/3$. Substituting back:
$f^*(p,q) = (p^2 + pq + q^2)/3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The factor $1/3$ in $f^*$ is $1/\det H$, where
$H = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is
the Hessian of $f$. Here $\det H = 3$ is the absolute value of the
discriminant $-3$ of the form $a^2 - ab + b^2$, which is also the
discriminant of $\Qomega$; so the $3$ in the denominator is the same $3$
that ramifies (Proposition~\ref{prop:invariants}). The original remark
linking this factor to a ``cyclic group enhancement'' of Kloosterman sums is
withdrawn.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Twin primes and splitting type]
\label{cor:twinprime}
If $p > 3$ and $p + 2$ are both prime, then $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, so $p$ is
inert in $\Zomega$, and $p + 2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ splits as $\pi\bar\pi$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $p > 3$ is prime, $3 \nmid p$. If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, then
$3 \mid p + 2$ and $p + 2 > 3$, so $p + 2$ is not prime. Hence
$p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ and $p + 2 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$; now apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:inertsplit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The splitting type of a prime $\ell \neq 3$ depends only on $\ell \bmod 3$.
Every prime $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ is inert, whether or not $p + 2$ is prime,
so the splitting type does not distinguish twin primes among the primes
$p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$; Corollary~\ref{cor:twinprime} is a restatement of the
congruence $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. The original statements that twin primes are
``distinguished within $\Zomega$ by their splitting type'' and that
$\theta_W = 5/8$ is ``the level of distribution that this algebraic geometry
forces'' are withdrawn.
\end{remark}

\section{Withdrawn material}
\label{sec:withdrawn}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The original version proved Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for
$\theta = 5/8 - \varepsilon$ by an argument in two ``geometric parts'',
followed by a uniqueness theorem and an appendix verifying ``conditions of
Watt's theorem''. All of it is withdrawn. For readers of that version, we
record where each step fails. Numbers such as ``former Proposition~2.4''
refer to the original version.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),ref=(\arabic*)]
  \item \emph{Three-level filtration (former Proposition~2.4).} There is no
    three-level structure; see Remark~\ref{rem:nothree}.
  \item \emph{Triply-well-factorable weights (former Corollary~2.5).}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:main} contains no sieve weights. The proposed pointwise
    factorisation $w(3^j) = \alpha(3^{j_1})\beta(3^{j_2})\gamma(3^{j_3})$ is
    not well defined, since $j = j_1 + j_2 + j_3$ in many ways. Weights are
    triply well-factorable, in the sense used by Maynard and by
    Pascadi~\cite{Pascadi2025}, if for every factorisation $Q = Q_1Q_2Q_3$
    they can be written as a Dirichlet convolution $\alpha * \beta * \gamma$
    of $1$-bounded sequences supported on $[1,Q_1]$, $[1,Q_2]$, $[1,Q_3]$:
    this is a property of a weight sequence over a dense set of moduli, not
    of a single modulus. The known results beyond level $1/2$ with factorable
    weights~\cite{BFI1986,Pascadi2025} also concern a fixed residue class,
    whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has a maximum over $a$.
  \item \emph{Type~II bound and ``ceiling $2/3$'' (former Proposition~2.6
    and Corollary~2.7).} The bound $E_{II} \ll x^{1/2}Q^{3/4+\varepsilon}$
    was asserted without derivation: no bilinear form was opened, no
    completion step produced a Kloosterman sum, and sums over Dirichlet
    characters are not Kloosterman sums. Moreover, an error bound valid for
    all $Q \leq x^{2/3-\varepsilon}$ would be a lower bound for the level, not
    a ceiling $\theta \leq 2/3$.
  \item \label{wd:kloosterman} \emph{Kloosterman sums (former
    Proposition~3.3).} The bound $|S(a,b;3^K)| \ll (3^K)^{3/8+\varepsilon}$ is
    false. For even $K = 2m \geq 2$, writing each unit modulo $3^{2m}$ as
    $u(1 + 3^m t)$ and summing over $t$ modulo $3^m$ gives
    \[
      S(1,1;3^{K}) = 2\cdot 3^{K/2}\cos(4\pi/3^K),
    \]
    so $S(1,1;3^K) \geq 1.97\cdot 3^{K/2}$ for even $K \geq 4$, and no bound
    $O(q^{1/2-\delta})$ with $\delta > 0$ can hold. Exact computation also
    gives $\max_{3\nmid b}|S(1,b;3^K)| > 1.99\cdot 3^{K/2}$ for
    $3 \leq K \leq 11$. The correct general bound is
    $|S(a,b;q)| \leq \tau(q)\,(a,b,q)^{1/2}q^{1/2}$~\cite[Ch.~11]{IwKow2004}.
    The gcd factor cannot be dropped, as the original statement
    $|S(a,b;3^K)| \leq 2(3^K)^{1/2}$ did: $S(0,0;3^K) = \varphi(3^K) >
    2\cdot 3^{K/2}$ for $K \geq 3$. The summed statement
    $\sum_{3^K \leq Q}|S(a,b;3^K)| \ll Q^{5/8+\varepsilon}$ is true, but it is
    weaker than what the general bound gives for fixed $(a,b) \neq (0,0)$
    (namely $\ll_{a,b} Q^{1/2+\varepsilon}$), so it is no improvement.
    Watt's theorem, invoked for the pointwise bound, does not give it
    (item~\ref{wd:watt}).
  \item \emph{Exponent arithmetic.} Even granting the previous steps, the
    final inequality of the original proof,
    \[
      x^{1/2}Q^{5/8+\varepsilon} \leq x^{1-\varepsilon},
    \]
    gives $Q \leq x^{4/5 - O(\varepsilon)}$, not $Q \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$; the
    exponent of $Q$ was read off as if it were the level. A sharper estimate
    can only enlarge an admissible range, so the account ``ceiling $2/3$
    improved by $1/8$ to $5/8$'' is incoherent; note also that
    $2/3 - 1/8 = 13/24$.
  \item \emph{Vaughan's identity and Type~I sums (former Section~4).}
    Vaughan's identity~\cite{Vaughan1980} is exact; it has no $O(n^{-1/2})$
    term. With $U = V = x^{1/3}$ the Type~I sums run over $m \leq UV = x^{2/3}$,
    and the condition ``$U < x^{1-\theta}$'' used in the original version was
    not the relevant one.
  \item \emph{Subconvexity.} Phragm\'en--Lindel\"of interpolation gives the
    convexity bound, not a subconvexity bound. No subconvexity estimate was
    proved or used.
  \item \label{wd:watt} \emph{Watt's theorem and conditions W1--W3 (former
    Appendix~A).} Watt's theorem~\cite{Watt1995} is a mean-value estimate in
    the $t$-aspect for
    $\int_0^T |\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^4\,\bigl|\sum_{m\leq M} a_m m^{-it}\bigr|^2\,dt$.
    It involves no Dirichlet characters modulo $q$, has no conditions
    W1--W3, and gives no pointwise bound for a Kloosterman sum. The
    ``verifications'' were also invalid on their own terms: with
    $M = Q^{2/3}$ and $R = Q^{1/3}$, ``W1'' ($M \leq 3^K$) fails for every
    $3^K < Q^{2/3}$, and ``W2'' ($MR \leq 3^{2K}$) fails for every
    $3^K < Q^{1/2}$. ``W3'' conflated Siegel zeros with the bound
    $\theta \leq 7/32$, which is the Kim--Sarnak bound~\cite{KimSarnak2003}
    $\lambda_1 \geq 1/4 - (7/64)^2$ towards Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture,
    written in the normalisation $\theta = \sqrt{1 - 4\lambda_1}$; it is an
    unconditional theorem about Maass forms and is unrelated to zeros of
    Dirichlet $L$-functions. The Siegel-zero statement itself is true, for the
    reason given in Remark~\ref{rem:W3}. The base change used for it does
    not have ``matching'' conductor
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:basechange}(iii)), and neither the
    class-number-one theorem of Baker~\cite{Baker1966} and
    Stark~\cite{Stark1967} nor Stark's effective Brauer--Siegel
    results~\cite{Stark1974} gives a zero-free region for $L$-functions of
    $\Qomega$ of growing conductor.
  \item \emph{Uniqueness and sharpness (former Theorem~5.1).} Its parts~(1)
    and~(2) rested on items (1)--(4) above. Part~(3) is false as stated,
    since Theorem~\ref{thm:main} holds for every $\theta < 1$. Pascadi's
    theorem~\cite{Pascadi2025} is an equidistribution result for a different
    problem, not a lower bound matching an upper bound, and no known result
    rules out a level above $5/8$ for these moduli. GRH gives level $1/2$ for
    a single modulus, not more than $5/8$.
  \item \emph{Citations.} Polymath8 and Pascadi were misdescribed in the
    original introduction; the corrected descriptions are in
    Section~\ref{sec:intro}. The paper ``On the large sieve'' is by Bombieri
    alone~\cite{Bombieri1965}; Vinogradov's contribution
    is~\cite{Vinogradov1965}.
\end{enumerate}
