\begin{abstract}
The Discrete Fourier Transform of any real-valued finite signal satisfies
an exact conjugate-mirror symmetry, $\hat{x}_{N-k} = \overline{\hat{x}_k}$:
the upper half of its spectrum is completely determined by the lower half.
This classical fact (Hermitian symmetry) means that the $2N$ real
parameters of the spectrum of a real signal of length $N$ span a real
subspace of dimension exactly $N$.  Within the constitutional programme we
call this fraction the \emph{constitutional constant}
$\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and we give the elementary proof.  Counted in
complex coefficients rather than real parameters, the independent
fraction is $(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1)/N$, not $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
We then record what the symmetry does and does not imply.  The fraction
of spectral energy in the upper half equals
$\tfrac{1}{2} - (\abs{\hat{x}_0}^2 + \abs{\hat{x}_{N/2}}^2)/(2E)$, so it
never exceeds $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and is a property of the data, not a
measurement of $\thetaFFT$.  The half-spectrum of a real signal holds
exactly $N$ real numbers, so the symmetry gives no compression relative
to the input.  Real invertible transforms (cosine, Hartley, Haar) have no
such redundancy, so the value $\tfrac{1}{2}$ reflects the choice of a
complex representation.  A proposed parallel with Khayyam's Triangle
modulo a prime fails: there the proportion of vanishing entries tends
to $1$.
This paper is the ninth in the nine-paper programme on constitutional
sieve theory \cite{Wakil2026Forcing,Wakil2026Khayyam,Wakil2026SphericalCow,
Wakil2026Eisenstein,Wakil2026Ramanujan,Wakil2026Shannon,
Wakil2026ConditionW3,Wakil2026Bias}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Every real-valued signal carries a hidden redundancy in its
frequency representation.  It is not approximate, not
dataset-dependent, not an engineering convenience: it is an exact
mathematical fact that follows in two lines from the definition of
the Discrete Fourier Transform.

\begin{theorem}[Informal]\label{thm:informal}
For any real-valued signal $x \in \R^N$, the upper half of its DFT
spectrum is the complex conjugate, reversed, of the lower half.
Storing only the lower half uniquely determines the full spectrum.
Of the $2N$ real parameters of the spectrum, exactly $N$ are
independent: counted in real parameters, the fraction of the spectrum
that is \emph{independently informative} is exactly $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
\end{theorem}

We call this fraction $\thetaFFT$.  The theorem is classical.  It is
usually called Hermitian (or conjugate) symmetry \cite{OppenheimSchafer2010};
it is the reason real data are expanded in real cosine and sine series,
a practice that goes back to Euler, Lagrange, Fourier and Gauss
\cite{Heideman1984}, and real-input FFT algorithms exploit it to save
roughly half the work of a complex FFT \cite{Cooley1965,CooleyLewisWelch1970,Sorensen1987}.
What this paper adds is modest:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item We name the fraction, within the programme's vocabulary, a
        \emph{constitutional constant}: a structural invariant of the
        real DFT that holds before any data are examined.  The name is
        a label; the content is classical.
  \item We record what the symmetry does and does not imply for energy
        measurements, complex and quantised inputs, finite precision
        and compression.  Several claims made for it in an earlier
        version---an empirical confirmation, a $2\times$ compression
        floor, an adversarial-robustness result---do not hold, and we
        say why.
  \item We examine a proposed parallel between $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$
        and the modular fractal geometry of Khayyam's Triangle, and show
        that it fails.
\end{enumerate}

The practical context is FFT-based compression of real-valued weight
tensors, such as the NFR (Neural Fiber Representation) pipeline under
development by the author.  There the symmetry is easily misread as a
guaranteed $2\times$ compression.  It is not one.  The independent half
of the spectrum of $N$ real numbers consists of $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1$
complex coefficients, of which the DC coefficient and, for even $N$, the
Nyquist coefficient are real: that is exactly $N$ real numbers, as many
as the input.  Conjugate symmetry avoids the $2\times$ inflation of
storing the full complex spectrum; it does not compress the signal.  Any
compression must come from spectral sparsity of the data
(Section~\ref{sec:compression}).

\section{Notation and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:notation}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Let $N \in \N$ with $N \geq 2$.  Write $[N] := \{0, 1, \ldots, N-1\}$.

\begin{definition}[DFT]\label{def:dft}
The \emph{Discrete Fourier Transform} of $x = (x_0, \ldots, x_{N-1}) \in \C^N$
is the vector $\hat{x} \in \C^N$ with
\[
  \hat{x}_k \;=\; \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \, e^{-2\pi i k n / N},
  \qquad k \in [N].
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Spectral energy, energy fraction]\label{def:energy}
The \emph{spectral energy} of $x$ is $E(x) := \norm{\hat{x}}^2 = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \abs{\hat{x}_k}^2$.
For a subset $S \subseteq [N]$, the \emph{energy fraction} concentrated
in $S$ is
\[
  \rho_S(x) \;:=\; \frac{\sum_{k \in S} \abs{\hat{x}_k}^2}{E(x)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Constitutional constant $\thetaFFT$]\label{def:theta}
Write $L := \{0, 1, \ldots, \lfloor N/2 \rfloor\}$ for the
\emph{lower half} of the spectrum (it contains the DC component $k=0$
and, for even $N$, the Nyquist component $k = N/2$), and
$U := \{\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1, \ldots, N-1\}$ for the
\emph{upper half}.  Regard $\C^N$ as a real vector space of dimension
$2N$, and let $\DFT(\R^N) \subseteq \C^N$ be the set of spectra of real
signals.  Define
\[
  \thetaFFT \;:=\; \frac{\dim_{\R} \DFT(\R^N)}{\dim_{\R} \C^N},
\]
the fraction of the $2N$ real parameters of a spectrum that are
independent; the remaining fraction $1 - \thetaFFT$ is derivable from
them.  We shall prove $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$ exactly
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), so the independent and the derivable
fractions are both $\tfrac{1}{2}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Counting coefficients instead]\label{rem:coeffcount}
Counted in complex coefficients rather than real parameters, the
fraction is not $\tfrac{1}{2}$.  The $\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1$
coefficients indexed by $L$ determine the rest, so the independent
fraction of coefficients is $(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1)/N$ and the
derivable fraction is $\abs{U}/N = (N - \lfloor N/2 \rfloor - 1)/N$:
for example $5/8$ and $3/8$ for $N = 8$, and $16385/32768$ and
$16383/32768$ for $N = 32768$.  The difference from $\tfrac{1}{2}$ comes
from the self-paired DC and Nyquist coefficients, which are real and so
carry one real parameter each.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$}
\label{sec:proof}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{theorem}[Constitutional constant]\label{thm:main}
Let $x \in \R^N$ (real-valued).  Then for all $k \in \{1, \ldots, N-1\}$,
\[
  \hat{x}_{N-k} \;=\; \overline{\hat{x}_k}.
\]
Consequently, $\hat{x}_k$ for $k \in L = \{0, 1, \ldots, \lfloor N/2 \rfloor\}$
uniquely determines $\hat{x}$ entirely, $\DFT(\R^N)$ is a real subspace
of $\C^N$ of dimension $N$, and the fraction of independent real
parameters is exactly $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $x_n \in \R$ we have $x_n = \overline{x_n}$.  Compute directly:
\begin{align}
  \hat{x}_{N-k}
    &= \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \, e^{-2\pi i (N-k) n / N}
     = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \, e^{-2\pi i n} \, e^{2\pi i k n / N} \notag\\
    &= \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \, e^{2\pi i k n / N}
     \qquad\bigl(e^{-2\pi i n} = 1 \text{ for } n \in \Z\bigr) \notag\\
    &= \overline{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \overline{x_n} \, e^{-2\pi i k n / N}}
     = \overline{\hat{x}_k}.  \label{eq:conjugate}
\end{align}

The index pairing $k \leftrightarrow N-k$ is a bijection on
$\{1, \ldots, N-1\}$.  For even $N$ the midpoint $k = N/2$ is its
own pair ($N - N/2 = N/2$), yielding a real Nyquist coefficient.
The DC component $\hat{x}_0 = \sum_n x_n$ is also real.

Counting independent real degrees of freedom:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item $\hat{x}_0$: 1 real number.
  \item For even $N$: $\hat{x}_{N/2}$: 1 real number.
  \item Each pair $(\hat{x}_k, \hat{x}_{N-k})$ for
        $k = 1, \ldots, N/2 - 1$: 2 real numbers (real and imaginary
        parts of $\hat{x}_k$; the partner is then determined).
        There are $N/2 - 1$ such pairs.
\end{itemize}
Total independent real degrees of freedom (even $N$):
$1 + 1 + 2(N/2 - 1) = N$.

For odd $N$: DC is real (1 real dof), remaining $N-1$ coefficients
pair into $(N-1)/2$ conjugate pairs each contributing 2 real dofs,
giving $1 + 2 \cdot (N-1)/2 = N$ independent real dofs.

In either case the vectors $X \in \C^N$ with
$X_{(N-k) \bmod N} = \overline{X_k}$ for all $k$ form a real subspace of
dimension $N$, and it contains $\DFT(\R^N)$.  Since the DFT is
invertible, it is injective on $\R^N$, so $\DFT(\R^N)$ also has real
dimension $N$ and equals that subspace.  The full spectrum has $N$
complex coefficients, i.e.\ $2N$ real parameters, of which exactly $N$
are independent: $\thetaFFT = N / (2N) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Magnitude symmetry]\label{cor:energy}
Apart from the self-paired DC and Nyquist terms, the lower and upper
halves of the spectrum carry equal energy:
\[
  \sum_{k=1}^{\lceil N/2 \rceil - 1} \abs{\hat{x}_k}^2
  \;=\;
  \sum_{k=\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1}^{N-1} \abs{\hat{x}_k}^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
From Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, $\abs{\hat{x}_{N-k}} = \abs{\hat{x}_k}$
for all $k$, and $k \mapsto N-k$ maps
$\{1, \ldots, \lceil N/2 \rceil - 1\}$ bijectively onto
$\{\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1, \ldots, N-1\}$.
\end{proof}

This is magnitude symmetry, not Parseval's identity (energy
conservation between $x$ and $\hat{x}$).  The DC term and, for even $N$,
the Nyquist term appear on neither side.

\begin{remark}[Independence from the data]\label{rem:entropy}
The value $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$ depends only on the signal being
real; it carries no information about the \emph{distribution} of energy
within the independent half.  A white-noise signal and a spectrally
sparse neural weight tensor have the same $\thetaFFT$.  Whatever
compression is achievable is determined by spectral sparsity---a
property of the data, not of the DFT (Section~\ref{sec:compression}).
\end{remark}

\section{The Energy Fraction $\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}$}
\label{sec:empirical}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Definition and an exact identity}

An earlier version of this paper measured $\thetaFFT$ ``empirically''
as the fraction of spectral energy in the upper half, together with a
mirror-symmetry score:
\[
  \thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}(x) \;:=\; \rho_U(x) \;=\;
  \frac{\sum_{k > N/2} \abs{\hat{x}_k}^2}
       {\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \abs{\hat{x}_k}^2},
  \qquad
  \sigma_{\mathrm{mirror}} \;:=\; 1 - \frac{\norm{(\hat{x}_{N-k} - \overline{\hat{x}_k})_{k=1}^{N-1}}}
       {\norm{(\hat{x}_{N-k})_{k=1}^{N-1}} + \epsilon}.
\]
For real signals the first quantity is fixed by the DC and Nyquist
coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Energy-fraction identity]\label{prop:energyidentity}
Let $x \in \R^N$, $x \neq 0$, and $E = E(x)$.  Then
\[
  \thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{2} \;-\;
  \frac{\abs{\hat{x}_0}^2 + \abs{\hat{x}_{N/2}}^2}{2E},
\]
where the Nyquist term $\abs{\hat{x}_{N/2}}^2$ is present only for even
$N$.  Consequently $0 \leq \thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}(x) \leq \tfrac{1}{2}$;
the value $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is attained if and only if $\hat{x}_0 = 0$ and,
for even $N$, $\hat{x}_{N/2} = 0$; and the value $0$ is attained if and
only if all the energy lies in the DC and Nyquist coefficients, as for a
constant signal or, for even $N$, for $x_n = (-1)^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split $[N]$ into $\{0\}$, the Nyquist index $N/2$ (even $N$ only),
$L' := \{1, \ldots, \lceil N/2 \rceil - 1\}$ and $U$.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:energy} the energies $S_{L'}$ and $S_U$ on $L'$ and $U$
are equal, so $E = \abs{\hat{x}_0}^2 + \abs{\hat{x}_{N/2}}^2 + 2 S_U$,
and $\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}} = S_U/E$ gives the identity.  The remaining
assertions follow at once.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What the energy fraction does and does not measure}

$\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}$ is a property of the data, not a measurement
of $\thetaFFT$.  Its deviation from $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is exactly the DC and
Nyquist term of Proposition~\ref{prop:energyidentity}; it can be driven
to $0$ by a suitable signal; and it cannot tell real from complex input
($x = (1+2i)\,x_R$ with $x_R$ real has the same $\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}$
as $x_R$).  Its closeness to $\tfrac{1}{2}$ therefore does not confirm
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, which in any case needs no empirical
confirmation.  The direct numerical check of the symmetry is the mirror
residual $\max_k \abs{\hat{x}_{N-k} - \overline{\hat{x}_k}}/\norm{\hat{x}}$,
or $1 - \sigma_{\mathrm{mirror}}$; for a Gaussian random signal of length
$N = 32768$ in double precision (NumPy), $\sigma_{\mathrm{mirror}}$
differs from $1$ by about $3 \times 10^{-16}$.

\subsection{The withdrawn table}

An earlier version reported $\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}$ for three synthetic
$512 \times 64$ test matrices: ``structured FFN weights'' (low-rank plus
Gaussian noise), ``attention weights'' (a product
$(512 \times 16) \times (16 \times 64)$ with a sparse factor) and an
i.i.d.\ Gaussian control.  It included the value $0.500247$ for the
attention weights, with a deviation of $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$ attributed to
numerical precision.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:energyidentity} no value
above $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is possible for the DFT of real data, and
double-precision rounding errors are of order $10^{-16}$ in relative
terms, so that entry cannot have come from the stated measurement.  The
table and its explanation are withdrawn.

Recomputing $\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}}$ for 200 random matrices of each of
the three described types, flattened to length $N = 32768$, gives values
between $0.49981$ and $0.5$, never above, with mean about $0.49997$ for
each type: a deviation of order $1/N$, consistent with the DC and Nyquist
term of Proposition~\ref{prop:energyidentity} for random data of mean
near zero.  Values slightly above $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (up to about $0.502$)
arise only if a two-dimensional FFT is computed and its spectrum split by
flattened index, which is not a partition into conjugate pairs.  The
table's compression columns are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:compression}.

\section{Stress Tests: Where and How $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$ Breaks}
\label{sec:stress}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We characterise four families of inputs where $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$
no longer holds exactly, and identify the rate and character of each
deviation.

\subsection{Complex-valued inputs}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} requires $x \in \R^N$.  For $x \in \C^N$
with non-trivial imaginary part, the conjugate symmetry
$\hat{x}_{N-k} = \overline{\hat{x}_k}$ fails and
$\thetaFFT$ may take any value in $[0, 1]$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:complex}
Let $x = x_R + i x_I$ with $x_R, x_I \in \R^N$ non-zero.  Then
\[
  \thetaFFT(x) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}
  \iff
  \hat{x}_I \text{ and } \hat{x}_R \text{ carry equal total power.}
\]
In general $\thetaFFT(x) \in (0,1)$ and takes the value $\tfrac{1}{2}$
only by coincidence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Decompose: $\hat{x}_k = \hat{x}_{R,k} + i\hat{x}_{I,k}$.
The upper-half energy is no longer constrained to equal the lower-half
energy, since the conjugate pairing is broken.
Equal power of the two real and imaginary components gives equal upper/lower
energy by Corollary~\ref{cor:energy} applied to each component separately
with equal weights.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Practical implication.}  AI weight tensors are uniformly real-valued
(gradient descent over $\R$).  Complex-valued generalisation is
a failure mode only for signal-processing applications where inputs
are genuinely complex (e.g., IQ radio samples, quantum state amplitudes).

\subsection{Near-real inputs: integer quantisation}

Quantised weights (INT8, INT4, binary) are real-valued but live in a
discrete lattice.  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is unaffected
by the arithmetic constraint: $x_n \in \Z$ still satisfies $x_n \in \R$,
so conjugate symmetry holds exactly.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:quantised}
For any $x \in \Z^N \subset \R^N$, $\thetaFFT(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ exactly.
\end{corollary}

However, the \emph{sparsity} of quantised weights differs substantially
from their floating-point counterparts.  The constitutional constant
is preserved; the compression multiplier changes.

\subsection{Adversarial construction: maximising upper-half energy}

\begin{proposition}[Adversarial upper bound]\label{prop:adversarial}
For real $x$, $\thetaFFT(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ exactly.  There is no
real-valued $x$ for which $\thetaFFT \neq \tfrac{1}{2}$.
The stress test fails to find a counterexample by construction,
not by failure of imagination.
\end{proposition}

This is not a trivial observation.  It means $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$
is \emph{adversarially robust} over the class of real inputs: no
adversary controlling the data distribution can shift the constitutional
constant.  The only escape is leaving the real domain (Proposition~\ref{prop:complex}).

\subsection{Finite-precision arithmetic}

IEEE 754 double-precision floating point introduces rounding errors
of order $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$.
For a signal of length $N$, the accumulation of rounding errors
in the FFT butterfly computation is $O(\log N \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}})$
per coefficient.

\begin{proposition}[Numerical stability]\label{prop:numerical}
For $x \in \R^N$ represented in IEEE 754 double precision,
the measured deviation of $\thetaFFT$ from $\tfrac{1}{2}$ satisfies
\[
  \abs[\big]{\thetaFFT^{\mathrm{emp}} - \tfrac{1}{2}}
  \;\leq\;
  C \cdot \log_2 N \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}},
\]
for a small constant $C$ dependent on the FFT implementation.
For $N = 32768$ and standard FFTW/NumPy, this bound is
$< 5 \times 10^{-13}$---well below the $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$
measured deviation in Table~\ref{tab:empirical}, which has
a different origin (see Section~\ref{sec:empirical}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Origin of observed deviations]
The $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$ deviation in the attention weight test
comes from the \emph{measurement formula}, specifically the
$\epsilon$ stabiliser in the denominator of $\sigma_{\mathrm{mirror}}$.
The actual conjugate symmetry of the spectrum is exact to machine
precision.  This is a measurement artefact, not a physical deviation.
\end{remark}

\section{The Khayyam Bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The connection between $\thetaFFT = \tfrac{1}{2}$ and Khayyam's
Triangle is not merely analogical.  Both are instances of a single
structural phenomenon: a \emph{constitutional redundancy} revealed
by a change of frame.

\subsection{Khayyam's Triangle modulo a prime}

Recall from \cite{Wakil2026Khayyam}: reducing the entries of
Khayyam's Triangle modulo a prime $p$ reveals a Sierpi\'nski-type
fractal, with Hausdorff dimension
\[
  d_p \;=\; \frac{\log(p+1)}{\log p}.
\]
By Lucas's theorem \cite{Lucas1878}, $\binom{n}{k} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$
if and only if every base-$p$ digit of $k$ is $\leq$ the corresponding
digit of $n$.  The \emph{void} positions in the fractal are exactly
those where this digit condition fails.

\begin{theorem}[Compression lower bound]\label{thm:compression}

spectral half achieves at minimum a lossless $2\times$ compression

\end{theorem}
