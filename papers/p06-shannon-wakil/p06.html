THE SHANNON–WAKIL EFFECT: CONSTITUTIONAL FORCING AS A UNIVERSAL PATTERN IN INFORMATION THEORY AND PRIME ARITHMETIC

KHAYYAM WAKIL

Abstract. We formalize a structural pattern that we call the Shannon–Wakil eﬀect: an exponential conﬁguration space on which a natural measure concentrates onto a strict subspace whose size, on the exponential scale, is a ﬁxed fraction \(\theta ^* \in (0,1)\) of the whole. Shannon’s Asymptotic Equipartition Property (AEP) is an instance: for a source with \(0 < H < \log _2|\mathcal {A}|\), the \(|\mathcal {A}|^n\) sequences concentrate onto a typical set of size \(2^{n(H+o(1))}\), so \(\theta ^* = H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\), although the typical set is a vanishing fraction of \(\mathcal {A}^n\). Shannon’s Channel Coding Theorem shares only the size condition: the largest reliable codebook in \(\mathcal {X}^n\) has \(2^{n(C+o(1))}\) codewords, with \(C = \max _{p(x)} I(X;Y)\), but no measure given by the channel concentrates on it. These constants vary continuously with the source and the channel and are not values of the \(k\)-hierarchy \(\theta _k = (2^k - k)/2^k\).

The ﬁrst version also claimed a third, arithmetic instance: primes modulo powers of \(3\), governed by \(\thetaW = 5/8\). That claim is withdrawn. Primes are equidistributed between the residue classes \(1\) and \(2\) modulo \(3\), so they do not concentrate on the proposed subspace; the summed estimate over the moduli \(3^K\) holds for every exponent up to \(1\); and \(\thetaW = 5/8\) for these moduli is a conjecture of the companion paper [12]. The Sophie Germain “non-instance” cannot discriminate between depths, since \(\theta _1 = \theta _2 = 1/2\), and is withdrawn as a test. We record the remaining parallels as informal analogies and state the Constitutional Forcing Conjecture, in which the counting formula is a postulate.

1. Introduction

Every mathematician knows at some level that information theory and number theory are diﬀerent subjects. Information theory studies the storage and transmission of data; number theory studies the arithmetic structure of integers. They have diﬀerent objects, diﬀerent tools, and diﬀerent communities.

This paper proposes a structural parallel between the two and tests it.

In 1948 Shannon published the two results that deﬁne the fundamental limits of compression and communication [1]. The Asymptotic Equipartition Property (AEP) says that most sequences are typical: the \(|\mathcal {A}|^n\) possible sequences of length \(n\) concentrate on an eﬀective set of size \(2^{n(H+o(1))}\), governed by the entropy \(H\); when \(H < \log _2|\mathcal {A}|\) this set is a vanishing fraction of \(\mathcal {A}^n\). The Channel Coding Theorem says that reliable communication at rate \(R\) is possible for \(R < C\) and impossible for \(R > C\), where \(C\) is the channel capacity. Shannon’s 1948 argument for the coding theorem was a random-coding sketch; rigorous proofs followed (Feinstein, 1954). In both cases the governing constant is a continuous function of the source or channel statistics.

The ﬁrst version of this paper proposed an analogous arithmetic instance: that the algebraic structure of \(\Zomega \) forces primes in arithmetic progressions modulo the cascade moduli \(3^K\) to concentrate onto an eﬀective subspace, governed by \(\thetaW = 5/8\). Section 5 shows that this proposal fails: primes are equidistributed between the classes \(1\) and \(2\) modulo \(3\), the summed estimate behind it holds for every exponent up to \(1\), and \(\thetaW = 5/8\) for these moduli is a conjecture [12].

1.1. Two instances and a withdrawn third.

(1)
Shannon AEP (1948). For \(0 < H < \log _2|\mathcal {A}|\), source sequences of length \(n\) concentrate onto \(2^{n(H+o(1))}\) typical sequences out of \(|\mathcal {A}|^n\). Governing constant: \(H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\), a continuous function of the source distribution \(p\) (Proposition 3.2).
(2)
Shannon Channel Coding (1948). Reliable codes exist at all rates \(R < C\) and not at rates \(R > C\), where \(C = \max _{p(x)} I(X;Y)\). Governing constant: \(C/\log _2|\mathcal {X}|\), a continuous function of the channel \(p(y|x)\). This case satisﬁes only the size conditions of the deﬁnition (Proposition 4.2).
(3)
Cascade moduli (proposed in the ﬁrst version). This is not an instance (Proposition 5.2); \(\thetaW = 5/8\) for the moduli \(3^K\) is conjectural [12].

Sophie Germain primes, presented in the ﬁrst version as a conﬁrmed non-instance, are discussed in Section 7: the example cannot discriminate between depths.

1.2. What this paper claims and does not claim. We attach logical-status labels to every signiﬁcant claim. Here we state the top-level ones.

  • Under the hypotheses \(0 < H < \log _2|\mathcal {A}|\) and \(\varepsilon _n \to 0\) with \(n\varepsilon _n^2 \to \infty \), the AEP satisﬁes (SW1)–(SW4) with \(\theta ^* = H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\) (Proposition 3.2).

  • Channel coding, recast on \(\mathcal {X}^n\), satisﬁes the size conditions with \(\theta ^* = C/\log _2|\mathcal {X}|\) but not the concentration condition (Proposition 4.2).

  • The cascade-moduli system does not satisfy (SW3) (Proposition 5.2), and the summed estimate of the ﬁrst version holds for every exponent up to \(1\) (Proposition 5.1).

  • \(\thetaW = 5/8\) for the moduli \(3^K\), per modulus [12]; this would go beyond the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis.

  • The Sophie Germain remark of Section 7; it is not a test of the framework.

  • The parallels of Section 6. They are informal analogies, not theorems.

  • The Constitutional Forcing Conjecture (Conjecture 9.1), whose hypothesis (iii) is a postulate.

  • Specifying any object for \(k = 4\) (Remark 11.1); without one, \(\theta _4 = 3/4\) is not a testable prediction.

  • A functorial or categorical equivalence between information theory and number theory.

1.3. Position in the programme. This paper is the sixth in a nine-paper programme (p01–p09) on constitutional sieve theory [9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14]. The formula \(\theta _k = (2^k - k)/2^k\) is obtained in [9, 13] from a counting postulate. The level \(\thetaW = 5/8\) for the moduli \(3^K\) is a conjecture of [12]. Condition W3 of [14] is true, with a one-line proof from the cyclicity of \((\Z /3^K\Z )^*\), and does not bear on any level of distribution. The methodological framework is articulated in [11].

2. The Shannon–Wakil Eﬀect: Deﬁnition

Deﬁnition 2.1 (Shannon–Wakil Eﬀect). A system \(\mathcal {S} = (\mathcal {C}_n, \mu _n)_{n \geq 1}\), consisting of ﬁnite sets \(\mathcal {C}_n\) and probability measures \(\mu _n\) on them, exhibits the Shannon–Wakil eﬀect with governing constant \(\Phi (\mathcal {S}) = \theta ^*\) if:

(SW1)
Exponential conﬁguration space. \(|\mathcal {C}_n|\) grows exponentially in \(n\): \(|\mathcal {C}_n| = e^{n(r + o(1))}\) for some \(r > 0\).
(SW2)
Intrinsic structural constraint. \(\mu _n\) is a measure or distributional law intrinsic to \(\mathcal {S}\)—not an external parameter.
(SW3)
Dimensional reduction. There exist eﬀective subspaces \(\mathcal {E}_n \subsetneq \mathcal {C}_n\) with \(|\mathcal {E}_n| = e^{n(\theta ^* r + o(1))}\) for some \(\theta ^* \in (0,1)\), onto which \(\mu _n\) concentrates: \(\mu _n(\mathcal {C}_n \setminus \mathcal {E}_n) \to 0\).
(SW4)
Determination of the governing constant. The constant \(\theta ^*\) is determined by the data of \(\mathcal {S}\).

Remark 2.2. (SW1) and (SW3) are the mathematical content of Deﬁnition 2.1. (SW2) and (SW4) are informal requirements: “intrinsic” is not deﬁned, and any quantity deﬁned from \(\mathcal {S}\) is determined by \(\mathcal {S}\), so (SW4) adds nothing beyond requiring \(\theta ^*\) to be deﬁned from the system. The ﬁrst version called (SW4) “the critical one”, required \(\theta ^*\) to be “uniquely determined by the algebraic structure”, and asserted that in the cascade case changing \(\theta ^*\) “would require changing the prime \(3\) itself”. Entropy and capacity are continuous functions of real parameters, not algebraic invariants, and the cascade case is withdrawn (Section 5); those statements are withdrawn.

3. First Instance: Shannon’s AEP

Let \(\mathcal {A}\) be a ﬁnite alphabet with probability distribution \(p\) on \(\mathcal {A}\). Let \(X_1, X_2, \ldots \) be i.i.d. with law \(p\). The entropy is \(H = H(p) = -\sum _{a \in \mathcal {A}} p(a) \log _2 p(a)\).

Theorem 3.1 (AEP, [2, Theorem 3.1.2]). For any \(\varepsilon > 0\), the typical set \[ T_\varepsilon ^{(n)} = \left \{ x^n \in \mathcal {A}^n : \left | -\frac {1}{n} \log _2 p(x^n) - H \right | < \varepsilon \right \} \] satisﬁes: (i) \(\Pr [X^n \in T_\varepsilon ^{(n)}] \to 1\); (ii) \(|T_\varepsilon ^{(n)}| \leq 2^{n(H+\varepsilon )}\); (iii) for large \(n\), \(|T_\varepsilon ^{(n)}| \geq (1-\varepsilon )2^{n(H-\varepsilon )}\).

Proposition 3.2 (AEP Exhibits SW Eﬀect). Suppose \(0 < H(p) < \log _2|\mathcal {A}|\), and let \(\varepsilon _n \to 0\) with \(n\varepsilon _n^2 \to \infty \). Then the system \(\mathcal {S}_{AEP} = (\mathcal {A}^n, p^{\otimes n})\) satisﬁes (SW1)–(SW4) with eﬀective subspaces \(\mathcal {E}_n = T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)}\) and governing constant \(\theta ^* = H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\).

Proof. (SW1): \(|\mathcal {A}^n| = e^{nr}\) with \(r = \ln |\mathcal {A}| > 0\). (SW2): \(p^{\otimes n}\) is the i.i.d. law of the source. (SW3): \(-\frac {1}{n}\log _2 p(X^n)\) is the mean of \(n\) i.i.d. copies of \(-\log _2 p(X_1)\), which has mean \(H\) and ﬁnite variance \(\sigma ^2\), so by Chebyshev’s inequality \(\Pr [X^n \notin T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)}] \leq \sigma ^2/(n\varepsilon _n^2) \to 0\). Every \(x^n \in T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)}\) has \(2^{-n(H+\varepsilon _n)} < p(x^n) < 2^{-n(H-\varepsilon _n)}\), so \((1-o(1))\,2^{n(H-\varepsilon _n)} \leq |T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)}| \leq 2^{n(H+\varepsilon _n)}\). Hence \(|T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)}| = 2^{n(H+o(1))} = e^{n(\theta ^* r + o(1))}\) with \(\theta ^* = H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}| \in (0,1)\), and \(T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)} \neq \mathcal {A}^n\) once \(\varepsilon _n < \log _2|\mathcal {A}| - H\). (SW4): \(\theta ^*\) is a function of \(p\). □

Remark 3.3. The hypotheses are needed. For the uniform source every \(x^n\) has \(-\frac {1}{n}\log _2 p(x^n) = H\), so the typical set is all of \(\mathcal {A}^n\) and the exponent ratio is \(1\); for a deterministic source it is \(0\). With \(\varepsilon \) ﬁxed, Theorem 3.1 pins \(|T_\varepsilon ^{(n)}|\) only between \(2^{n(H \pm \varepsilon )}\). The typical set is a vanishing fraction of \(\mathcal {A}^n\): \(|T_{\varepsilon _n}^{(n)}|/|\mathcal {A}|^n = 2^{-n(\log _2|\mathcal {A}| - H + o(1))} \to 0\). The governing constant \(H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\) takes every value in \((0,1)\) as \(p\) varies (for example \(0.918\ldots \) for \(p = (1/3, 2/3)\)); it is not \(1/2\) in general.

4. Shannon’s Channel Coding Theorem: A Partial Instance

A discrete memoryless channel is a conditional distribution \(p(y|x)\) from input alphabet \(\mathcal {X}\) to output alphabet \(\mathcal {Y}\). The channel capacity is \[ C = \max _{p(x)} I(X;Y) = \max _{p(x)} \sum _{x,y} p(x)p(y|x) \log \frac {p(y|x)}{p(y)}. \]

Theorem 4.1 (Channel Coding, [2, Ch. 7]). (Achievability) For any \(R < C\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exist codes of rate \(R\) and blocklength \(n\) with maximal error probability \(< \varepsilon \) for all suﬃciently large \(n\).

(Converse) For any \(R > C\), every sequence of codes of rate \(R\) has error probability bounded away from \(0\).

The ﬁrst version took as conﬁguration space the set of all codebooks of rate \(R\) and blocklength \(n\). There are \(|\mathcal {X}|^{n \cdot 2^{nR}}\) of them, which is doubly exponential in \(n\), so that choice violates (SW1); the ﬁrst version nevertheless asserted (SW1). It also took the channel law \(p(y|x)\) as the measure, but \(p(y|x)\) is not a measure on codebooks. We use instead \(\mathcal {C}_n = \mathcal {X}^n\), in which a codebook is a subset. For \(0 < \varepsilon < 1\) let \(M^*(n,\varepsilon )\) be the largest number of codewords in a code of blocklength \(n\) with maximal error probability at most \(\varepsilon \).

Proposition 4.2 (Channel coding and the SW conditions). Suppose \(0 < C < \log _2|\mathcal {X}|\). Then \[ \lim _{\varepsilon \to 0}\, \liminf _{n \to \infty } \tfrac {1}{n}\log _2 M^*(n,\varepsilon ) = \lim _{\varepsilon \to 0}\, \limsup _{n \to \infty } \tfrac {1}{n}\log _2 M^*(n,\varepsilon ) = C. \] Thus a largest codebook \(\mathcal {E}_n \subsetneq \mathcal {X}^n\) with maximal error at most \(\varepsilon \) has \(|\mathcal {E}_n| = 2^{n(C+o(1))}\) in this iterated sense, and (SW1) and the size part of (SW3) hold with \(\theta ^* = C/\log _2|\mathcal {X}|\). The concentration part of (SW3) has no counterpart: no measure on \(\mathcal {X}^n\) given by the channel concentrates on \(\mathcal {E}_n\). Channel coding is therefore not an instance of Deﬁnition 2.1 as stated.

Proof. By achievability, \(\liminf _n \frac {1}{n}\log _2 M^*(n,\varepsilon ) \geq R\) for every \(R < C\) and every \(\varepsilon > 0\). A code with maximal error at most \(\varepsilon \) has average error at most \(\varepsilon \), so Fano’s inequality gives \((1-\varepsilon )\log _2 M \leq nC + 1\) for its size \(M\), and \(\limsup _n \frac {1}{n}\log _2 M^*(n,\varepsilon ) \leq C/(1-\varepsilon )\). Let \(\varepsilon \to 0\). □

Remark 4.3. The AEP satisﬁes Deﬁnition 2.1 (Proposition 3.2); channel coding satisﬁes only its size conditions (Proposition 4.2). The ﬁrst version took the fact that one deﬁnition “captures both source coding and channel coding limits” as evidence that Deﬁnition 2.1 is the right one; that inference is withdrawn. As with the AEP, the constant \(C/\log _2|\mathcal {X}|\) varies continuously with the channel: for the binary symmetric channel with crossover probability \(\epsilon \) it is \(1 - H(\epsilon )\), which equals \(1/2\) only at \(\epsilon \approx 0.110\).

5. The Proposed Third Instance: Cascade Moduli

The ﬁrst version stated, as its third instance, the estimate below with the range \(3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon }\), citing Theorem 1.1 of the ﬁrst version of [12]. It asserted that \(5/8\) is “uniquely forced” by a “three-level Eisenstein ﬁltration” of \(\Zomega /(3^K)\) and the cyclicity of \((\Z /3^K\Z )^*\), and that no \(\theta > 5/8\) is achievable for cascade moduli. In fact the estimate holds for every exponent up to \(1\).

Proposition 5.1 (The summed estimate is trivial). For every \(A > 0\), \[ \sum _{\substack {K \geq 1 \\ 3^K \leq x}} \max _{\gcd (a,3)=1} \left | \sum _{\substack {n \leq x \\ n \equiv a\,(3^K)}} \Lambda (n) - \frac {x}{\varphi (3^K)} \right | \ll _A \frac {x}{(\log x)^A}. \]

Proof. Write \(\psi (x;q,a)\) for the inner sum and let \(L = (\log x)^{A+1}\). For \(3^K \leq L\) the Siegel–Walﬁsz theorem gives \(\max _a |\psi (x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi (3^K)| \ll _A x\exp (-c\sqrt {\log x})\), and there are \(O(\log \log x)\) such \(K\). For \(3^K > L\), the trivial bounds \(\psi (x;q,a) \leq (x/q + 1)\log x\) and \(x/\varphi (3^K) = 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)\) give \(|\psi (x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi (3^K)| \leq (x/3^K + 1)\log x + 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)\). Summing over the \(O(\log x)\) values of \(K\) with \(L < 3^K \leq x\) gives \(\ll x\log x/L + (\log x)^2 \ll x/(\log x)^A\). □

So the estimate of the ﬁrst version is true, but its exponent plays no role, and the claims that \(5/8\) is “uniquely forced” and that “no \(\theta > 5/8\) is achievable” are withdrawn. The \((1-\omega )\)-adic ﬁltration of \(\Zomega /(3^K)\) has \(2K\) steps, each with quotient \(\mathbb {F}_3\), not three levels, and it does not determine the value \(5/8\). The meaningful statement is per modulus: for \(q = 3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon }\), \(\max _{a}|\psi (x;q,a) - x/\varphi (q)| \ll x/(\varphi (q)(\log x)^A)\). This is a conjecture of [12]. For a single modulus the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis gives it only for \(q \leq x^{1/2-\varepsilon }\), and the best known results for prime-power moduli are those of Barban–Linnik–Tshudakov (exponent \(3/8\)), Gallagher (\(2/5\)) and Huxley (\(5/12\)); Banks and Shparlinski obtain lower bounds of the correct order for moduli up to \(x^{0.4736}\). Pascadi’s exponent \(5/8\) [7] concerns smooth numbers, and primes with triply-well-factorable weights, in arithmetic progressions to general moduli; it is obtained by spectral methods and is unrelated to the moduli \(3^K\) or to \(\Zomega \). Its agreement with \(\theta _3\) is a numerical coincidence.

In [9, 13] the value \(5/8 = (2^3 - 3)/2^3\) comes from a counting postulate: a system of depth \(k\) has exactly \(k\) of its \(2^k\) binary conﬁgurations eliminated. It is not derived: if “invalid” means “some constraint fails”, there are \(2^k - 1\) invalid conﬁgurations. The assignment of depth \(3\) to cascade moduli or twin primes is proposed and currently unsupported: for \(p > 3\) twin primes satisfy only \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\), and the conditions modulo \(9\) and \(27\) are implied by it.

Proposition 5.2 (Cascade moduli do not exhibit the SW eﬀect). Let \(\mathcal {C}_K = (\Z /3^K\Z )^*\), let \(\mathcal {E}_K = \{a \in \mathcal {C}_K : a \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\}\), and let \(\mu _K\) be the limiting distribution, as \(x \to \infty \), of the primes \(p \leq x\) among the classes of \(\mathcal {C}_K\). Then (SW1) holds, but \(\mu _K(\mathcal {C}_K \setminus \mathcal {E}_K) = 1/2\) for every \(K\) and \(\log |\mathcal {E}_K|/\log |\mathcal {C}_K| \to 1\). So (SW3) fails on both counts.

Proof. \(|\mathcal {C}_K| = \varphi (3^K) = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}\), which is exponential in \(K\). By the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions, \(\mu _K\) is the uniform distribution on \(\mathcal {C}_K\), and exactly half of the classes lie in \(\mathcal {E}_K\). Finally \(|\mathcal {E}_K| = 3^{K-1}\), so \(\log |\mathcal {E}_K|/\log |\mathcal {C}_K| = (K-1)\log 3/(\log 2 + (K-1)\log 3) \to 1\). □

The ﬁrst version asserted that “primes concentrate onto the inert sublattice \(\mathcal {E}_K\)” and that “the level of distribution \(5/8\) measures the depth of this concentration”. The condition \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\) constrains the smaller member of a twin-prime pair (and Sophie Germain primes \(p > 3\)), not primes in general, and Proposition 5.1 concerns all primes through \(\Lambda (n)\). “Depth of concentration” was not deﬁned, and \(5/8\) is not the exponent ratio of any set deﬁned in this paper. Levels of distribution are downward closed (if a level \(\theta \) holds, so does every smaller one), so \(5/8\) cannot be “the unique value consistent” with any structure. Condition W3 of [14] is true, with a one-line proof from the cyclicity of \((\Z /3^K\Z )^*\), and does not bear on the level.

6.2. The formula analogy. The ﬁrst version asserted that all three governing constants obey \[ \theta ^* = \frac {\text {total} - \text {constraint}}{\text {total}}. \] Any \(\theta \in [0,1]\) can be written in this form (take total \(T\) and constraint \((1-\theta )T\)), so the form constrains nothing. Speciﬁcally:

  • AEP: \(H = \log _2|\mathcal {A}| - D(p \| u)\), where \(D(p\|u)\) is the Kullback–Leibler divergence from the uniform distribution \(u\) on \(\mathcal {A}\). This identity is correct, and gives \(H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}| = 1 - D(p\|u)/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\).

  • Channel Coding: the ﬁrst version wrote \(C = \log |\mathcal {X}| - \min _{p(x)} H(X|Y)\). That formula is false: a point mass \(p(x)\) gives \(H(X|Y) = 0\), so it would give \(C = \log |\mathcal {X}|\) for every channel. The correct formula is \(C = \max _{p(x)} I(X;Y) = \max _{p(x)}\bigl [H(X) - H(X|Y)\bigr ]\), a maximum of a diﬀerence rather than a ﬁxed total minus a ﬁxed constraint. For a symmetric channel it equals \(\log _2|\mathcal {Y}| - H(\mathbf {r})\), where \(\mathbf {r}\) is a row of the transition matrix [2, Ch. 7]; for the binary symmetric channel with crossover probability \(\epsilon \) this is \(1 - H(\epsilon )\).

  • Cascade moduli: \(\thetaW = (2^k - k)/2^k\) with \(k = 3\), read as \(8\) conﬁgurations of which \(3\) are eliminated. The ﬁrst version said that the Eisenstein ﬁltration “provides \(2^k = 8\) total conﬁguration dimensions”. The ﬁltration has \(2K\) steps and provides no such count, \(\{0,1\}^3\) has \(8\) points (not \(8\) dimensions), and the elimination count is the postulate of [9, 13].

6.3. The reduction analogy.

  • AEP: typical sequences are those whose empirical log-probability per symbol is close to the entropy.

  • Channel Coding: in the random-coding proof of achievability, decoding uses joint typicality of codewords and channel outputs.

  • Cascade moduli: no analogue. Primes are equidistributed between the inert and the split classes (Proposition 5.2).

7. Sophie Germain Primes

The ﬁrst version presented Sophie Germain pairs \((p, 2p+1)\) as a “conﬁrmed non-instance”. It stated as a theorem, with a citation to itself, that they have dimensional parameter \(k = 2\) because the residue of \(2p+1\) modulo \(3\) is determined by that of \(p\); that their predicted level of distribution is \(\theta _2 = 1/2\); and that a computation gives \(\delta \cdot \log x = 2.38 \pm 0.18\), “inconsistent with \(\theta > 1/2\) at \(p < 0.001\)”. All of this is withdrawn. We keep only the following remark.

Remark 7.1 (Sophie Germain pairs; heuristic). For \(p > 3\), if \(p \equiv 1 \pmod {3}\) then \(3 \mid 2p+1\), so Sophie Germain primes \(p > 3\) satisfy \(p \equiv 2 \pmod {3}\). This is the same single condition as for the smaller member of a twin-prime pair, where the residue of \(p+2\) is equally determined by that of \(p\); the “coupling” argument for \(k = 2\) would therefore apply to twin primes too. Since \(\theta _1 = \theta _2 = 1/2\), assigning either depth predicts \(1/2\), so the example cannot discriminate between depths and is not a falsiﬁcation test. A “level of distribution of Sophie Germain pairs” is not a deﬁned notion, and no such result is known. The quantity \(\delta \) of the ﬁrst version was never deﬁned, and no ﬁnite computation can conﬁrm or refute an asymptotic level of distribution.

The ﬁrst version compared this coupling to a degraded channel, saying that a degraded channel has lower capacity “because extra dependence between input and output is introduced”. That is backwards. Degradation \(X \to Y \to Z\) destroys input–output dependence: \(I(X;Z) \leq I(X;Y)\) by the data-processing inequality, so the degraded channel has capacity at most that of the original. With this correction the analogy to “coupling reduces \(k\)” does not hold.

Remark 8.1. Note that \(k=1\) and \(k=2\) both give \(\theta = 1/2\). Bombieri–Vinogradov gives level \(1/2\) for primes on average over moduli \(q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}\); that \(1/2\) comes from the large sieve inequality, not from a count of constraints. Levels beyond \(1/2\) are known for primes with well-factorable weights and a ﬁxed residue class: \(4/7\) [4], \(3/5\) [6], \(66/107\) [5] and \(5/8\) [7]. 9. The Constitutional Forcing Conjecture

Conjecture 9.1 (Constitutional Forcing Conjecture). Let \(\mathcal {S}\) be a system in which:

(i)
the conﬁguration space is exponential with growth rate \(r > 0\);
(ii)
the structural law is intrinsic (not an external parameter);
(iii)
the system has depth \(k\) in the sense of the counting postulate of [9, 13]: exactly \(k\) of its \(2^k\) binary conﬁguration classes are eliminated by its \(k\) independent constitutional constraints.

Then \(\mathcal {S}\) exhibits the Shannon–Wakil eﬀect with governing constant \(\theta ^* = (2^k - k)/2^k\).

If a conﬁguration \(c \in \{0,1\}^k\) is called invalid when some constraint fails (\(c_i = 0\) for some \(i\)), then \(k\) constraints invalidate \(2^k - 1\) conﬁgurations, not \(k\). Hypothesis (iii) is therefore a postulate about the system, not a consequence of its having \(k\) binary constraints.

Remark 9.2. The ﬁrst version said that Conjecture 9.1 “encompasses information theory (\(k=1\): entropy/capacity)” and “prime constellations (\(k=3\): twin primes via Eisenstein structure)”. Neither holds. The Shannon constants \(H/\log _2|\mathcal {A}|\) and \(C/\log _2|\mathcal {X}|\) are continuous functions of the source and the channel and take every value in \((0,1)\), so they are not values \(\theta _k\), and in particular not \(\theta _1 = 1/2\); and twin primes have depth \(1\) by the programme’s own independence criterion (Section 5). No system is currently known that satisﬁes (iii) and exhibits the eﬀect, so the conjecture is at present a proposal. It would be refuted by a system satisfying (i)–(iii) whose governing constant diﬀers from \(\theta _k\).

10. Shannon–Wakil Eﬀect vs. Analogies

It is important to distinguish the Shannon–Wakil eﬀect from informal analogy.

An analogy between two ﬁelds observes superﬁcial similarity and draws heuristic inspiration. The comparison is suggestive but not formally veriﬁable. Analogies are valuable but limited: they can be wrong in ways that are hard to detect, and they generate no falsiﬁable predictions.

The ﬁrst version argued that the Shannon–Wakil eﬀect diﬀers from an analogy in three ways. As revised, the situation is as follows.

First, only the AEP satisﬁes Deﬁnition 2.1 (Proposition 3.2). Channel coding satisﬁes its size conditions only (Proposition 4.2), and the cascade case fails (SW3) (Proposition 5.2).

Second, the framework has not yet produced a falsiﬁable prediction. The Sophie Germain example cannot discriminate between depths (Remark 7.1), and no object is speciﬁed for \(k=4\) (Remark 11.1).

Third, the “formula correspondence” is not quantitatively precise: any \(\theta \in [0,1]\) can be written as \((\text {total} - \text {constraint})/\text {total}\) (Section 6.2).

The parallels of Section 6 are therefore, at present, informal analogies in the sense of the preceding paragraph.

11. Discussion and Open Problems

11.1. Chronology. Shannon’s paper appeared in 1948 [1]. It contains the AEP (for ergodic Markov sources) and the channel coding theorem, the latter proved by a random-coding argument that was made rigorous later (Feinstein, 1954). The arithmetic case was proposed in 2026 and is not established (Section 5). The ﬁrst version argued that the gap between the two guarantees that neither result was constructed to match the other; since the arithmetic case is withdrawn, the chronology carries no evidential weight.

11.2. No object for \(k=4\).

Remark 11.1 (No object for \(k = 4\)). The formula gives \(\theta _4 = (2^4 - 4)/2^4 = 12/16 = 3/4\). The ﬁrst version predicted “a prime constellation with exactly \(k = 4\) independent modular constraints, exhibiting level of distribution \(\theta = 3/4\)”. No such object (sequence, family of moduli or sieve) is speciﬁed, and a level of distribution is a property of a sequence in arithmetic progressions, not of a constellation. So \(\theta _4 = 3/4\) is not at present a testable prediction. Specifying such an object is the open problem.

11.3. The ratio \(5/4\). The ﬁrst version called \(\thetaW / \thetaBV = (5/8)/(1/2) = 5/4\) the gain in level of distribution achieved by the Eisenstein structure over Bombieri–Vinogradov. That interpretation is withdrawn. Bombieri–Vinogradov is an average over all moduli \(q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}\), whereas the cascade family has \(O(\log x)\) moduli. For that family the summed estimate holds trivially up to exponent \(1\) (Proposition 5.1), and the per-modulus level \(5/8\) is conjectural. Levels above \(1/2\) for general moduli are known with well-factorable weights (Section 8).

11.4. Channel capacity and levels of distribution. Both the channel capacity \(C = \max _{p(x)} I(X;Y)\) and a level of distribution are deﬁned as suprema. For the moduli \(3^K\) the supremum of the admissible \(\theta \) depends on the formulation: for the summed estimate of Proposition 5.1 it is \(1\), and in the per-modulus form it is unknown (Montgomery’s conjecture predicts \(1\)). If \(5/8\) were proved for these moduli, it would be a lower bound for that supremum, not the supremum itself. The ﬁrst version’s claim that this “maximisation correspondence is the most precise of the ﬁve” is withdrawn.
