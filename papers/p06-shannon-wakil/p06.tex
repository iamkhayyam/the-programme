\begin{abstract}
We formalize a structural pattern that we call the \emph{Shannon--Wakil effect}:
an exponential configuration space on which a natural measure concentrates onto a
strict subspace whose size, on the exponential scale, is a fixed fraction
$\theta^* \in (0,1)$ of the whole. Shannon's Asymptotic Equipartition Property
(AEP) is an instance: for a source with $0 < H < \log_2|\mathcal{A}|$, the
$|\mathcal{A}|^n$ sequences concentrate onto a typical set of size
$2^{n(H+o(1))}$, so $\theta^* = H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}|$, although the typical set
is a vanishing fraction of $\mathcal{A}^n$. Shannon's Channel Coding Theorem
shares only the size condition: the largest reliable codebook in
$\mathcal{X}^n$ has $2^{n(C+o(1))}$ codewords, with $C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$,
but no measure given by the channel concentrates on it. These constants vary
continuously with the source and the channel and are not values of the
$k$-hierarchy $\theta_k = (2^k - k)/2^k$.

The first version also claimed a third, arithmetic instance: primes modulo powers
of $3$, governed by $\thetaW = 5/8$. That claim is withdrawn. Primes are
equidistributed between the residue classes $1$ and $2$ modulo $3$, so they do
not concentrate on the proposed subspace; the summed estimate over the moduli
$3^K$ holds for every exponent up to $1$; and $\thetaW = 5/8$ for these moduli is
a conjecture of the companion paper~\cite{WakilEisenstein}. The Sophie Germain
``non-instance'' cannot discriminate between depths, since
$\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 1/2$, and is withdrawn as a test. We record the remaining
parallels as informal analogies and state the Constitutional Forcing Conjecture,
in which the counting formula is a postulate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every mathematician knows at some level that information theory and number theory
are different subjects. Information theory studies the storage and transmission of
data; number theory studies the arithmetic structure of integers. They have
different objects, different tools, and different communities.

This paper proposes a structural parallel between the two and tests it.

In 1948 Shannon published the two results that define the fundamental limits of
compression and communication~\cite{Shannon1948}. The Asymptotic Equipartition
Property (AEP) says that most sequences are typical: the $|\mathcal{A}|^n$
possible sequences of length $n$ concentrate on an effective set of size
$2^{n(H+o(1))}$, governed by the entropy $H$; when $H < \log_2|\mathcal{A}|$ this
set is a vanishing fraction of $\mathcal{A}^n$. The Channel Coding Theorem says
that reliable communication at rate $R$ is possible for $R < C$ and impossible
for $R > C$, where $C$ is the channel capacity. Shannon's 1948 argument for the
coding theorem was a random-coding sketch; rigorous proofs followed (Feinstein,
1954). In both cases the governing constant is a continuous function of the
source or channel statistics.

The first version of this paper proposed an analogous arithmetic instance: that
the algebraic structure of $\Zomega$ forces primes in arithmetic progressions
modulo the cascade moduli $3^K$ to concentrate onto an effective subspace,
governed by $\thetaW = 5/8$. Section~\ref{sec:cascade} shows that this proposal
fails: primes are equidistributed between the classes $1$ and $2$ modulo $3$, the
summed estimate behind it holds for every exponent up to $1$, and $\thetaW = 5/8$
for these moduli is a conjecture~\cite{WakilEisenstein}.

\subsection{Two instances and a withdrawn third}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Shannon AEP (1948).} For $0 < H < \log_2|\mathcal{A}|$, source
    sequences of length $n$ concentrate onto $2^{n(H+o(1))}$ typical sequences
    out of $|\mathcal{A}|^n$. Governing constant: $H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}|$, a
    continuous function of the source distribution $p$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:aep-sw}).

  \item \textbf{Shannon Channel Coding (1948).} Reliable codes exist at all rates
    $R < C$ and not at rates $R > C$, where $C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$. Governing
    constant: $C/\log_2|\mathcal{X}|$, a continuous function of the channel
    $p(y|x)$. This case satisfies only the size conditions of the definition
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel-sw}).

  \item \textbf{Cascade moduli (proposed in the first version).} This is not an
    instance (Proposition~\ref{prop:cascade-sw}); $\thetaW = 5/8$ for the moduli
    $3^K$ is conjectural~\cite{WakilEisenstein}.
\end{enumerate}

Sophie Germain primes, presented in the first version as a confirmed
non-instance, are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sg}: the example cannot
discriminate between depths.

\subsection{What this paper claims and does not claim}
\label{sec:claims}

We attach logical-status labels to every significant claim. Here we state the
top-level ones.

\begin{itemize}

  \item[\textbf{[Conjectured]}] $\thetaW = 5/8$ for the moduli $3^K$, per

  \item[\textbf{[Heuristic]}] The Sophie Germain remark of
    Section~\ref{sec:sg}; it is not a test of the framework.

  \item[\textbf{[Analogy]}] The parallels of

  \item[\textbf{[Conjectured]}] The Constitutional Forcing Conjecture

  \item[\textbf{[Not claimed]}] A functorial or categorical equivalence between

\section{The Shannon--Wakil Effect: Definition}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{definition}[Shannon--Wakil Effect]
\label{def:sw}
A system $\mathcal{S} = (\mathcal{C}_n, \mu_n)_{n \geq 1}$, consisting of finite
sets $\mathcal{C}_n$ and probability measures $\mu_n$ on them, exhibits the
\emph{Shannon--Wakil effect} with governing constant $\Phi(\mathcal{S}) =
\theta^*$ if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(SW\arabic*)]
  \item \textbf{Exponential configuration space.} $|\mathcal{C}_n|$ grows
    exponentially in $n$: $|\mathcal{C}_n| = e^{n(r + o(1))}$ for some $r > 0$.

  \item \textbf{Intrinsic structural constraint.} $\mu_n$ is a measure or
    distributional law intrinsic to $\mathcal{S}$---not an external parameter.

  \item \textbf{Dimensional reduction.} There exist effective subspaces
    $\mathcal{E}_n \subsetneq \mathcal{C}_n$ with $|\mathcal{E}_n| = e^{n(\theta^* r
    + o(1))}$ for some $\theta^* \in (0,1)$, onto which $\mu_n$ concentrates:
    $\mu_n(\mathcal{C}_n \setminus \mathcal{E}_n) \to 0$.

  \item \textbf{Determination of the governing constant.} The constant $\theta^*$
    is determined by the data of $\mathcal{S}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:sw-informal}
(SW1) and (SW3) are the mathematical content of Definition~\ref{def:sw}. (SW2)
and (SW4) are informal requirements: ``intrinsic'' is not defined, and any
quantity defined from $\mathcal{S}$ is determined by $\mathcal{S}$, so (SW4) adds
nothing beyond requiring $\theta^*$ to be defined from the system. The first
version called (SW4) ``the critical one'', required $\theta^*$ to be ``uniquely
determined by the algebraic structure'', and asserted that in the cascade case
changing $\theta^*$ ``would require changing the prime $3$ itself''. Entropy and
capacity are continuous functions of real parameters, not algebraic invariants,
and the cascade case is withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:cascade}); those statements
are withdrawn.
\end{remark}

\section{First Instance: Shannon's AEP}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a finite alphabet with probability distribution $p$ on
$\mathcal{A}$. Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be i.i.d.\ with law $p$. The entropy is
$H = H(p) = -\sum_{a \in \mathcal{A}} p(a) \log_2 p(a)$.

\begin{theorem}[AEP,~{\cite[Theorem~3.1.2]{CoverThomas2006}}]
\label{thm:aep}
For any $\varepsilon > 0$, the typical set
\[
  T_\varepsilon^{(n)} = \left\{ x^n \in \mathcal{A}^n :
    \left| -\frac{1}{n} \log_2 p(x^n) - H \right| < \varepsilon \right\}
\]
satisfies: \emph{(i)} $\Pr[X^n \in T_\varepsilon^{(n)}] \to 1$;
\emph{(ii)} $|T_\varepsilon^{(n)}| \leq 2^{n(H+\varepsilon)}$;
\emph{(iii)} for large $n$, $|T_\varepsilon^{(n)}| \geq (1-\varepsilon)2^{n(H-\varepsilon)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[AEP Exhibits SW Effect]
\label{prop:aep-sw}
Suppose $0 < H(p) < \log_2|\mathcal{A}|$, and let $\varepsilon_n \to 0$ with
$n\varepsilon_n^2 \to \infty$. Then the system
$\mathcal{S}_{AEP} = (\mathcal{A}^n, p^{\otimes n})$ satisfies
\emph{(SW1)--(SW4)} with effective subspaces
$\mathcal{E}_n = T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)}$ and governing constant
$\theta^* = H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(SW1): $|\mathcal{A}^n| = e^{nr}$ with $r = \ln|\mathcal{A}| > 0$. (SW2):
$p^{\otimes n}$ is the i.i.d.\ law of the source. (SW3): $-\frac{1}{n}\log_2
p(X^n)$ is the mean of $n$ i.i.d.\ copies of $-\log_2 p(X_1)$, which has mean $H$
and finite variance $\sigma^2$, so by Chebyshev's inequality
$\Pr[X^n \notin T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)}] \leq \sigma^2/(n\varepsilon_n^2) \to 0$.
Every $x^n \in T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)}$ has
$2^{-n(H+\varepsilon_n)} < p(x^n) < 2^{-n(H-\varepsilon_n)}$, so
$(1-o(1))\,2^{n(H-\varepsilon_n)} \leq |T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)}| \leq
2^{n(H+\varepsilon_n)}$. Hence $|T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)}| = 2^{n(H+o(1))} =
e^{n(\theta^* r + o(1))}$ with $\theta^* = H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}| \in (0,1)$, and
$T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)} \neq \mathcal{A}^n$ once $\varepsilon_n <
\log_2|\mathcal{A}| - H$. (SW4): $\theta^*$ is a function of $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:aep}
The hypotheses are needed. For the uniform source every $x^n$ has
$-\frac{1}{n}\log_2 p(x^n) = H$, so the typical set is all of $\mathcal{A}^n$ and
the exponent ratio is $1$; for a deterministic source it is $0$. With
$\varepsilon$ fixed, Theorem~\ref{thm:aep} pins $|T_\varepsilon^{(n)}|$ only
between $2^{n(H \pm \varepsilon)}$. The typical set is a vanishing fraction of
$\mathcal{A}^n$: $|T_{\varepsilon_n}^{(n)}|/|\mathcal{A}|^n =
2^{-n(\log_2|\mathcal{A}| - H + o(1))} \to 0$. The governing constant
$H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}|$ takes every value in $(0,1)$ as $p$ varies (for example
$0.918\ldots$ for $p = (1/3, 2/3)$); it is not $1/2$ in general.
\end{remark}

\section{Second Instance: Shannon's Channel Coding Theorem}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A discrete memoryless channel is a conditional distribution $p(y|x)$ from input
alphabet $\mathcal{X}$ to output alphabet $\mathcal{Y}$. The channel capacity is
\[
  C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)
    = \max_{p(x)} \sum_{x,y} p(x)p(y|x) \log \frac{p(y|x)}{p(y)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Channel Coding,~{\cite[Ch.~7]{CoverThomas2006}}]
\label{thm:channel}
\emph{(Achievability)} For any $R < C$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, there exist codes
of rate $R$ and blocklength $n$ with maximal error probability $< \varepsilon$
for all sufficiently large $n$.

\emph{(Converse)} For any $R > C$, every sequence of codes of rate $R$ has error
probability bounded away from $0$.
\end{theorem}

The first version took as configuration space the set of all codebooks of rate
$R$ and blocklength $n$. There are $|\mathcal{X}|^{n \cdot 2^{nR}}$ of them, which
is doubly exponential in $n$, so that choice violates (SW1); the first version
nevertheless asserted (SW1). It also took the channel law $p(y|x)$ as the
measure, but $p(y|x)$ is not a measure on codebooks. We use instead
$\mathcal{C}_n = \mathcal{X}^n$, in which a codebook is a subset. For
$0 < \varepsilon < 1$ let $M^*(n,\varepsilon)$ be the largest number of codewords
in a code of blocklength $n$ with maximal error probability at most $\varepsilon$.

\begin{proposition}[Channel coding and the SW conditions]
\label{prop:channel-sw}
Suppose $0 < C < \log_2|\mathcal{X}|$. Then
\[
  \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0}\, \liminf_{n \to \infty} \tfrac{1}{n}\log_2
  M^*(n,\varepsilon)
  = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0}\, \limsup_{n \to \infty} \tfrac{1}{n}\log_2
  M^*(n,\varepsilon) = C.
\]
Thus a largest codebook $\mathcal{E}_n \subsetneq \mathcal{X}^n$ with maximal
error at most $\varepsilon$ has $|\mathcal{E}_n| = 2^{n(C+o(1))}$ in this iterated
sense, and (SW1) and the size part of (SW3) hold with
$\theta^* = C/\log_2|\mathcal{X}|$. The concentration part of (SW3) has no
counterpart: no measure on $\mathcal{X}^n$ given by the channel concentrates on
$\mathcal{E}_n$. Channel coding is therefore not an instance of
Definition~\ref{def:sw} as stated.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By achievability, $\liminf_n \frac{1}{n}\log_2 M^*(n,\varepsilon) \geq R$ for
every $R < C$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$. A code with maximal error at most
$\varepsilon$ has average error at most $\varepsilon$, so Fano's inequality gives
$(1-\varepsilon)\log_2 M \leq nC + 1$ for its size $M$, and
$\limsup_n \frac{1}{n}\log_2 M^*(n,\varepsilon) \leq C/(1-\varepsilon)$. Let
$\varepsilon \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:two-shannon}
The AEP satisfies Definition~\ref{def:sw} (Proposition~\ref{prop:aep-sw});
channel coding satisfies only its size conditions
(Proposition~\ref{prop:channel-sw}). The first version took the fact that one
definition ``captures both source coding and channel coding limits'' as evidence
that Definition~\ref{def:sw} is the right one; that inference is withdrawn. As
with the AEP, the constant $C/\log_2|\mathcal{X}|$ varies continuously with the
channel: for the binary symmetric channel with crossover probability $\epsilon$
it is $1 - H(\epsilon)$, which equals $1/2$ only at $\epsilon \approx 0.110$.
\end{remark}

\section{The Proposed Third Instance: Cascade Moduli}
\label{sec:cascade}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The first version stated, as its third instance, the estimate below with the
range $3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$, citing Theorem~1.1 of the first version
of~\cite{WakilEisenstein}. It asserted that $5/8$ is ``uniquely forced'' by a
``three-level Eisenstein filtration'' of $\Zomega/(3^K)$ and the cyclicity of
$(\Z/3^K\Z)^*$, and that no $\theta > 5/8$ is achievable for cascade moduli. In
fact the estimate holds for every exponent up to $1$.

\begin{proposition}[The summed estimate is trivial]
\label{thm:cascade}
For every $A > 0$,
\[
  \sum_{\substack{K \geq 1 \\ 3^K \leq x}}
  \max_{\gcd(a,3)=1}
  \left| \sum_{\substack{n \leq x \\ n \equiv a\,(3^K)}} \Lambda(n)
    - \frac{x}{\varphi(3^K)} \right|
  \ll_A \frac{x}{(\log x)^A}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\psi(x;q,a)$ for the inner sum and let $L = (\log x)^{A+1}$. For
$3^K \leq L$ the Siegel--Walfisz theorem gives
$\max_a |\psi(x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi(3^K)| \ll_A x\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x})$, and there
are $O(\log\log x)$ such $K$. For $3^K > L$, the trivial bounds
$\psi(x;q,a) \leq (x/q + 1)\log x$ and $x/\varphi(3^K) = 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)$ give
$|\psi(x;3^K,a) - x/\varphi(3^K)| \leq (x/3^K + 1)\log x + 3x/(2 \cdot 3^K)$.
Summing over the $O(\log x)$ values of $K$ with $L < 3^K \leq x$ gives
$\ll x\log x/L + (\log x)^2 \ll x/(\log x)^A$.
\end{proof}

So the estimate of the first version is true, but its exponent plays no role,
and the claims that $5/8$ is ``uniquely forced'' and that ``no $\theta > 5/8$ is
achievable'' are withdrawn. The $(1-\omega)$-adic filtration of
$\Zomega/(3^K)$ has $2K$ steps, each with quotient $\mathbb{F}_3$, not three
levels, and it does not determine the value $5/8$. The meaningful statement is
per modulus: for $q = 3^K \leq x^{5/8-\varepsilon}$,
$\max_{a}|\psi(x;q,a) - x/\varphi(q)| \ll x/(\varphi(q)(\log x)^A)$. This is a
conjecture of~\cite{WakilEisenstein}. For a single modulus the Generalised
Riemann Hypothesis gives it only for $q \leq x^{1/2-\varepsilon}$, and the best
known results for prime-power moduli are those of Barban--Linnik--Tshudakov
(exponent $3/8$), Gallagher ($2/5$) and Huxley ($5/12$); Banks and Shparlinski
obtain lower bounds of the correct order for moduli up to $x^{0.4736}$.
Pascadi's exponent $5/8$~\cite{Pascadi2025} concerns smooth numbers, and primes
with triply-well-factorable weights, in arithmetic progressions to general
moduli; it is obtained by spectral methods and is unrelated to the moduli $3^K$
or to $\Zomega$. Its agreement with $\theta_3$ is a numerical coincidence.

In~\cite{WakilCF,WakilRamanujan} the value $5/8 = (2^3 - 3)/2^3$ comes from a
counting postulate: a system of depth $k$ has exactly $k$ of its $2^k$ binary
configurations eliminated. It is not derived: if ``invalid'' means ``some
constraint fails'', there are $2^k - 1$ invalid configurations. The assignment of
depth $3$ to cascade moduli or twin primes is proposed and currently unsupported:
for $p > 3$ twin primes satisfy only $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, and the conditions
modulo $9$ and $27$ are implied by it.

\begin{proposition}[Cascade moduli do not exhibit the SW effect]
\label{prop:cascade-sw}
Let $\mathcal{C}_K = (\Z/3^K\Z)^*$, let
$\mathcal{E}_K = \{a \in \mathcal{C}_K : a \equiv 2 \pmod{3}\}$, and let
$\mu_K$ be the limiting distribution, as $x \to \infty$, of the primes
$p \leq x$ among the classes of $\mathcal{C}_K$. Then (SW1) holds, but
$\mu_K(\mathcal{C}_K \setminus \mathcal{E}_K) = 1/2$ for every $K$ and
$\log|\mathcal{E}_K|/\log|\mathcal{C}_K| \to 1$. So (SW3) fails on both counts.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$|\mathcal{C}_K| = \varphi(3^K) = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}$, which is exponential in $K$.
By the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions, $\mu_K$ is the uniform
distribution on $\mathcal{C}_K$, and exactly half of the classes lie in
$\mathcal{E}_K$. Finally $|\mathcal{E}_K| = 3^{K-1}$, so
$\log|\mathcal{E}_K|/\log|\mathcal{C}_K| = (K-1)\log 3/(\log 2 + (K-1)\log 3) \to 1$.
\end{proof}

The first version asserted that ``primes concentrate onto the inert sublattice
$\mathcal{E}_K$'' and that ``the level of distribution $5/8$ measures the depth
of this concentration''. The condition $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ constrains the
smaller member of a twin-prime pair (and Sophie Germain primes $p > 3$), not
primes in general, and Proposition~\ref{thm:cascade} concerns all primes through
$\Lambda(n)$. ``Depth of concentration'' was not defined, and $5/8$ is not the
exponent ratio of any set defined in this paper. Levels of distribution are
downward closed (if a level $\theta$ holds, so does every smaller one), so $5/8$
cannot be ``the unique value consistent'' with any structure. Condition~W3
of~\cite{WakilW3} is true, with a one-line proof from the cyclicity of
$(\Z/3^K\Z)^*$, and does not bear on the level.

\section{Structural Analogies}
\label{sec:correspondences}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The first version called the parallels below ``five structural
correspondences'' and labelled them proven; the five were never listed. We
present them as informal analogies (matchings of roles), not as theorems,
isomorphisms or functorial equivalences. The arithmetic case is withdrawn
(Section~\ref{sec:cascade}); its column records what the first version proposed
and what is actually the case.

\subsection{The analogy table}

\medskip

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\bfseries}lXXX}
\toprule

\midrule

\subsection{The formula analogy}
\label{sec:formula}

The first version asserted that all three governing constants obey
\[
  \theta^* = \frac{\text{total} - \text{constraint}}{\text{total}}.
\]
Any $\theta \in [0,1]$ can be written in this form (take total $T$ and
constraint $(1-\theta)T$), so the form constrains nothing. Specifically:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AEP}: $H = \log_2|\mathcal{A}| - D(p \| u)$, where $D(p\|u)$ is
    the Kullback--Leibler divergence from the uniform distribution $u$ on
    $\mathcal{A}$. This identity is correct, and gives
    $H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}| = 1 - D(p\|u)/\log_2|\mathcal{A}|$.

  \item \textbf{Channel Coding}: the first version wrote
    $C = \log|\mathcal{X}| - \min_{p(x)} H(X|Y)$. That formula is false: a point
    mass $p(x)$ gives $H(X|Y) = 0$, so it would give $C = \log|\mathcal{X}|$ for
    every channel. The correct formula is
    $C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y) = \max_{p(x)}\bigl[H(X) - H(X|Y)\bigr]$, a maximum of
    a difference rather than a fixed total minus a fixed constraint. For a
    symmetric channel it equals $\log_2|\mathcal{Y}| - H(\mathbf{r})$, where
    $\mathbf{r}$ is a row of the transition matrix~\cite[Ch.~7]{CoverThomas2006};
    for the binary symmetric channel with crossover probability $\epsilon$ this
    is $1 - H(\epsilon)$.

  \item \textbf{Cascade moduli}: $\thetaW = (2^k - k)/2^k$ with $k = 3$, read as
    $8$ configurations of which $3$ are eliminated. The first version said that
    the Eisenstein filtration ``provides $2^k = 8$ total configuration
    dimensions''. The filtration has $2K$ steps and provides no such count,
    $\{0,1\}^3$ has $8$ points (not $8$ dimensions), and the elimination count is
    the postulate of~\cite{WakilCF,WakilRamanujan}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The reduction analogy}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AEP}: typical sequences are those whose empirical
    log-probability per symbol is close to the entropy.
  \item \textbf{Channel Coding}: in the random-coding proof of achievability,
    decoding uses joint typicality of codewords and channel outputs.
  \item \textbf{Cascade moduli}: no analogue. Primes are equidistributed between
    the inert and the split classes (Proposition~\ref{prop:cascade-sw}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Sophie Germain Primes}
\label{sec:sg}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The first version presented Sophie Germain pairs $(p, 2p+1)$ as a ``confirmed
non-instance''. It stated as a theorem, with a citation to itself, that they have
dimensional parameter $k = 2$ because the residue of $2p+1$ modulo $3$ is
determined by that of $p$; that their predicted level of distribution is
$\theta_2 = 1/2$; and that a computation gives
$\delta \cdot \log x = 2.38 \pm 0.18$, ``inconsistent with $\theta > 1/2$ at
$p < 0.001$''. All of this is withdrawn. We keep only the following remark.

\begin{remark}[Sophie Germain pairs; heuristic]
\label{rem:sg}
For $p > 3$, if $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ then $3 \mid 2p+1$, so Sophie Germain
primes $p > 3$ satisfy $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$. This is the same single condition
as for the smaller member of a twin-prime pair, where the residue of $p+2$ is
equally determined by that of $p$; the ``coupling'' argument for $k = 2$ would
therefore apply to twin primes too. Since $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 1/2$, assigning
either depth predicts $1/2$, so the example cannot discriminate between depths
and is not a falsification test. A ``level of distribution of Sophie Germain
pairs'' is not a defined notion, and no such result is known. The quantity
$\delta$ of the first version was never defined, and no finite computation can
confirm or refute an asymptotic level of distribution.
\end{remark}

The first version compared this coupling to a degraded channel, saying that a
degraded channel has lower capacity ``because extra dependence between input and
output is introduced''. That is backwards. Degradation $X \to Y \to Z$ destroys
input--output dependence: $I(X;Z) \leq I(X;Y)$ by the data-processing
inequality, so the degraded channel has capacity at most that of the original.
With this correction the analogy to ``coupling reduces $k$'' does not hold.

\section{The $k$-Hierarchy}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The formula $\theta_k = (2^k - k)/2^k$, obtained from the counting postulate
of~\cite{WakilCF,WakilRamanujan}, assigns a value to each depth $k$.
Table~\ref{tab:hierarchy} displays the first five levels with the status of each
proposed instance.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{The $k$-hierarchy of constitutional sieve levels.}
\label{tab:hierarchy}
\begin{tabular}{ccccp{7.2cm}}
\toprule
$k$ & $2^k$ & $2^k - k$ & $\theta_k$ & Status \\
\midrule
$1$ & $2$ & $1$ & $1/2$  & Bombieri--Vinogradov level $1/2$ for
  primes~\cite{Bombieri1965,Vinogradov1965}; not derived from $(2-1)/2$ \\
$2$ & $4$ & $2$ & $1/2$  & None established (Sophie Germain does not
  discriminate; Remark~\ref{rem:sg}) \\
$3$ & $8$ & $5$ & $5/8$  & Cascade moduli: conjectural~\cite{WakilEisenstein};
  not an SW instance (Proposition~\ref{prop:cascade-sw}) \\
$4$ & $16$& $12$& $3/4$  & No object specified (Remark~\ref{rem:k4}) \\
$5$ & $32$& $27$& $27/32$& No object specified \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{remark}
Note that $k=1$ and $k=2$ both give $\theta = 1/2$. Bombieri--Vinogradov gives
level $1/2$ for primes on average over moduli $q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}$;
that $1/2$ comes from the large sieve inequality, not from a count of
constraints. Levels beyond $1/2$ are known for primes with well-factorable
weights and a fixed residue class: $4/7$~\cite{BFI1986}, $3/5$~\cite{Maynard2020},
$66/107$~\cite{Lichtman2023} and $5/8$~\cite{Pascadi2025}. None of $4/7$, $3/5$
and $66/107$ is of the form $(2^k - k)/2^k$. The first version described $k=2$
as ``a non-instance of the enhanced effect'' and $k=3$ as ``the first genuinely
new level''; no system of depth $2$ or $3$ is established.
\end{remark}

\section{The Constitutional Forcing Conjecture}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{conjecture}[Constitutional Forcing Conjecture]
\label{conj:cfc}
Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a system in which:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item the configuration space is exponential with growth rate $r > 0$;
  \item the structural law is intrinsic (not an external parameter);
  \item the system has depth $k$ in the sense of the counting postulate
    of~\cite{WakilCF,WakilRamanujan}: exactly $k$ of its $2^k$ binary
    configuration classes are eliminated by its $k$ independent constitutional
    constraints.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\mathcal{S}$ exhibits the Shannon--Wakil effect with governing constant
$\theta^* = (2^k - k)/2^k$.
\end{conjecture}

If a configuration $c \in \{0,1\}^k$ is called invalid when some constraint
fails ($c_i = 0$ for some $i$), then $k$ constraints invalidate $2^k - 1$
configurations, not $k$. Hypothesis~(iii) is therefore a postulate about the
system, not a consequence of its having $k$ binary constraints.

\begin{remark}
The first version said that Conjecture~\ref{conj:cfc} ``encompasses information
theory ($k=1$: entropy/capacity)'' and ``prime constellations ($k=3$: twin
primes via Eisenstein structure)''. Neither holds. The Shannon constants
$H/\log_2|\mathcal{A}|$ and $C/\log_2|\mathcal{X}|$ are continuous functions of
the source and the channel and take every value in $(0,1)$, so they are not
values $\theta_k$, and in particular not $\theta_1 = 1/2$; and twin primes have
depth $1$ by the programme's own independence criterion
(Section~\ref{sec:cascade}). No system is currently known that satisfies (iii)
and exhibits the effect, so the conjecture is at present a proposal. It would be
refuted by a system satisfying (i)--(iii) whose governing constant differs from
$\theta_k$.
\end{remark}

\section{Shannon--Wakil Effect vs.\ Analogies}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

It is important to distinguish the Shannon--Wakil effect from informal analogy.

An \emph{analogy} between two fields observes superficial similarity and draws
heuristic inspiration. The comparison is suggestive but not formally verifiable.
Analogies are valuable but limited: they can be wrong in ways that are hard to
detect, and they generate no falsifiable predictions.

The first version argued that the Shannon--Wakil effect differs from an analogy
in three ways. As revised, the situation is as follows.

\textbf{First}, only the AEP satisfies Definition~\ref{def:sw}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:aep-sw}). Channel coding satisfies its size conditions
only (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel-sw}), and the cascade case fails (SW3)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cascade-sw}).

\textbf{Second}, the framework has not yet produced a falsifiable prediction.
The Sophie Germain example cannot discriminate between depths
(Remark~\ref{rem:sg}), and no object is specified for $k=4$
(Remark~\ref{rem:k4}).

\textbf{Third}, the ``formula correspondence'' is not quantitatively precise:
any $\theta \in [0,1]$ can be written as
$(\text{total} - \text{constraint})/\text{total}$
(Section~\ref{sec:formula}).

The parallels of Section~\ref{sec:correspondences} are therefore, at present,
informal analogies in the sense of the preceding paragraph.

\section{Discussion and Open Problems}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Chronology}

Shannon's paper appeared in 1948~\cite{Shannon1948}. It contains the AEP (for
ergodic Markov sources) and the channel coding theorem, the latter proved by a
random-coding argument that was made rigorous later (Feinstein, 1954). The
arithmetic case was proposed in 2026 and is not established
(Section~\ref{sec:cascade}). The first version argued that the gap between the
two guarantees that neither result was constructed to match the other; since the
arithmetic case is withdrawn, the chronology carries no evidential weight.

\subsection{No object for $k=4$}

\begin{remark}[No object for $k = 4$]
\label{rem:k4}
The formula gives $\theta_4 = (2^4 - 4)/2^4 = 12/16 = 3/4$. The first version
predicted ``a prime constellation with exactly $k = 4$ independent modular
constraints, exhibiting level of distribution $\theta = 3/4$''. No such object
(sequence, family of moduli or sieve) is specified, and a level of distribution
is a property of a sequence in arithmetic progressions, not of a constellation.
So $\theta_4 = 3/4$ is not at present a testable prediction. Specifying such an
object is the open problem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The ratio $5/4$}

The first version called $\thetaW / \thetaBV = (5/8)/(1/2) = 5/4$ the gain in
level of distribution achieved by the Eisenstein structure over
Bombieri--Vinogradov. That interpretation is withdrawn. Bombieri--Vinogradov is
an average over all moduli $q \leq x^{1/2}(\log x)^{-B}$, whereas the cascade
family has $O(\log x)$ moduli; for that family the summed estimate holds
trivially up to exponent $1$ (Proposition~\ref{thm:cascade}), and per modulus
$5/8$ is conjectural; and levels above $1/2$ for general moduli are known with
well-factorable weights (Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}).

\subsection{Channel capacity and levels of distribution}

Both the channel capacity $C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$ and a level of distribution
are defined as suprema. For the moduli $3^K$ the supremum of the admissible
$\theta$ depends on the formulation: for the summed estimate of
Proposition~\ref{thm:cascade} it is $1$, and in the per-modulus form it is
unknown (Montgomery's conjecture predicts $1$). A proved exponent such as the
conjectured $5/8$ would be a lower bound for that supremum, not the supremum
itself. The first version's claim that this ``maximisation correspondence is the
most precise of the five'' is withdrawn.
